About Fort Lauderdale

Fort Lauderdale is a full city with a beach out front and 165 miles of canals running through it. People keep boats behind their houses, take the Water Taxi to dinner and can be at the airport in 15 minutes. It's busier and more social than most of the Florida coast, with live music, a big restaurant scene and nights that go well past midnight.

It's not a sleepy beach town and it's not cheap. Traffic backs up on A1A and at the drawbridges, waterfront homes carry big price tags, and flood and wind insurance add up. If you want city energy with a boat and a beach in reach, though, few places do it better.

Where it shines

Airport accessTop 2%

FLL is about 10 to 15 minutes from downtown and the beach. Miami (MIA) is 40 minutes or so south, and Brightline trains to Miami, West Palm Beach and Orlando leave from the downtown station.

NightlifeTop 3%

Among the biggest nights out on the coast. Revolution Live and Culture Room book touring bands, Himmarshee Street and Las Olas are lined with bars, the beach strip has its own scene, and plenty of doors stay open well past midnight.

Dining and artsTop 6%

Las Olas Boulevard is lined with restaurants and galleries, the Broward Center brings touring shows to the river, and the beach hotels add ocean view dining rooms. Weekend markets set up at Las Olas Oceanside Park.

Meet peopleTop 11%

Lots of ways in. Free jazz on the Riverwalk the first Sunday of each month, leagues and open play on 43 pickleball courts at The Fort, weekend markets by the beach, weekly run clubs, and volunteer crews for the Winterfest Boat Parade.

On the waterTop 15%

They call it the Venice of America for a reason. There are 165 miles of inland waterways, about 100 marinas and boatyards, a deep inlet at Port Everglades, and the largest in water boat show in the world every fall.

BeachesTop 15%

Seven miles of city beachfront, with a two mile promenade behind the wave wall along A1A. Wide sand and plenty of rentals. It draws crowds, and the Tortuga Music Festival takes over a stretch for three days each April.

WalkabilityTop 22%

Walkable in pockets. Las Olas, the Riverwalk and the beach promenade each work well on foot, and the Water Taxi links them. Getting between neighborhoods or to a grocery store usually means a car.

Where it falls short

AffordabilityBottom 30%

Expensive by Florida coast standards. Waterfront houses carry big price tags, and flood and wind insurance add up fast. Condos and the neighborhoods west of US 1 are the more reachable way in.

Coastal charmBottom 15%

Mostly new build, chains and towers. Not much old town feel.

Peace and quietBottom 5%

A busy city. Las Olas, A1A and the drawbridges back up, the beach fills on weekends and through spring, and boat show week snarls everything near the water. The side canals and older neighborhoods are far calmer.

About average for 1 more: active outdoors
Active outdoorsTop 45%

Hugh Taylor Birch State Park puts 180 acres of trails, bike paths and a paddling launch between the Intracoastal and the beach. Add the Riverwalk, the A1A promenade for running and riding, and parks spread along the canals.

Common questions

Is Fort Lauderdale walkable?

In parts. Some errands are walkable, most are a short drive. Walkable in pockets. Las Olas, the Riverwalk and the beach promenade each work well on foot, and the Water Taxi links them. Getting between neighborhoods or to a grocery store usually means a car.

Does Fort Lauderdale have a good beach?

Yes, a very good one. Seven miles of city beachfront, with a two mile promenade behind the wave wall along A1A. Wide sand and plenty of rentals. It draws crowds, and the Tortuga Music Festival takes over a stretch for three days each April.

Is Fort Lauderdale affordable?

What is the nightlife like in Fort Lauderdale?

Really good for a beach town. Start with dinner on Las Olas, then pick a lane. A touring band at Revolution Live or Culture Room, the bars along Himmarshee Street downtown, or the open air spots across from the sand on the beach strip. Things run late here, well past midnight on weekends.

How busy does Fort Lauderdale get?

It gets busy, especially in season. A busy city. Las Olas, A1A and the drawbridges back up, the beach fills on weekends and through spring, and boat show week snarls everything near the water. The side canals and older neighborhoods are far calmer.

Is Fort Lauderdale good for boating and fishing?

Yes. Life here revolves around the water. They call it the Venice of America for a reason. There are 165 miles of inland waterways, about 100 marinas and boatyards, a deep inlet at Port Everglades, and the largest in water boat show in the world every fall.

Which airport is closest to Fort Lauderdale?

FLL is about 10 to 15 minutes from downtown and the beach. Miami (MIA) is 40 minutes or so south, and Brightline trains to Miami, West Palm Beach and Orlando leave from the downtown station.

What should I know before moving to Fort Lauderdale?

The main things to plan for: Flooding, high insurance. Florida homeowners insurance averages about $10,384 a year for $250K of dwelling coverage (MoneyGeek, 2026). Get a quote and check the flood zone for any address before you commit.
