About Perdido Key

Perdido Key is mostly Gulf front condos along one road, with state and national seashore land at each end that will never be built on. People here walk long empty beaches, paddle the lagoon, fish and boat the back waters. Locals and snowbirds share the island with summer visitors, and the Flora-Bama sits right on the state line.

It's a barrier island, so hurricane exposure is the big trade off. Ivan and Sally both hit hard here. Flood insurance and condo association costs matter. Summers are busy, and the Mullet Toss weekend in April packs the road. Groceries and doctors mean a drive back toward Pensacola.

Common questions

Is Perdido Key walkable?

No. Plan on driving for almost everything. Not walkable. It is one long road down a skinny island, and you drive for groceries and most errands. Walking is the beach and the boardwalks.

Does Perdido Key have a good beach?

Yes. It is among the best on the Florida coast. Some of the least crowded Gulf beach in Florida. Johnson Beach in the national seashore and Perdido Key State Park keep long stretches of dunes completely undeveloped.

Is Perdido Key affordable?

It sits around the middle for the Florida coast. Home prices: About $735K median sale on the island (Momentum, Oct 2026). Mostly condos, so prices span a wide range. It is usually cheaper than Pensacola Beach for Gulf front, but HOA fees and wind and flood insurance add up fast.

What is the nightlife like in Perdido Key?

Good bars and some live music, though it is only busy now and then. Dinner on the water, then the Flora-Bama for a band on one of its stages. Late April means the Interstate Mullet Toss, and the whole key turns into a party.

How busy does Perdido Key get?

Is Perdido Key good for boating and fishing?
