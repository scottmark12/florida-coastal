About Mexico Beach

Mexico Beach is a few miles of white sand along Highway 98, with low houses, a canal for boats and almost no crowds outside of summer. Days here are simple: a walk on the beach, a little fishing, a boat ride out to the reefs. Most people know their neighbors because there just aren't that many of them.

Hurricane Michael nearly wiped the town out in 2018, and it is still rebuilding. Wind and flood insurance are high, there are no lifeguards, and the pier rebuild will narrow traffic for about a year. Groceries, doctors and most errands mean a drive to Port St. Joe or Panama City.

Where it shines

Peace and quietTop 8%

One of the quietest beach towns on the Gulf. Summers bring rental guests, but there are no high rises and very little traffic, and winters are almost silent.

Where it falls short

On the waterBottom 23%

Good. A canal and public boat ramp give quick access to the Gulf, and the local reef association has built artificial reefs offshore for fishing and diving. The Al Cathey pier is funded and permitted, with about a year of construction ahead.

Dining and artsBottom 20%

Limited. A few casual seafood spots like Killer Seafood, an ice cream and candy shop with summer lines, and Toucans coming back in a bigger building. Port St. Joe, about 15 minutes east, adds more.

Meet peopleBottom 17%

Small and tight knit. The volunteer special events team runs things like the gumbo cookoff that pays for the Fourth of July fireworks, and the kingfish tournament at the boat ramp draws a crowd in July. Beyond that, you meet people on the beach and at the ramp.

Airport accessBottom 12%

Northwest Florida Beaches (ECP) is about 50 minutes west. Tallahassee is about two hours. Most trips mean a connection.

NightlifeBottom 9%

Barely any, and that is the point. A beach bar or a deck with music on summer weekends, and the newly rebuilt Toucans was set to reopen in fall 2026. Panama City is the nearest place for a real night out.
