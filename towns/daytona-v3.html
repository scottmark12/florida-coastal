About Daytona Beach

Daytona Beach is a working beach city, not a polished resort town. People walk, bike and even drive on the wide, hard sand, fish off the piers and spend Saturdays on City Island by the river. You'll find retirees, young families, college students and race fans side by side, and a lot of people move here because they can afford to be near the ocean.

The trade offs are real. Speedweeks in February, Bike Week in March and Biketoberfest in October bring big crowds and traffic, and some parts of town feel rundown. The whole coast is exposed to hurricanes, beachside condo buildings can come with special assessments, and insurance should be priced before you fall for a place.

A week here

Most morningsCoffee, then a walk or bike ride on the hard sand, where cars still roll along parts of the beach.
Late afternoonA loop through Riverfront Park downtown, with the Halifax River and the boats right beside you.
SaturdayThe farmers market on City Island, where you'll see the same vendors and half your neighbors every week.

Meet peopleTop 26%

Big events do a lot of the work here. Bandshell concerts, baseball at Jackie Robinson Ballpark, the Halifax Art Festival each November and the motorcycle rallies all pull a crowd. Smaller clubs take a bit more looking.

Dining and artsTop 29%

Plenty of places to eat, mostly casual and chain heavy. Beach Street downtown has more of the independents, the Peabody Auditorium brings in touring shows, and the Halifax Art Festival fills the Riverfront Arts District in November.

Active outdoorsTop 50%

The beach is the main park. Beyond it you have the riverfront along Beach Street for a walk or a ride, and Tomoka State Park about 20 minutes north in Ormond Beach for paddling and shaded trails.

Airport accessBottom 48%

Daytona Beach International (DAB) is right in town, next to the Speedway, with American, Delta, Avelo and Breeze. Orlando (MCO) is about an hour and 10 minutes down I-4, and Sanford (SFB) about 45 minutes.

Common questions

Is Daytona Beach walkable?

Not really. You will drive for most things. The city is spread out and built for cars. You can walk Beach Street along the river, the blocks around Main Street by the ocean, or ONE DAYTONA across from the Speedway, but getting between them means driving.

Does Daytona Beach have a good beach?

Yes, a very good one. The famous one. Wide, flat, hard packed sand where you can still drive on and park in marked zones, with plenty of public access. It gets crowded and noisy near Main Street and calmer the farther you go from it.

Is Daytona Beach affordable?

Yes. It is one of the more affordable spots on the Florida coast. Home prices: About $211K median sale over the past year, condos included (Momentum, Nov 2026). Some of the lowest home prices on the Florida coast, from older condos near the ocean to small houses on the mainland. Look closely at insurance, flood zones and condo fees and assessments before you buy.

What is the nightlife like in Daytona Beach?

Good bars and some live music, though it is only busy now and then. Pick your lane. It can be beer and motorcycles on Main Street, clubs on Seabreeze Boulevard, or dinner and drinks at ONE DAYTONA by the Speedway. In concert season there are shows at the oceanfront Bandshell, and during the rallies all of it runs later and louder.

How busy does Daytona Beach get?

It gets busy, especially in season. Loud by design a few times a year. Race weeks at the Speedway, Bike Week and Biketoberfest fill the roads and hotels, and International Speedway Boulevard and A1A stay busy in between. Calmer streets are a few blocks off the main drags.

Is Daytona Beach good for boating and fishing?

Yes, with decent access to ramps and marinas. The Halifax River runs the length of town, with a large city marina downtown and public ramps along the river. The catch is distance from the ocean. Ponce Inlet, the only way out, is roughly ten miles south.

Which airport is closest to Daytona Beach?

Daytona Beach International (DAB) is right in town, next to the Speedway, with American, Delta, Avelo and Breeze. Orlando (MCO) is about an hour and 10 minutes down I-4, and Sanford (SFB) about 45 minutes.

What should I know before moving to Daytona Beach?

The main things to plan for: Event crowds, hurricane exposure. Florida homeowners insurance averages about $10,384 a year for $250K of dwelling coverage (MoneyGeek, 2026). Get a quote and check the flood zone for any address before you commit.
