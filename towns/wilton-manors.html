Wilton Manors

A small island city ringed by the Middle River, built around the restaurants, bars and shops of Wilton Drive.

A day in Wilton Manors

You kayak the river loop around the city in the morning and walk Wilton Drive for dinner and a show at night.

The scorecard

Nightlife4

Wilton Drive lines up bars, lounges, dance floors and show rooms within a few walkable blocks, busy seven nights a week. Hunters Nightclub runs until 2am on weeknights and 3am on Fridays and Saturdays.

Meet people5

About as easy as it gets. Something is on along Wilton Drive every night, from karaoke to line dancing, and the city calendar adds Taste of the Island, a citywide yard sale, a historical society and street parties like Wicked Manors.

Peace and quiet2

Wilton Drive is lively every night, weekends bring crowds and tight parking, and the big street parties close the Drive to traffic. The side streets toward the river are noticeably calmer.

Walkability4

The whole city is under two square miles, and Wilton Drive packs its restaurants, bars and shops onto one main street. From most blocks you can walk or bike to dinner. Bigger errands and the beach take a car.

On the water3

The two forks of the Middle River wrap around the city, so you can paddle a loop around it, and Colohatchee Park has a public boat ramp. This is small boat and kayak water. The big marinas are in Fort Lauderdale.

Beaches1

No beach in town. The city sits inland, ringed by river and not by ocean. Fort Lauderdale beach is the nearest sand, roughly a 10 to 15 minute drive east.

Dining and arts4

Wilton Drive covers a lot of ground for one street, from Greek and Thai to sushi, tacos and pizza, with a few galleries mixed in. Taste of the Island gathers the restaurants each November. Big theaters and museums are 10 minutes south.

Outdoors and wellness2

Pocket parks, not big ones. Colohatchee Park has a mangrove boardwalk and a dog park, Richardson Historic Park has a riverside nature preserve, and Mickel Field has a walking trail. For real trails you drive.

Affordability2

Pricey for a city with no beach. Being this close to downtown Fort Lauderdale keeps house prices well above the Florida median. Condos and townhomes cost less, and low lying streets near the river need flood coverage.

Airport access5

FLL is about 20 minutes south by I-95 or US 1, and Miami (MIA) is around 45 to 50 minutes. Brightline's Fort Lauderdale station is about 10 minutes away for trains to Miami, West Palm Beach and Orlando.
