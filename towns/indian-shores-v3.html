About Indian Shores

Indian Shores is a thin, quiet stretch of the Pinellas barrier islands, mostly condos and small beach buildings along Gulf Boulevard. On one side is the Gulf, and on the other is the Narrows, the skinniest part of the Intracoastal, where dolphins and boats pass all day. Days here are simple: the beach in the morning, a bike ride along the island, and sunset on the sand. It suits retirees, snowbirds and second home owners who want to live right at the beach without a big resort scene.

The trade offs are serious. Helene and Milton flooded much of the island in 2024, and rebuilding is still uneven, so some streets look finished and others do not. It is a low lying barrier island with real storm surge risk and high flood insurance. Most homes are condos, many with high monthly fees, and property taxes reset high on a new purchase. Groceries and doctors are over the bridge on the mainland.

Where it falls short

WalkabilityBottom 35%

You can walk the beach for miles and reach a few restaurants along Gulf Boulevard on foot. Groceries and most errands mean crossing the bridge to Seminole or driving down to Madeira Beach.

On the waterBottom 29%

Water on both sides. The Gulf is out front and the Intracoastal Waterway runs along the back, so boaters keep slips and lifts on the bay side and kayakers paddle the calm water. Fishing from the sand is common.

Meet peopleBottom 26%

A small town of around 1,200 people, so it is more about neighbors on the beach and at your condo pool than organized clubs. The seabird sanctuary takes volunteers, and the nearby towns run picnics and holiday parades you can join.

AffordabilityBottom 26%

Not cheap. Redfin put the median sale price around $650K in August 2026, and most of what sells is condos. Flood and wind insurance on the island can add a lot to monthly costs, especially after 2024.

Active outdoorsBottom 9%

Mostly beach and water life: walking the shoreline, paddling the Intracoastal and watching shorebirds. Sand Key Park to the north and the county beach access parks nearby add more room to roam.

Coastal charmBottom 10%

Mostly new build, chains and towers. Not much old town feel.

Dining and artsTop 48%

A handful of casual spots along Gulf Boulevard, mostly seafood, burgers and beach bar food, some of them rebuilt or reopened after the storms. For real choice you drive 10 to 20 minutes to Indian Rocks Beach, Madeira Beach or Seminole.

BeachesTop 50%

A wide, soft Gulf beach that runs the length of town, part of the long Sand Key stretch. Pinellas County ran a beach nourishment project here in 2025 to rebuild sand lost in the storms.

NightlifeBottom 48%

Low key. Evenings mean a beach bar on the sand, a sunset drink on a condo deck, or a quick drive to the livelier strips in Madeira Beach or Clearwater Beach. Nobody comes here for clubs.

Common questions

Is Indian Shores walkable?

Not really. You will drive for most things. You can walk the beach for miles and reach a few restaurants along Gulf Boulevard on foot. Groceries and most errands mean crossing the bridge to Seminole or driving down to Madeira Beach.

Does Indian Shores have a good beach?

Yes, a very good one. A wide, soft Gulf beach that runs the length of town, part of the long Sand Key stretch. Pinellas County ran a beach nourishment project here in 2025 to rebuild sand lost in the storms.

Is Indian Shores affordable?

It is on the expensive side for the Florida coast. Home prices: About $575K median sale (Momentum, Oct 2026). Not cheap. Redfin put the median sale price around $650K in August 2026, and most of what sells is condos. Flood and wind insurance on the island can add a lot to monthly costs, especially after 2024.

What is the nightlife like in Indian Shores?

How busy does Indian Shores get?

It is quiet most of the year. Pretty quiet. Gulf Boulevard carries steady traffic in season, but the beach itself rarely feels packed, and summer weeknights are calm. Some lots are still construction sites after the 2024 storms, so expect some daytime noise.

Is Indian Shores good for boating and fishing?

Yes. This is a strong boating town. Water on both sides. The Gulf is out front and the Intracoastal Waterway runs along the back, so boaters keep slips and lifts on the bay side and kayakers paddle the calm water. Fishing from the sand is common.
