About Port Richey

Port Richey is a working, unfussy fishing town at the mouth of the Pithlachascotee, which everyone just calls the Cotee River. Canal streets run off the river, docks sit behind modest block homes, and plenty of neighbors keep a boat on a lift. It is mostly retirees, tradespeople and young families who want to be on the water without paying Pinellas prices. Days are about fishing the grass flats, a cold drink on the river and a trip to the Gulf when the weather is right.

The honest trade offs: most of town is low lying and in a flood zone, and Hurricane Helene in 2024 pushed water into river and street neighborhoods. Elevation, roof age and flood insurance matter more here than finishes. US 19 is the main road, and it is busy, lined with strip malls and slow at rush hour. Tampa International is about 45 minutes away.

A week here

Most morningsCoffee, then a slow walk on the trails at Werner Boyce Salt Springs State Park, where the spring runs out into the salt marsh.
Late afternoonTake the boat or a kayak down the Cotee River, past the fish houses and docks, toward the open Gulf.
SaturdaySet up on the sand at Robert K. Rees Park on Green Key, where the same families stake out the same picnic tables every weekend.
Saturday nightFrankie's Raw Bar out on State Road 54. Fresh fish, a raw bar and ribs, and a crowd of regulars who have been coming for over twenty years.
SundayFish the grass flats off the mouth of the Cotee with a neighbor who always knows where the trout are biting.

Where it falls short

Meet peopleBottom 24%

It is a small city, so you meet people at the boat ramp, the bait shop and the city's events at Waterfront Park, like the Boom on the Bayou Fourth of July celebration. The Nature Coast Chamber runs a lot of what happens locally.

BeachesBottom 8%

No real beach in town. The shoreline is mangroves and marsh, so for sand you boat out to the Anclote Key sandbars or drive south to Honeymoon Island, about 35 minutes.

Active outdoorsBottom 6%

Werner Boyce Salt Springs State Park has mangrove paddling, a 351 foot deep salt spring and trails, and the Anclote Key preserve is a boat ride away. It is flat, green and good for kayaking.

Common questions

Is Port Richey walkable?

Not really. You will drive for most things. Not very walkable. Neighborhoods are spread out along canals, and errands mean a drive on US 19.

Does Port Richey have a good beach?

Not really. The beach is not the draw here. No real beach in town. The shoreline is mangroves and marsh, so for sand you boat out to the Anclote Key sandbars or drive south to Honeymoon Island, about 35 minutes.

Is Port Richey affordable?

Yes. It is one of the more affordable spots on the Florida coast. Home prices: About $236K median sale (Momentum, Nov 2026). One of the more affordable waterfront towns around Tampa Bay, with typical values in the low $200Ks. Flood insurance and elevation matter a lot here, so check a home's history before you buy.

What is the nightlife like in Port Richey?

Quiet, with a handful of places to go. Dinner on a river deck as the fishing boats come in, then a drink and maybe a cover band. Most nights wind down early, and the bigger nights out are in downtown New Port Richey.

How busy does Port Richey get?

It is busy in season and calm the rest of the year. Mostly quiet residential streets and canals. The noise is US 19, which is busy and commercial, so it depends how close you live to it.

Is Port Richey good for boating and fishing?

Yes. This is a strong boating town. This is the reason to be here. Canal homes have docks, the Pithlachascotee River runs out to the Gulf, and you are a short run from the grass flats, Anclote Key and nearshore fishing for trout, redfish and grouper.

Which airport is closest to Port Richey?

Tampa International (TPA) is about 45 minutes down the Suncoast Parkway or US 19, more at rush hour. It is a big airport with lots of nonstops.

What should I know before moving to Port Richey?

The main things to plan for: Low lying and mostly in a flood zone, with river and street flooding in big storms like Helene in 2024; US 19 traffic.
