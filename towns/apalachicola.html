Apalachicola

An oyster town with brick storefronts, shrimp boats and no hurry at all.

A day in Apalachicola

You walk to breakfast past the shrimp boats, browse the old brick downtown and watch the sun set over the bay.

The scorecard

Nightlife1

Evenings are a beer at the Oyster City Brewing taproom or live music at High Five, and that's about it. Nothing in town stays open much past 10.

Meet people2

A short list, but a real one. The farmers market runs two Saturdays a month, Apalachicola Riverkeeper volunteers do cleanups and paddle trips, and the Panhandle Players put on community theater.

Peace and quiet5

Most days it's you, the river and a few slow blocks of brick storefronts. The exception is festival weekends, when the Florida Seafood Festival brings tens of thousands of people to Battery Park in November.

Walkability4

More than 900 historic buildings sit on a walkable grid by the river, and there's a full grocery store in town. For big box shopping you'll drive.

On the water4

A true working waterfront. Launch at Battery Park or Scipio Creek and you're on the river or the bay in minutes. Wild oyster harvesting reopened in 2026 after five years closed.

Beaches3

There's no beach in town, but St. George Island is 11 miles away over two bridges. Dr. Beach named its state park the number one beach in America in 2023.

Dining and arts2

A handful of very good seafood spots like the Owl Café and Up the Creek, eight galleries, and a plein air painting festival each March. Don't expect a new opening every month.

Outdoors and wellness4

You're surrounded: nearly 100 miles of paddling trails, Tate's Hell State Forest next door and the half million acres of the Apalachicola National Forest to the north.

Affordability4

Still one of the better values on the coast for a town with this much character. With so few sales, prices can jump around from month to month.

Airport access1

Plan on about 90 minutes to Tallahassee (TLH) or 80 to Panama City Beach (ECP). There is a small hospital in town with a 24 hour ER.

Common questions about Apalachicola

Is Apalachicola walkable?

Yes, for the most part. More than 900 historic buildings sit on a walkable grid by the river, and there's a full grocery store in town. For big box shopping you'll drive.

Does Apalachicola have a good beach?

Yes, a decent one. There's no beach in town, but St. George Island is 11 miles away over two bridges. Dr. Beach named its state park the number one beach in America in 2023.

Is Apalachicola affordable?

Yes, it costs less than most of the Florida coast. Home prices: About $300K to $385K, depending on the source (Zillow and Redfin, Aug 2026). Still one of the better values on the coast for a town with this much character. With so few sales, prices can jump around from month to month.

What is the nightlife like in Apalachicola?

Very quiet. Most of town is dark early. A night out here is dinner on the water, a beer at the Oyster City Brewing taproom and maybe live music at High Five. It's an early town, and most places are closed by 10.

How busy does Apalachicola get?

It is one of the quietest places on the Florida coast. Most days it's you, the river and a few slow blocks of brick storefronts. The exception is festival weekends, when the Florida Seafood Festival brings tens of thousands of people to Battery Park in November.

Is Apalachicola good for boating and fishing?

Yes. This is a strong boating town. A true working waterfront. Launch at Battery Park or Scipio Creek and you're on the river or the bay in minutes. Wild oyster harvesting reopened in 2026 after five years closed.

Which airport is closest to Apalachicola?

Plan on about 90 minutes to Tallahassee (TLH) or 80 to Panama City Beach (ECP). There is a small hospital in town with a 24 hour ER.
