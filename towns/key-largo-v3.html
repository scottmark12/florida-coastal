About Key Largo

Key Largo is the closest of the Keys to Miami, and it feels like a real place to live, not just a vacation spot. People dive and snorkel the reef in John Pennekamp Coral Reef State Park, fish the bay, and kayak through the mangroves. There's a grocery store, schools and a mix of working families, retirees and weekend people from Miami.

Insurance in Monroe County is the highest in the state, and flood insurance adds more. Evacuating for a hurricane means a long drive up one or two roads. Weekends and winters bring heavy traffic on US 1. Prices range widely, from mobile homes to big waterfront homes, so it pays to know which part of the island you're in.

Where it shines

On the waterTop 15%

John Pennekamp Coral Reef State Park, the country's first undersea park, is right here with a marina, a ramp for boats up to 36 feet and daily snorkel, dive and glass bottom boats. The reef is east and Florida Bay is west.

Where it falls short

BeachesBottom 32%

Thin on sand. Pennekamp has two small beaches, Cannon Beach for snorkeling and Far Beach for a swim under the palms, and Harry Harris Park in Tavernier has a protected lagoon. Expect shallow, calm water, not a long shoreline walk.

AffordabilityBottom 11%

Pricey, though a notch below Islamorada and Key West. Canal homes and anything on open water cost well above the Florida median, and Keys wind and flood insurance add heavily. Older condos and dry lots are the entry point.

WalkabilityBottom 10%

Key Largo is a long, thin strip of shops and marinas along US 1 with no real center, so plan to drive. The Overseas Heritage Trail runs alongside for bike rides, but crossing the highway on foot is no fun.

Airport accessBottom 10%

Miami International (MIA) is about an hour and 15 minutes north by US 1 or Card Sound Road, more on a weekend. Fort Lauderdale is closer to an hour and 45. There is no airline service in the Upper Keys.

Active outdoorsTop 34%

Outstanding. Reefs and wrecks offshore, mangrove paddling trails at Pennekamp, and more than six miles of mostly paved trails through tropical hardwood forest at Dagny Johnson Key Largo Hammock Botanical State Park.

Dining and artsTop 36%

Plenty of casual seafood, from dockside fish sandwiches to key lime pie, with a few nicer bayfront dining rooms for sunset. The arts side is light, mostly small galleries and bar bands, with a bit more in Islamorada.

NightlifeTop 42%

Modest. The Caribbean Club, a bayside bar that dates to 1938, has live music Thursday through Sunday and stays open until around 1am. Beyond that it is sunset tiki bars and early mornings, with no late night district.

Coastal charmTop 46%

A little character in spots, but mostly modern.

Meet peopleBottom 50%

The reef is the meeting point. REEF trains volunteer fish surveyors and hosts a monthly Fish and Friends talk, the Coral Restoration Foundation runs dive programs you can join, and the Upper Keys Sailing Club sits on the bay side.

Peace and quietBottom 40%

US 1 is busy here, since every car headed down the Keys passes through, and weekends and holidays bring real backups. The side streets and bayside canals are much calmer, especially in late summer and fall.

Common questions

Is Key Largo walkable?

No. Plan on driving for almost everything. Key Largo is a long, thin strip of shops and marinas along US 1 with no real center, so plan to drive. The Overseas Heritage Trail runs alongside for bike rides, but crossing the highway on foot is no fun.

Does Key Largo have a good beach?

It has a modest one. Better beaches are a drive away. Thin on sand. Pennekamp has two small beaches, Cannon Beach for snorkeling and Far Beach for a swim under the palms, and Harry Harris Park in Tavernier has a protected lagoon. Expect shallow, calm water, not a long shoreline walk.

Is Key Largo affordable?

It is on the expensive side for the Florida coast. Home prices: About $809K median sale (Momentum, Nov 2026). Pricey, though a notch below Islamorada and Key West. Canal homes and anything on open water cost well above the Florida median, and Keys wind and flood insurance add heavily. Older condos and dry lots are the entry point.

What is the nightlife like in Key Largo?

Quiet, with a handful of places to go. A night out is sunset over Florida Bay with a band and a rum drink, maybe at the Caribbean Club, then fish at a dockside table. A few bars run later, but with dive boats leaving in the morning, most nights wrap up early.

How busy does Key Largo get?

It is busy in season and calm the rest of the year. US 1 is busy here, since every car headed down the Keys passes through, and weekends and holidays bring real backups. The side streets and bayside canals are much calmer, especially in late summer and fall.

Is Key Largo good for boating and fishing?

Yes. Life here revolves around the water. John Pennekamp Coral Reef State Park, the country's first undersea park, is right here with a marina, a ramp for boats up to 36 feet and daily snorkel, dive and glass bottom boats. The reef is east and Florida Bay is west.

Which airport is closest to Key Largo?

Miami International (MIA) is about an hour and 15 minutes north by US 1 or Card Sound Road, more on a weekend. Fort Lauderdale is closer to an hour and 45. There is no airline service in the Upper Keys.

What should I know before moving to Key Largo?

The main things to plan for: A long evacuation drive, high insurance. Florida homeowners insurance averages about $10,384 a year for $250K of dwelling coverage (MoneyGeek, 2026). Get a quote and check the flood zone for any address before you commit.
