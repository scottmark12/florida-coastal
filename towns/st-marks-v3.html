About St. Marks

St. Marks is a handful of streets where the Wakulla and St. Marks rivers meet, half an hour south of Tallahassee. People come here to fish, paddle, bike the rail trail and watch birds in the refuge. It's quiet during the week. On weekends, boaters and cyclists from Tallahassee roll in, eat on the river and head home.

Flooding is the big issue. Storm surge has put water in town five times since 2005, most recently with Helene in 2024. Raised homes and flood insurance are a must. There are very few sales, few stores and no hospital in town, so you'll drive to Tallahassee for most things.

Where it shines

AffordabilityTop 9%

Low prices, with a big catch. Redfin showed a median near $219,000 in spring 2026, based on very few sales, and it rates nearly every property in town at severe flood risk. Rebuilding rules can require raising a house, so get the elevation and a flood insurance quote before anything else.

Peace and quietTop 17%

The whole town covers less than two square miles, and the road ends at the river. Weekdays are still. The trail and the boat ramps bring day visitors on weekends, and the Stone Crab Festival is the one truly crowded day.

Where it falls short

On the waterBottom 28%

Two rivers meet here and the bay is four miles downstream. Shields Marina has slips, dry storage and a launch that works around the clock, Shell Island Fish Camp has a ramp on the Wakulla, and the city has ramps at both river parks for $5 a day.

Dining and artsBottom 11%

A couple of waterfront places for seafood, plus the small museum at San Marcos de Apalache, where a fort was begun in 1679. Bo Lynn's Grocery dates to 1936 and is on the National Register. For anything more, Tallahassee is half an hour north.

Meet peopleBottom 9%

More than you'd expect for a town this size. A community market runs the fourth Saturday of each month, the Stone Crab Festival has taken over town on the last Saturday of October since 1997, and the Friends of St. Marks Wildlife Refuge run a weekly trail crew and Second Sunday talks.

BeachesBottom 1%

No beach in town. This is river and marsh. Shell Point Beach is the closest sand, about 25 minutes away, and Mashes Sands is about the same. Alligator Point is around 45 minutes.

Active outdoorsTop 37%

Hard to beat. The St. Marks National Wildlife Refuge covers more than 86,000 acres and 43 miles of coast, with a lighthouse first lit in 1831. The paved rail trail runs 16 miles toward Tallahassee, the Wakulla River is an easy paddle, and Wakulla Springs State Park is 17 minutes away.

Coastal charmTop 45%

A little character in spots, but mostly modern.

WalkabilityTop 47%

Small enough to cover on foot. The cafe, the marina, the river park, the fort site, the historic Bo Lynn's Grocery and city hall all sit along Riverside Drive and Port Leon Drive, and the rail trail ends right in town. For a supermarket you drive 16 minutes to Crawfordville.

NightlifeBottom 46%

Two spots, both casual. Riverside Cafe sits on the river and stays open until 11 on Friday and Saturday, and Cooter Stew Cafe has an outdoor stage. The rest of the week things close by 9 or 10.

Airport accessBottom 44%

Tallahassee (TLH) is about 21 miles and 30 minutes away, the shortest airport run on this stretch of coast. It is a regional airport, so many trips include a connection.

Common questions

Is St. Marks walkable?

In parts. Some errands are walkable, most are a short drive. Small enough to cover on foot. Does St. Marks have a good beach?

Not really. The beach is not the draw here. No beach in town. This is river and marsh. Shell Point Beach is the closest sand, about 25 minutes away, and Mashes Sands is about the same. Alligator Point is around 45 minutes.

Quiet, with a handful of places to go. Dinner on the deck at Riverside Cafe while the boats come in, then a band on the outdoor stage at Cooter Stew Cafe if one is playing. Friday and Saturday run until 11. After that it's quiet.
