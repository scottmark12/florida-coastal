The scorecard

Nightlife2

Modest. Concerts at the Sims Park amphitheater, shows at the Richey Suncoast Theatre and a handful of bars and taprooms around Main Street and Grand Boulevard. Weeknights are quiet.

Meet people3

Decent options. Leagues at the Recreation and Aquatic Center, free evening yoga at Cavalaire Square, the monthly Fourth Friday Bazaar and volunteer committees with New Port Richey Main Street.

Peace and quiet3

Downtown is calm on most days and loud on purpose a few times a year, above all during the nine days of Chasco Fiesta each spring. US 19, just west of downtown, is heavy traffic all the time.

Walkability3

Downtown itself is easy on foot. Main Street, Grand Boulevard, Sims Park, Orange Lake and the river all sit within a few blocks. Beyond that core it's a driving town built around US 19.

On the water3

The Cotee River winds through downtown and out to the Gulf, good for kayaks and smaller boats, and it hosts a boat parade every spring. Bigger water and marinas are a few minutes north in Port Richey.

Beaches1

No real beach. The shoreline here is mangrove and shallow flats. A few small county waterfront parks are nearby, but for proper sand you drive south to Fred Howard Park or Honeymoon Island.

Dining and arts2

A small lineup of casual restaurants and cafes on Main Street and Grand Boulevard, with spots like Sasha's On The Park near the water. The Richey Suncoast Theatre is the main stage in town.

Outdoors and wellness3

Sims Park and Orange Lake are right downtown, James E. Grey Preserve has trails along the river, and Werner-Boyce Salt Springs State Park in Port Richey has a kayak launch and marsh trails.

Affordability5

One of the lowest priced places to buy near the Gulf in the Tampa Bay area, with plenty of small older homes. Ask about flood zones near the river and the coast, since insurance can change the math.

Airport access4

Tampa (TPA) is about 45 minutes south and St. Pete Clearwater (PIE) is about the same. Both depend on how US 19 or the Suncoast Parkway is moving.

Common questions about New Port Richey

Is New Port Richey walkable?

In parts. Some errands are walkable, most are a short drive. Downtown itself is easy on foot. Main Street, Grand Boulevard, Sims Park, Orange Lake and the river all sit within a few blocks. Beyond that core it's a driving town built around US 19.

Does New Port Richey have a good beach?

Not really. The beach is not the draw here. No real beach. The shoreline here is mangrove and shallow flats. A few small county waterfront parks are nearby, but for proper sand you drive south to Fred Howard Park or Honeymoon Island.

Is New Port Richey affordable?

Yes. It is one of the more affordable spots on the Florida coast. Home prices: About $235K median sale price, all home types (fall 2026). One of the lowest priced places to buy near the Gulf in the Tampa Bay area, with plenty of small older homes. Ask about flood zones near the river and the coast, since insurance can change the math.

What is the nightlife like in New Port Richey?

Quiet, with a handful of places to go. Start with dinner on Main Street or Grand Boulevard, then a show at the Richey Suncoast Theatre or a free concert at Sims Park by the river. A few bars and taprooms keep going after, but it's not a late town.

How busy does New Port Richey get?

It is busy in season and calm the rest of the year. Downtown is calm on most days and loud on purpose a few times a year, above all during the nine days of Chasco Fiesta each spring. US 19, just west of downtown, is heavy traffic all the time.

Is New Port Richey good for boating and fishing?

Yes, with decent access to ramps and marinas. The Cotee River winds through downtown and out to the Gulf, good for kayaks and smaller boats, and it hosts a boat parade every spring. Bigger water and marinas are a few minutes north in Port Richey.

Which airport is closest to New Port Richey?

Tampa (TPA) is about 45 minutes south and St. Pete Clearwater (PIE) is about the same. Both depend on how US 19 or the Suncoast Parkway is moving.
