About Miami (Brickell and the Grove)

Mainland Miami runs along Biscayne Bay. Brickell is the city of glass towers, where people walk to work, to the gym and to dinner, and many never need a car during the week. Edgewater, just north of downtown, is newer towers on the bay with a little more room to breathe. Coconut Grove is the opposite feel: old banyan trees, sailboats at Dinner Key, and homes tucked behind hedges. You'll hear Spanish as much as English, and the coffee culture is Cuban.

The trade offs are real. Insurance in Miami Dade is the highest outside the Keys, and flood insurance matters in low streets that take on water during king tides and heavy rain. Traffic is heavy most of the day. Condo fees are rising, and many older buildings face big special assessments under Florida's newer condo safety rules, so read the reserve study before you buy. Hurricanes are a fact of life here.

Where it shines

Airport accessTop 1%

Miami International is about 15 to 25 minutes from Brickell with nonstop flights almost anywhere, including Latin America and Europe. Fort Lauderdale is a second option about 40 minutes north.

NightlifeTop 2%

This is a real late night city. Brickell has rooftop bars and lounges, Wynwood has breweries and music venues in old warehouses, and Downtown has the arena and big clubs. Things start late and go later.

Dining and artsTop 2%

One of the best food cities in the country. Cuban coffee windows and bakeries, Latin American and Caribbean spots, high end tasting menus in Brickell and Design District, and casual waterfront places in the Grove.

On the waterTop 18%

Biscayne Bay is the backyard. Dinner Key in Coconut Grove has a big city marina, mooring field and sailing clubs, and the bay, Key Biscayne and the Keys are close for fishing, paddling and boating.

WalkabilityTop 18%

Brickell, Downtown and Edgewater are some of the most walkable areas in Florida, with the free Metromover loop and Metrorail tying them together. The Grove center is walkable too. Outside those cores you drive, and traffic is heavy.

Meet peopleTop 20%

Easy if you put yourself out there. Run clubs, sailing clubs, rec leagues, gallery nights and a steady calendar of festivals mean there is always something on, and a lot of people are new in town too.

Where it falls short

Coastal charmBottom 32%

Mostly new build, chains and towers. Not much old town feel.

AffordabilityBottom 31%

Expensive. Typical home values ran around $570K in early 2026, condos dominate the core, and insurance, HOA dues and post Surfside reserve rules have pushed many condo assessments up. Check a building's reserves before you buy.

Active outdoorsBottom 30%

You get the bay, The Underline trail south from Brickell, the Grove's waterfront parks and the gardens at Vizcaya. Everglades and Biscayne National Parks are under an hour away, but the city itself is mostly paved.

Common questions

Is Miami walkable?

Yes, for the most part. Brickell, Downtown and Edgewater are some of the most walkable areas in Florida, with the free Metromover loop and Metrorail tying them together. The Grove center is walkable too. Outside those cores you drive, and traffic is heavy.

Does Miami have a good beach?

It has a modest one. Better beaches are a drive away. There is no ocean beach on the mainland itself. Key Biscayne is about 20 minutes over the causeway with calm, clear water, and Miami Beach is across the bay. Many people make it a weekend habit rather than a daily walk.

Is Miami affordable?

It is on the expensive side for the Florida coast. Home prices: About $588K median sale (Zillow, Jun 2026). Expensive. Typical home values ran around $570K in early 2026, condos dominate the core, and insurance, HOA dues and post Surfside reserve rules have pushed many condo assessments up. Check a building's reserves before you buy.

What is the nightlife like in Miami?

Big city nightlife, with late hours and plenty of choice. Sunset drinks on a rooftop or by the bay, then dinner somewhere loud and good. Wynwood and Brickell stay busy past midnight, while the Grove is more of a sit outside under the trees evening.

How busy does Miami get?

It is busy most of the year. Not a quiet place. Brickell and Downtown have construction, traffic and sirens, and weekends are loud. Coconut Grove and parts of Coral Gables are the calmer pockets, with big trees and slower streets.

Is Miami good for boating and fishing?

Yes. Life here revolves around the water. Biscayne Bay is the backyard. Dinner Key in Coconut Grove has a big city marina, mooring field and sailing clubs, and the bay, Key Biscayne and the Keys are close for fishing, paddling and boating.

Which airport is closest to Miami?

Miami International is about 15 to 25 minutes from Brickell with nonstop flights almost anywhere, including Latin America and Europe. Fort Lauderdale is a second option about 40 minutes north.

What should I know before moving to Miami?

The main things to plan for: Hurricane exposure, king tide and rain flooding in low streets, heavy traffic, rising condo fees and assessments. Florida homeowners insurance averages about $10,384 a year for $250K of dwelling coverage (MoneyGeek, 2026). Get a quote and check the flood zone for any address before you commit.
