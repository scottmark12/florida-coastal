About St. Augustine

St. Augustine is a real small city wrapped around a historic downtown, with coquina walls, Spanish moss, horse carriages and the old fort on the bay. Locals walk to dinner, bike over the Bridge of Lions to the beach and still find a quiet street after the day trippers go home.

Downtown gets crowded on weekends and holidays, and some low streets flood in big storms. Prices run higher near the historic district. Newer neighborhoods west and south of town cost less.

Easy to find a regular thing. The Ancient City Road Runners meet behind the Lightner Museum for a Wednesday evening run, almost 30 galleries open for the First Friday Art Walk, and a farmers market sets up every Saturday morning at the Amphitheatre.

Dining and artsTop 3%

Hard to beat on this coast for eating out, and you can do it all on foot. Restaurants fill the old town, almost 30 galleries open late for the First Friday Art Walk, and the Amphitheatre brings touring acts just across the bridge.

Active outdoorsTop 9%

Downtown has music most nights. Colonial Oak Music Park puts on free shows under the trees on St. George Street, Prohibition Kitchen has a stage down the block, and O.C. White's has live music in its bayfront courtyard every night.

Common questions

Is St. Augustine walkable?

Yes. It is one of the most walkable towns on the Florida coast. The old town is built for feet. St. George Street is closed to cars, and the Castillo de San Marcos, the bayfront, the plaza and the Lightner Museum are all a few blocks apart. The Bridge of Lions carries you on to Anastasia Island by bike.

Does St. Augustine have a good beach?

Yes, a very good one. The old city sits on the bay, so the sand is a bridge away. Anastasia State Park has four miles of protected beach just over the Bridge of Lions, and Vilano Beach is a short drive north with public parking and seasonal lifeguards.

Is St. Augustine affordable?

It sits around the middle for the Florida coast. Home prices: About $422K median (Momentum, Jun 2026). Middle of the pack for the Florida coast. Houses in the historic district cost the most, and prices ease as you move away from the old town. Low lying streets flood, so price flood insurance before you fall for a house.

What is the nightlife like in St. Augustine?

Good bars and some live music, though it is only busy now and then. A night out here happens on foot. Dinner in the old town, a free show at Colonial Oak Music Park or a band at Prohibition Kitchen, then a walk along the bayfront. On concert nights the Amphitheatre across the bridge draws a big crowd, and its music ends by 10.

How busy does St. Augustine get?

It gets busy, especially in season. Visitors come all year and the old town feels it. Tour trolleys, tight parking and packed sidewalks on St. George Street are normal, and the winter holiday lights bring the heaviest crowds. Early morning is the calm window.

Is St. Augustine good for boating and fishing?

Yes, with decent access to ramps and marinas. The Municipal Marina sits on the bayfront with a mooring field out front. Public ramps are a short drive away at Lighthouse Park on Salt Run and at Vilano, which has three ramps, and the inlet to the ocean is close by.

Which airport is closest to St. Augustine?

Jacksonville (JAX) is about an hour north. Daytona Beach (DAB) is about an hour south with a shorter list of flights. The local airport just north of town has no airline service right now.

What should I know before moving to St. Augustine?

The main things to plan for: Low lying streets flood in big storms. Florida homeowners insurance averages about $10,384 a year for $250K of dwelling coverage (MoneyGeek, 2026). Get a quote and check the flood zone for any address before you commit.
