About Redington Shores

Redington Shores is a small beach town where people still know each other. There is a big public beach park on the Gulf, a mix of condo towers and beach homes along Gulf Boulevard, and bay side canal streets where nearly every house has a boat lift. The town keeps a calendar of picnics and holiday parades, and the local tavern has been the neighborhood living room for generations. It suits retirees, boaters and anyone who wants a beach town with a real community.

The trade offs are serious. Helene and Milton flooded the island heavily in 2024, and many homes have been demolished or are being rebuilt and raised, so expect construction on a lot of streets. This is a low barrier island with storm surge risk and high flood insurance. Condo fees can be steep, and property taxes reset high on a new purchase. Doctors and big stores are over the bridge.

A week here

Most morningsCoffee, then a walk on the sand at the town's big beach park before the lot fills up.
Late afternoonTake the boat off the lift and out into Boca Ciega Bay while the water is calm.
SaturdayA town picnic or a holiday golf cart parade, where it feels like the whole island turns out.
Saturday nightFriendly Tavern, open since 1947 and back after the storms. Cold drinks, one of the longest running karaoke nights around, and bartenders who know most people by name.
SundayWalk down to the Gulf for sunset, then home along the canal streets as the dock lights come on.

Where it shines

On the waterTop 14%

Strong water access. Bay side streets run along canals off the Intracoastal with docks and lifts, there is a local yacht club, and the Gulf is a short boat ride through the passes.

Peace and quietTop 23%

Generally quiet, especially on the bay side streets. In 2026 there is still plenty of construction as storm damaged homes come down and new elevated ones go up, so weekday noise is real.

Where it falls short

AffordabilityBottom 29%

Pricey for the area. Redfin put the median sale price around $625K in September 2026 with homes sitting about 117 days. Rebuild costs, elevation rules and flood insurance weigh heavily on older homes.

Active outdoorsBottom 14%

Beach walks, paddling the bay and fishing from docks are the daily routine. Del Bello Nature Park in town is a small green space, and Sand Key Park and Fort De Soto are short drives.

Coastal charmBottom 12%

Mostly new build, chains and towers. Not much old town feel.
