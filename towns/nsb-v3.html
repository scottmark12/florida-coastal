About New Smyrna Beach

New Smyrna Beach has two sides. The beachside has Flagler Avenue's surf shops, cafes and music running down to the ocean, and the mainland has the shady, historic Canal Street downtown. It's a well known surf town, and parts of the beach still let you drive on and park on the sand.

The beachside sits in flood zones, and summer weekends bring crowds from Orlando. Mainland neighborhoods cost less and carry less flood risk.

Where it shines

Active outdoorsTop 2%

Smyrna Dunes Park has two miles of boardwalk over the dunes at the inlet and a beach where dogs are welcome. Canaveral National Seashore is just south for paddling Mosquito Lagoon, and there is a farmers market every Saturday.

NightlifeTop 10%

Flagler Avenue is the night out. Flagler Tavern has live music every evening and keeps it going until close on Fridays and Saturdays, with more bars and patios within a couple of blocks. Lively, but not wild.

BeachesTop 11%

Thirteen miles of wide, hard packed sand you can drive and bike on, with surfers gathered at the break by the inlet jetty. At the south end it runs into 24 more miles of undeveloped shoreline at Canaveral National Seashore.

WalkabilityTop 13%

Two walkable pockets. Flagler Avenue runs a few blocks from the Indian River to the sand, lined with restaurants and shops, and Canal Street does the same job on the mainland. You need a bike or a car to get between them.

Coastal charmTop 15%

A charming town with a real old center, local shops and pretty streets.

Meet peopleTop 24%

Plenty of standing dates. A Saturday morning farmers market that has run since 1991, an art stroll on Canal Street the first Saturday of the month, a monthly wine walk on Flagler Avenue and a shuffleboard club right on the Avenue.

Dining and artsTop 24%

Flagler Avenue and Third Avenue cover seafood, Italian, barbecue and tacos, and Norwood's has its treehouse bar. The Little Theatre stages community productions, and the IMAGES art festival reached its 50th year in 2026.

Where it falls short

Peace and quietBottom 31%

Summer weekends and holidays bring day trip crowds from Orlando, and the causeways and beach ramps back up. Midweek and in the cooler months it settles into a small town pace, especially on the mainland side.

Common questions

Is New Smyrna Beach walkable?

Yes, for the most part. Two walkable pockets. Flagler Avenue runs a few blocks from the Indian River to the sand, lined with restaurants and shops, and Canal Street does the same job on the mainland. You need a bike or a car to get between them.

Does New Smyrna Beach have a good beach?

Yes. It is among the best on the Florida coast. Thirteen miles of wide, hard packed sand you can drive and bike on, with surfers gathered at the break by the inlet jetty. At the south end it runs into 24 more miles of undeveloped shoreline at Canaveral National Seashore.

Beach side houses and anything near Flagler Avenue cost well above the state median, while mainland neighborhoods come in lower. Wind and flood insurance add up on the barrier island.

What is the nightlife like in New Smyrna Beach?

Good bars and some live music, though it is only busy now and then. How busy does New Smyrna Beach get?

It is busy in season and calm the rest of the year. Summer weekends and holidays bring day trip crowds from Orlando, and the causeways and beach ramps back up. Midweek and in the cooler months it settles into a small town pace, especially on the mainland side.

Is New Smyrna Beach good for boating and fishing?

Yes, with decent access to ramps and marinas. The Indian River runs between the mainland and the beach side. Swoope Boat Ramp has two ramps with floating docks and a run out to Ponce Inlet and the ocean, but only 36 parking spaces, so go early on weekends.

Which airport is closest to New Smyrna Beach?

Daytona Beach (DAB) is about 30 minutes north with a short list of airlines. Orlando (MCO) is a little over an hour away and flies nearly everywhere. Sanford (SFB) is about 50 minutes.
