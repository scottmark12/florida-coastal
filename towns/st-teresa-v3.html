About St. Teresa

St. Teresa is a short stretch of beach houses on US 98 east of Carrabelle, next to the FSU Coastal and Marine Lab. Some families have had their cottages here for generations, and the newer SummerCamp community sits nearby. There is nothing to buy and nothing to do but walk the bay beach, fish, paddle and sit on the porch. It suits Tallahassee families who want a weekend place, and retirees who truly want quiet.

The trade offs: storm surge is a real risk, and Hurricane Michael pushed about 12 feet of water into the marine lab next door in 2018. Many lots sit in high risk flood zones. There are no stores or medical care close by; groceries mean a drive to Carrabelle or Crawfordville, and a hospital means a longer one. Sales are few, so prices are hard to pin down.

Where it shines

Peace and quietTop 2%

Very quiet. It is a single line of beach houses along US 98 with forest behind it. SummerCamp Beach next door is a planned community on more than 750 acres, with over half of that set aside as conservation land.

Where it falls short

Active outdoorsBottom 32%

This is the strength. Alligator Harbor Aquatic Preserve protects more than 14,000 acres of water out the back door, Tate's Hell State Forest has the 8.9 mile High Bluff Coastal Trail, and Bald Point and Ochlockonee River state parks are each about 20 minutes away.

Airport accessBottom 13%

Tallahassee (TLH) is about 42 miles and roughly an hour away, which makes this one of the closest Gulf beaches to the capital.

On the waterBottom 5%

Leonard's Landing is a small county ramp on Alligator Harbor with one lane and six parking spaces, good for skiffs and kayaks. A bigger public ramp and a marina are at Alligator Point, about 15 minutes away, and Carrabelle has full service marinas.

NightlifeBottom 2%

None in St. Teresa. The nearest bar is at the Crooked River Grill, at the golf resort a few miles west. For live music you drive about 20 minutes to Carrabelle, where Fathom's stays open until 11 Thursday through Saturday.

Meet peopleBottom 2%

The marine lab is the gathering place. It gives free guided tours on Fridays, holds free evening lectures and runs volunteer restoration programs. Beyond that the calendar is in Carrabelle and Sopchoppy, each about 20 minutes away, including the Sopchoppy Opry on the last Saturday of the month.

WalkabilityBottom 2%

There is nothing to walk to but the beach. We found no store or restaurant in the community. The nearest full grocery is in Carrabelle, about 20 minutes west, so every errand is a drive.

Dining and artsBottom 1%

Nothing in St. Teresa. The Crooked River Grill is a few miles west, Carrabelle has a handful of seafood and breakfast spots, and Panacea is about 18 minutes east. Plan on cooking.
