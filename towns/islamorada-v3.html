About Islamorada

Islamorada is a string of small keys in the Upper Keys, tied together by the Overseas Highway. Life runs around the water. Charter captains head out at dawn, people fish the flats and the reef, and evenings end on the Florida Bay side watching the sun go down. It's laid back, with a mix of longtime locals, fishing families and second home owners.

The trade offs are real. Insurance in Monroe County is the highest in the state, and flood insurance adds to it. When a hurricane comes, you have a long drive up one road to evacuate. Miami's airport and the big hospitals are about an hour and a half away. Homes are expensive, and winter brings traffic on the highway.

Where it shines

Coastal charmTop 12%

A charming town with a real old center, local shops and pretty streets.

Active outdoorsTop 18%

Hard to beat. Walk the old quarry walls at Windley Key Fossil Reef, paddle to Lignumvitae Key, snorkel the reef or bike the Overseas Heritage Trail. Indian Key closed in April 2026 for a dock rebuild, so check before you paddle out.

On the waterTop 23%

Ocean on one side, Florida Bay on the other. Charter fleets and marinas line the highway, the reef and Alligator Reef Light are a short run out, and the backcountry flats are known for bonefish, tarpon and redfish.

Where it falls short

Meet peopleBottom 33%

Founders Park is the hub, with a pool, courts, a marina and a monthly rec schedule. The Morada Way Art Walk brings art, food and live music on the third Thursday of the month, and the History of Diving Museum hosts evening talks.

BeachesBottom 30%

Not a beach town. Anne's Beach is a shallow strip with a boardwalk where you wade out, and Founders Park and Library Beach Park have small sandy pockets. The water is clear and calm, but there is little sand and no surf.

WalkabilityBottom 9%

The village stretches across several islands along about 18 miles of US 1, so nearly everything is a drive. The Overseas Heritage Trail gives you a bike path beside the road, and Morada Way has a few walkable blocks.

AffordabilityBottom 5%

Among the most expensive places on the Florida coast. Even modest canal homes carry very high prices, and wind and flood insurance in the Keys cost far more than on the mainland.

Airport accessBottom 5%

Miami International (MIA) is about an hour and a half north when US 1 is moving, and longer on a busy weekend. Fort Lauderdale is around two hours. The Key West airport is about two hours the other way.

About average for 3 more: peace and quiet, dining and arts and nightlife
Peace and quietTop 33%

Off the highway it is calm, with dark skies and water on both sides. The trade off is US 1, the only road through, which backs up on weekends, holidays and during lobster mini season in late July.

Dining and artsTop 44%

Good waterfront fish houses, many of which will cook your catch, plus galleries along Morada Way and the History of Diving Museum at mile marker 83. The selection is solid for a small place, without big venues or theaters.

NightlifeBottom 38%

Modest and early. The Lorelei at mile marker 82 has a band by the water most evenings, playing roughly 5 to 9, and it closes at 10. Other tiki bars follow the same pattern. No clubs and no late night district.

Common questions

Is Islamorada walkable?

No. Plan on driving for almost everything. The village stretches across several islands along about 18 miles of US 1, so nearly everything is a drive. The Overseas Heritage Trail gives you a bike path beside the road, and Morada Way has a few walkable blocks.

Does Islamorada have a good beach?

It has a modest one. Better beaches are a drive away. Not a beach town. Anne's Beach is a shallow strip with a boardwalk where you wade out, and Founders Park and Library Beach Park have small sandy pockets. The water is clear and calm, but there is little sand and no surf.

Is Islamorada affordable?

No. It is one of the most expensive spots on the Florida coast. Home prices: About $1.44M median sale (Broker One, Nov 2026). Among the most expensive places on the Florida coast. Even modest canal homes carry very high prices, and wind and flood insurance in the Keys cost far more than on the mainland.

What is the nightlife like in Islamorada?

Quiet, with a handful of places to go. A night out is sunset on the bay side, often at the Lorelei with a band playing by the water, then fresh fish at a dockside table. Most of it is over by 10, because the charter boats leave at dawn.

How busy does Islamorada get?

It is quiet most of the year. Off the highway it is calm, with dark skies and water on both sides. The trade off is US 1, the only road through, which backs up on weekends, holidays and during lobster mini season in late July.

Is Islamorada good for boating and fishing?

Yes. Life here revolves around the water. Ocean on one side, Florida Bay on the other. Charter fleets and marinas line the highway, the reef and Alligator Reef Light are a short run out, and the backcountry flats are known for bonefish, tarpon and redfish.

Which airport is closest to Islamorada?

Miami International (MIA) is about an hour and a half north when US 1 is moving, and longer on a busy weekend. Fort Lauderdale is around two hours. The Key West airport is about two hours the other way.

What should I know before moving to Islamorada?

The main things to plan for: Evacuation distance, high insurance. Florida homeowners insurance averages about $10,384 a year for $250K of dwelling coverage (MoneyGeek, 2026). Get a quote and check the flood zone for any address before you commit.
