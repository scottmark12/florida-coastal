About Everglades City

Everglades City is a small fishing town where the stone crab boats tie up along the Barron River and the Ten Thousand Islands start just past the last dock. The streets are flat and quiet, with a town circle, a few fish houses and old Florida homes. Winters bring anglers, paddlers and seasonal neighbors, and summers are very slow. People here fish, paddle and know each other by name.

The honest part is water. Wilma in 2005, Irma in 2017 and Ian in 2022 all flooded town, and nearly every property sits in a flood zone, so check elevation and insurance before anything else. The nearest airport and hospital are over an hour away, and several restaurants close for the summer. Very few homes sell each year.

Where it shines

Peace and quietTop 18%

One road in, and it dead ends a few miles past town, so nobody is passing through. The whole city is about one square mile. November through April brings anglers, paddlers and tour traffic, and summer is very still.

AffordabilityTop 20%

Much lower than Naples or Marco Island, though so few homes sell that the numbers jump around. Redfin and Zillow put typical prices between about $250,000 and $400,000 over the past year. Nearly every property is in a flood zone and new homes have to be built up high, so flood insurance is a big part of the math.

Coastal charmTop 24%

Some charm: a few older blocks, local spots and low buildings.

Where it falls short

Dining and artsBottom 34%

Seafood is the draw, with stone crab in season at places along the river like Triad and Camellia Street Grill, plus the old Rod and Gun Club. The Museum of the Everglades is open Tuesday through Saturday, and the Smallwood Store museum is about ten minutes down the causeway on Chokoloskee.

Meet peopleBottom 21%

A lot for a town this size. A fresh market sets up Fridays and Sundays beside the post office, a coffee group meets every Thursday, the museum holds free Third Thursday talks, and the Seafood Festival fills town each February. The Friends of the Museum take volunteers.

Airport accessBottom 6%

A long haul. Southwest Florida International (RSW) is about an hour and a half north, and Miami (MIA) is closer to two hours east. The small Everglades Airpark in town serves private planes.

BeachesBottom 3%

No beach in town. This is mangrove coast. The closest sand is on the outer islands by boat, like Indian Key about four and a half miles out, or on Marco Island, about 50 minutes by car.

About average for 4 more: walkability, active outdoors, on the water and nightlife
WalkabilityTop 47%

Small and flat enough to cover on foot or by bike. City Hall sits on the circle with the museum, restaurants and a small grocery close by, and there is a school in town. A full supermarket, a pharmacy and the nearest hospital are 35 to 45 minutes away toward Naples.

Active outdoorsBottom 47%

Wild country in every direction. Everglades National Park starts at the edge of town, with the 99 mile Wilderness Waterway running to Flamingo. Big Cypress National Preserve borders the road in, and the Fakahatchee Strand boardwalk is about 15 minutes away.

On the waterBottom 46%

This is the reason to live here. The Barron River runs through town to Chokoloskee Bay and the Ten Thousand Islands. Everglades Isle has more than 130 docks, there are three boat ramps, and the national park's new visitor center has a kayak launch and boat tours. Stone crab season runs October 15 to May 1.

NightlifeBottom 47%

Early and seasonal. Camellia Street Grill has live music on the river, the Rod and Gun Club holds a First Friday social, and HavAnnA Cafe out on Chokoloskee has run karaoke nights in summer. Most kitchens close by 9, and a few places keep seasonal hours.
