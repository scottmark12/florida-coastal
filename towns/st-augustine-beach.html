About St. Augustine Beach

St. Augustine Beach sits on Anastasia Island, with a wide, flat beach, a pier and a laid back strip of restaurants on A1A. Cross the Bridge of Lions and you're in old St. Augustine, with its fort, brick streets and a calendar full of things to do. You get beach life and a real city, close together.

It's on the Atlantic, so the water is cooler and the surf has more push than the Gulf. Downtown gets crowded on weekends and holidays, and the island has hurricane and erosion exposure. Prices are still more reasonable than most of the Florida coast for what you get.

Where it shines

Active outdoorsTop 3%

Anastasia State Park sits at the north end with four miles of beach, the Ancient Dunes Nature Trail, paddling on Salt Run and 139 campsites. Add a Saturday farmers market at the Amphitheatre and firm sand you can bike at low tide.

BeachesTop 14%

A wide beach, made wider by a 2024 renourishment. Pier Park has more than 200 parking spaces, smaller lots like Pope Road fill in the gaps, and you can drive onto the sand at the A Street and Ocean Trace ramps with a toll from March through September.

WalkabilityTop 25%

You can walk or bike A1A Beach Boulevard between Pier Park, the beach walkovers and a string of restaurants. It is a long, thin town though, so groceries and most errands still mean a car or a longer bike ride.

Where it falls short

On the waterBottom 15%

The ocean pier has been closed for piling repairs since May 2026, with work running into November. For boats, the county keeps free ramps at Frank Butler West on the Intracoastal and at Lighthouse Park on Salt Run.

Airport accessBottom 17%

Jacksonville (JAX) is a little over an hour north. Daytona Beach (DAB) is about an hour south with a shorter list of flights. The small airport in St. Augustine has no airline service right now.
