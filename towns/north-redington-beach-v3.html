About North Redington Beach

North Redington Beach is only a few blocks wide. Resorts and condo buildings sit on the Gulf, and quiet streets of single family homes run out into Boca Ciega Bay on narrow strips of land, many with a dock out back. Mornings are for walking the beach, evenings are for the bay. A lot of owners are retirees and second home people, and it feels calm most of the year.

The trade off is water. Helene and Milton flooded many bay side homes in 2024, and some streets are still rebuilding. Flood insurance and rebuild rules matter a lot here, so look at the flood zone, elevation and permit history before anything else. Prices are high for the size of the homes, and Gulf Boulevard gets busy in spring and on summer weekends.

A week here

Most morningsAn early walk on the sand behind the condos, before the beach chairs come out.
Late afternoonTake the boat or a paddleboard out on Boca Ciega Bay while the water is flat.
SaturdayRun up the beach road to the John's Pass area with friends, or anchor off one of the bay islands.
Saturday nightThe Friendly Tavern in Redington Shores. A beach bar that's been around nearly 80 years, where the regulars know each other and everyone looks out for each other.
SundaySunset on the Gulf from the beach at the end of your street, with the neighbors who do the same thing every week.

Where it falls short

A small town of roughly 1,500 year round residents with several little parks, so meeting people happens at the tennis courts, on the beach and through town events. Organized clubs are mostly found off the island.

Limited in town: resort restaurants and a beach bar plus a few casual places along Gulf Boulevard. Madeira Beach, Seminole and St. Pete Beach are the go to for more variety.

AffordabilityBottom 12%

Common questions

Is North Redington Beach walkable?

In parts. Some errands are walkable, most are a short drive. Moderately walkable. The town is only about ten blocks long, so the beach, the parks and the resort restaurants are close, but groceries and errands mean a drive.

Does North Redington Beach have a good beach?

Yes, a very good one. A wide, soft Gulf beach running the length of town, widened by renourishment over the years. Gulf Front Park and the crosswalks on Gulf Boulevard make access easy.

Is North Redington Beach affordable?

It is on the expensive side for the Florida coast. Home prices: About $1.08M median sale over the last year (Momentum, Nov 2026). The priciest of the Redington towns. Redfin put the September 2026 median sale price near $886K, and bay front single family homes run well above that. Insurance and elevation costs add up fast.

What is the nightlife like in North Redington Beach?

Quiet, with a handful of places to go. Most evenings mean a sunset walk and a drink at the resort beach bar, or friends gathering on a dock on the bay side. A late night means driving to Madeira Beach. It is calm and early to bed.

How busy does North Redington Beach get?

It is quiet most of the year. Quiet. Single family streets sit on bay side fingers away from Gulf Boulevard, and the resort side gets busier in spring. Storm rebuilding still brings some construction noise.

Is North Redington Beach good for boating and fishing?

Yes, with decent access to ramps and marinas. Bay side homes on the fingers back onto the Intracoastal, so docks and boat lifts are common there. The Gulf is out front for swimming and fishing.

Which airport is closest to North Redington Beach?

St. Pete Clearwater (PIE) is about 25 minutes away and Tampa International (TPA) about 40 minutes.

What should I know before moving to North Redington Beach?

The main things to plan for: 2024 Helene and Milton flooding; low lying bay side streets, storm surge risk, high flood insurance and rebuild costs.
