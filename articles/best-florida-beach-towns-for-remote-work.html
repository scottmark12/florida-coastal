The Best Florida Beach Towns for Remote Work Are Near an Airport, and That's Where the People Are

A real airport, a desk downtown and something to do after work. In our scores, the Florida towns near big airports are also where it's easiest to meet people.

By Mark and Betsy ScottNov 3, 20266 min read

The first month of working from the beach is wonderful. You close the laptop at five and walk to the water.

The third month is when people find out whether they picked the right town. The flight for the Monday meeting is two hours away, or there's nobody to have dinner with on a Wednesday.

The beach is the easy part. The two hard parts, getting out and getting together, point to the same towns.

Airports and people travel together

We score every town on airport access and on how easy it is to meet people: weekly clubs, rec leagues, meetups and community events. None of the 21 towns that score a 1 or 2 for airport access gets a 4 or better for meeting people. Among the 26 towns with a 5 for airport access, 54% do.

So you rarely have to choose. The towns near a big airport are the ones with run clubs, art walks and coworking desks, and those are the towns that make remote work feel like a life instead of a long vacation.

None of the Florida coastal towns far from an airport scores high for meeting people.

The nine we'd look at first

Seventeen towns carry our Remote Work style. We ranked them on airport access, meeting people and dining and arts.

Tampa puts the busiest airport on the Gulf coast about 15 minutes from downtown, Industrious has a coworking location on East Jackson Street, and after work there's the 2.6 mile Riverwalk, the Armature Works food hall and the free streetcar to Ybor City. No Gulf beach in town; plan on 40 minutes.

St. Petersburg gives you two airports and a coworking space at 200 Central Avenue, a short walk from the Pier and the Dalí. Evenings are Jannus Live, the Second Saturday ArtWalk or the SunRunner out to the Gulf for sunset. Prices are up about 7 percent in a year, to near $430K.

Sarasota has its airport about 15 minutes from downtown and day passes at the Regus at 1990 Main Street. The 99 Bottles Run Club meets Wednesdays at 6, and Lido Beach is about ten minutes over the bridge. US 41 backs up all winter.

South Beach is one of the few places on the coast you can live without a car, with a coworking space on Lincoln Road and MIA 20 to 25 minutes away. About $600K, plus fees and flood insurance.

Fort Lauderdale has FLL 10 to 15 minutes away, Brightline to Miami, West Palm and Orlando, and Industrious on Las Olas. After work: the Water Taxi or 43 pickleball courts at The Fort.

Delray Beach has the free Freebee shuttle, a Wednesday night run club and an art walk the first Friday of the month. Condos run about $210K.

Boca Raton sits between two airports with Brightline downtown and Mizner Park for after work. Outside downtown it's built for driving.

Dunedin is the small town pick: coffee on Main Street, the Pinellas Trail after work and the Wednesday open jam at Dunedin Brewery, with PIE about 25 minutes away.

Key West has its own airport with six airlines, which matters on an island about four hours by car from Miami. About $1.05M.

Also on the list: Clearwater, with two airports close, quiet Bal Harbour, and Mexico Beach if a wide empty beach matters more than a quick flight.

Before you choose a town

  • Book a fake trip. Price and time a Monday morning flight from the airport you'd use.
  • Test the internet at the exact address, not the town average.
  • Find your Wednesday. Pick a run club, jam or art walk and go every week. Finding your people has more.
  • Price the whole year. Taxes reset when you buy, flood coverage is separate and condo dues are rising. The six bills nobody puts on the listing, our insurance guide and the hurricane checklist cover it.

The beach is the easy part. Pick the town with a flight and a Wednesday night.
