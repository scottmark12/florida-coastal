The Best Florida Beach Towns to Live In: All 167 Ranked

We scored every beach town we cover on nine things that shape daily life, from walkability and beaches to schools and the drive to the airport. Dunedin comes out on top, and the famous names land lower than you'd think.

By Mark and Betsy ScottNov 16, 20266 min read

Ask ten people what makes a beach town a good place to live and you'll get ten answers. So we didn't pick one. We scored all 167 towns we cover on the same nine things, weighed them the same, and ranked them.

Dunedin comes out on top. It's the 3rd most walkable town on our list and has the 5th most coastal charm, and with Honeymoon and Caladesi islands just offshore its beaches rank 15th. Naples and Venice tie for second on score, then Fernandina Beach and Stuart.

No town wins everything

Even Dunedin has soft spots. It ranks 84th for boating and waterfront access and 86th for affordability. The towns at the top aren't the best at any one thing. They're good at almost everything and bad at almost nothing.

30A shows the flip side. It has the 2nd best beaches and the 3rd best public schools on our list, but it ranks 154th of 167 for affordability, and that one score holds it at No. 12.

The Gulf and the Atlantic split the top 10

Five of the top 10 are on the Gulf and five are on the Atlantic. Go to the top 25 and the Atlantic pulls ahead, 13 to 10, with two from the Panhandle: 30A at No. 12 and Pensacola at No. 24. The Keys' best is Key West at No. 36.

The famous names land in the middle

Siesta Key has the No. 1 beach on our list and ranks 50th overall. Destin is 7th for beaches and 47th overall. Panama City Beach has the 3rd best beach and comes in at No. 80. Key West is the 4th most walkable town we cover and 36th overall.

The pattern is the same each time. These towns are known for one or two things, and they give up points elsewhere: Siesta Key ranks 150th for affordability, Key West 156th, and Destin and Panama City Beach sit near the bottom for coastal charm. Great places to visit can be harder places to live.

What we score

Nine categories, each ranked from 1 to 167. The overall score is a blend of all nine, and affordability counts one and a half times because it touches everything else. We left out quiet versus lively on purpose: that's a preference, not a measure of quality.

The nine categories

  • Walkability: how many daily needs are within a short walk of the center of town.
  • Beaches: public beach access, sand and water quality, and national beach rankings.
  • On the water: marinas, boat ramps, fishing piers and waterfront access.
  • Active outdoors: paddling, a real surf break, trails and bike paths, and nature preserves close by.
  • Social scene: restaurants, cafes, gyms, clubs and community spots near downtown, in a town the size where you keep running into people.
  • Coastal charm: a real historic downtown, local spots over chains, no wall of condo towers, and cared for streets.
  • Public schools: the Florida Department of Education grade for the county district that serves the town (2025-26).
  • Airport access: drive time to the nearest major airport.
  • Affordability: home prices against the Florida median, plus typical insurance costs.
