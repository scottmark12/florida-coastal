8 Florida "Beach Towns" Where You Can't Walk to the Beach

Some Florida towns sell the beach life from a bridge, a boat or a drive away. Know which ones before you sign.

Area Guides·By Florida Coastal·Oct 1, 2026·3 min read

Florida has a naming problem. Plenty of towns sell the beach life, and some even put the word in their name, but the sand is a bridge, a boat or a drive away.

That is not always a bad thing. A few of these are among the best towns on our site. You just want to know before you sign.

Highland Beach

The ocean is right there. A public way onto it is not. There is no public beach access or parking in Highland Beach today, and a planned county beach park was still waiting on permits in spring 2026. Check that any home or condo comes with its own access, or plan on Delray Beach next door.

Apollo Beach

Apollo Beach sits on Tampa Bay, not the Gulf. You get canals, bay sunsets and a manatee viewing center. For a Gulf beach day you are crossing the bay.

Dunedin

Dunedin scores a 5 for walkability and it is the most common top match on our site. But its beaches are state park islands. Honeymoon Island is a causeway drive, and Caladesi Island, third on Dr. Beach's 2026 list, takes a boat.

St. Augustine

The old city of St. Augustine has a bayfront, a fort and brick streets. It does not have an ocean beach. Anastasia State Park and Vilano Beach are each a bridge away.

Punta Gorda

Fourth on moveBuddha's 2026 list of cities people want to move to. Punta Gorda is a harbor town, with canals and sailboats on Charlotte Harbor. The Gulf beaches are a drive.

Cape Coral

Cape Coral has about 400 miles of canals and one small river beach at the Yacht Club. The Gulf is a boat ride or a couple of bridges away. Life here is on the water every day. It is just in a boat, not on a towel.

Palm Bay

The Palm Bay area is 13th on U-Haul's 2025 list of growth metros, and the city has no oceanfront at all. Palm Bay sits on the Indian River side. The ocean is across the lagoon in Melbourne Beach and Indialantic.

Ponte Vedra Beach

Miles of ocean and very few public ways to reach it. In Ponte Vedra Beach, Mickler's Landing, with about 200 spaces, is the only large public lot at the north end.

Ask yourself one question

How many days a month will you really put your feet in the sand? If the answer is 20, cross these off. If it is four, a bay sunset, a canal or a walkable downtown may matter more, and that opens up a lot more of the map.
