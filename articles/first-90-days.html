Your First 90 Days: How to Get Settled and Build a Community in a New Town

Unpacking is the easy half. Here is a simple plan for making a new Florida town feel like home.

Moving Tips·By Florida Coastal·Oct 5, 2026·3 min read

A move has two halves. The first is getting set up: the license, the doctor, the grocery store you like. The second is getting connected, and that is the one people leave to chance. It rarely happens on its own, so give it a plan too.

Week one: get your bearings

  • Find your daily five. Grocery store, pharmacy, coffee, a place to exercise and a hardware store. Once those are set, the town stops feeling like a vacation.
  • Drive your real routes at rush hour. Work, the airport and the nearest hospital. You want to know the bad version before you need it.
  • Walk your street at different times of day. Say hello first. Most people are waiting for the new person to do it.
  • Look up your storm basics. The state's Know Your Zone map shows your evacuation zone, lettered A through F, with A the first to leave. Check your flood zone too. They are two different maps.

The first month: make it official

  • Get your Florida driver license. New residents have 30 days. Plan to register your car around the same time. Your county tax collector's office handles both.
  • File for homestead if you bought. If the home is your permanent residence on January 1, you can apply with your county property appraiser by March 1. It lowers your taxable value and limits how fast your assessed value can rise.
  • Register to vote and get a library card. The library is usually the best list of classes, clubs and free events in town.
  • Pick your doctor, dentist and vet now. New patient appointments can take weeks, and longer in winter when towns are full.

Months one to three: build your week

The fastest way into a town is a standing weekly date, something that happens at the same time every week where the same faces show up. Aim for three:

  • Something active. A run club, pickleball open play, a sailing club, a morning walk on the trail.
  • Something local. The farmers market, an art walk, a free concert downtown. Go to the same stand or the same spot each time.
  • Something useful. A volunteer shift at a garden, a theater, a trail group or a festival. It's the quickest way to feel like you belong somewhere.

Go three times before you decide. The first visit is always a little awkward, and the third is where people start to know your name. Our guide to finding your people lists real weekly events town by town.

Small habits that speed it up

  • Become a regular. Same coffee shop, same morning. Familiar turns into friendly faster than you'd think.
  • Say yes to the first three invitations. Even the ones that sound like not your thing.
  • Host something small. Sunset on the porch or drinks in the driveway. It doesn't have to be a party.
  • Learn the names on your street. Both sides and across. Write them down if you have to.
  • Ask one question everywhere. "What should I not miss here?" Locals love answering it.

Give it a full year

A Florida beach town is a different place in February than it is in August. Winter is full and busy. Summer is hot, quiet and stormy. Don't judge the town, or your social life, until you've seen both. A lot of the people you meet in season will be back next year.

Pick a town that makes it easy

Some towns do half this work for you. A walkable downtown means you run into people without trying, and a full calendar means there's always a next thing.
