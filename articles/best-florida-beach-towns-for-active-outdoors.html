The Best Florida Beach Towns for an Active Outdoor Life Are a Long Way From the Airport

Reef dives, spring rivers, long trails and empty seashore. The better a Florida town's outdoors, the farther it usually is from an airport.

By Mark and Betsy ScottNov 3, 20266 min read

We score all 167 towns on the Florida coast on the same ten point scorecard, built from things we can count and check, not reviews or ads. Fifty towns carry our Active Outdoors style. We ranked them on our outdoors score and kept a spread across the coast, so there's a strong pick near wherever you're looking. Every score below shows where the town ranks out of all 167. How we score

1. Key Largo

Florida KeysTypical home price About $985KBest for Diving, snorkeling, fishing

No. 1 on our listOutdoors and wellness 3/5 #59 of 167

Key Largo is what people mean when they talk about Florida diving. Reach the reef by dive boat, snorkel trip or glass bottom boat. There are few sandy beaches, insurance runs high and the evacuation drive is long. The Keys, Mile by Mile compares it with the islands farther down.

Why it ranks
World class diving and fishing
The closest Key to Miami
Keep in mind
A long evacuation drive, high insurance
See Key Largo's full scorecard →

2. Weeki Wachee

Nature Coast (Gulf)Typical home price About $341KBest for Paddling a spring river

No. 2 on our listOutdoors and wellness 3/5 #60 of 167

Weeki Wachee has a clear, spring fed river running a few miles out to the Gulf, and paddling it in the morning is the whole reason to be here. The state park has the mermaid shows and a guided kayak run, and the park limits paddle launches. It's spread out, dining is thin and Helene flooded low areas of western Hernando in 2024. Our guide to Florida's springs explains why this water changes your weekends.

Why it ranks
Clear spring river and huge public lands
Lower prices than the coast just west
Keep in mind
Riverfront homes near the coast sit in surge zones and Helene (2024) flooded low areas of western Hernando; paddle launch limits at the state park
See Weeki Wachee's full scorecard →

3. Navarre Beach

PanhandleTypical home price About $880KBest for Pier fishing, calm sound paddling

No. 3 on our listOutdoors and wellness 4/5 #17 of 167

Navarre Beach has open dunes, protected seashore next door and a calm sound side for paddling. One causeway on and off.

Why it ranks
Uncrowded beaches with protected seashore next door
Calm sound side for paddling and kids
Keep in mind
Barrier island storm exposure. One causeway on and off the island
See Navarre Beach's full scorecard →

4. Crystal River

Nature Coast (Gulf)Typical home price About $272KBest for Manatees, springs, scalloping

No. 4 on our listOutdoors and wellness 4/5 #32 of 167

Crystal River is the best value here at about $272K. Hundreds of manatees spend the winter in Kings Bay, and paddling with them at first light is hard to beat anywhere. In summer you go out for scallops. It's about an hour and a half to Tampa's airport.

Why it ranks
Springs, manatees and scalloping
Affordable
Keep in mind
Storm surge flooding. Hit by recent hurricanes
See Crystal River's full scorecard →

5. Perdido Key

PanhandleTypical home price About $591KBest for Lagoon paddling, open seashore

No. 5 on our listOutdoors and wellness 4/5 #44 of 167

Perdido Key means Big Lagoon in the morning and Johnson Beach, where the road runs out into undeveloped seashore, in the afternoon. Mostly condos, and very car dependent.

Why it ranks
Quiet beaches and lots of protected land
Easy boating on the bays behind the island
Keep in mind
Barrier island hurricane exposure. Ivan and Sally both hit hard here
See Perdido Key's full scorecard →

6. Cocoa Beach

Space Coast (Atlantic)Typical home price About $469KBest for Surfing

No. 6 on our listOutdoors and wellness 5/5 #6 of 167

Cocoa Beach is the classic surf town. Paddle out near the pier in the morning and watch a rocket launch from the sand in the evening. Orlando's airport is about 50 minutes away.

Why it ranks
Surf culture
Near Orlando
Keep in mind
Hurricane exposure
See Cocoa Beach's full scorecard →

7. Atlantic Beach

Northeast AtlanticTypical home price About $630KBest for Surf, then park trails

No. 7 on our listOutdoors and wellness 5/5 #5 of 167

Atlantic Beach is surf at sunrise, a bike ride to coffee at Beaches Town Center and the trails at Hanna Park. Winters run cooler than South Florida.

Why it ranks
A walkable center by the beach
Great parks
Keep in mind
Hurricane exposure, rising prices
See Atlantic Beach's full scorecard →

8. Palm Harbor

Tampa Bay (Gulf)Typical home price About $365KBest for Riding a long paved trail

No. 8 on our listOutdoors and wellness 3/5 #104 of 167

Palm Harbor has the Pinellas Trail running right through the old downtown, so a ride to Wall Springs Park and its 35 foot tower is just part of the week. The beaches are a short drive.

Why it ranks
The Pinellas Trail and great parks right through town
Calm waterfront villages and Honeymoon Island close by
Keep in mind
Waterfront pockets like Ozona and Crystal Beach are in flood zones; US 19 traffic; beaches are a short drive, not in town
See Palm Harbor's full scorecard →

9. Melbourne Beach

Space Coast (Atlantic)Typical home price About $659KBest for Sea turtle beaches, quiet

No. 9 on our listOutdoors and wellness 4/5 #26 of 167

Melbourne Beach sits next to Archie Carr National Wildlife Refuge, one of the most important sea turtle nesting beaches in the world. Book a guided night walk in summer. Few restaurants and few homes for sale.

Why it ranks
Very quiet and residential
Natural, uncrowded beaches
Keep in mind
Barrier island hurricane exposure
See Melbourne Beach's full scorecard →

10. Palm Coast

Central AtlanticTypical home price About $350KBest for Trails and canal boating

No. 10 on our listOutdoors and wellness 4/5 #19 of 167

Palm Coast has more than 130 miles of trails and a web of saltwater canals, plus Washington Oaks Gardens on a Sunday. No real downtown, and you drive to the beach.

Why it ranks
Canal homes and trails at fair prices
Quiet
Keep in mind
Canal homes can flood in storms. Car dependent
See Palm Coast's full scorecard →

Also carrying the style: Satellite Beach and Indialantic for surf, St. Augustine Beach, Ormond Beach, Jupiter, Islamorada, Sanibel Island and 30A, where we live.

The most outdoors for the money is on the Gulf

Typical prices on our list run from about $272K in Crystal River to about $985K in Key Largo. The Nature Coast and Palm Coast give you the most for the money. Barrier islands and the Keys cost more, and so does insuring them. Our town pages use a statewide average of about $10,384 a year for $250K of dwelling coverage (MoneyGeek, 2026). The six bills nobody puts on the listing walks through the rest.

Before you choose a town

  • Time the airport drive you'll actually use, both ways, on a weekday.
  • Pick your sport first. The Keys for the reef, the Nature Coast for springs and manatees, the Panhandle for empty sand, the Atlantic for surf. Gulf, Atlantic or Panhandle? goes deeper.
  • Check the flood zone and elevation. The water that makes these towns great is what brings the storms. Read our insurance guide and the hurricane season checklist.
  • Check the launch rules for the river, spring or park you plan to use most.

Choose the morning you want. Then make peace with the drive to the airport.
