What Is It Like Living in the Florida Panhandle?

Slow days, good seafood, whiter sand, cooler winters and, outside 30A, lower home prices than South Florida. The Panhandle has also taken more direct hurricane hits than any other part of the state.

By Mark and Betsy ScottNov 22, 20267 min read

Drive west from Tallahassee and Florida starts to feel like somewhere else. The clocks fall back an hour at the Apalachicola River, Mobile and New Orleans are closer than Miami, and on fall Saturdays plenty of people are pulling for Alabama or Auburn.

The Panhandle has some of the whitest sand and clearest water in the country, cooler winters than the rest of the state and, outside of 30A and the barrier islands, home prices well below South Florida's. It has also taken more direct hurricane hits than any other part of Florida.

Daily life

Life here moves slowly, and most people who move here want it that way. Outside of summer, a full weekend might be the Saturday market on Palafox Street in downtown Pensacola, a morning on a charter boat out of Destin Harbor and dinner early enough to catch the sunset. In winter, many beach restaurants cut back their hours, and a few close for a couple of weeks.

In a lot of ways, this is where the South comes to retire. Plenty of the people who settle here grew up spending summers on this coast, and they come back from Alabama, Georgia and Tennessee for good. The quieter towns to the east skew older. In Franklin County, home to Apalachicola and St. George Island, about 28 percent of residents are 65 or older, compared with about 22 percent statewide, according to the Census Bureau.

The food is better than the pace suggests, and most of it starts with what comes off the boats. Joe Patti's, the Pensacola seafood market that began in 1931 with a couple selling fish off their front porch, still sells shrimp and Gulf fish on the waterfront. Bud and Alley's opened in Seaside in January 1986, when the town had about a dozen houses. Its owner has said there were five places to eat on 30A back then. Now there are more than a hundred.

Social life tends to run through church, school sports, the bases and the water. Pensacola has celebrated Mardi Gras since 1874, and its parades throw MoonPies along with the beads. Nights end early. If you want restaurants that are still full at 10 on a Tuesday night, this is not the place.

The sand

Panhandle sand is nearly pure quartz washed down from the Appalachians, which is why it is so white and squeaks underfoot. The water off Destin, 30A and Navarre Beach turns that emerald green on calm days. Long stretches are protected, including Gulf Islands National Seashore around Pensacola Beach and the state parks on St. George Island and Cape San Blas.

Winters

Pensacola's normal January runs a high of about 63 degrees and a low of about 44, according to the National Weather Service. Miami's average January temperature is about 67. Freezes happen most winters, and snow fell near Pensacola in both January 2025 and January 2026. Summers are as hot and humid as anywhere in Florida, with afternoon storms most days.

For people who want something closer to four seasons, that is part of the draw.

Hurricanes

From 1851 through 2018, NOAA counted 66 hurricane direct hits on Northwest Florida, including 14 majors, the most of any part of the state. Recent ones include Ivan near Pensacola in 2004, Michael as a Category 5 at Mexico Beach in 2018, Sally near Pensacola in 2020, and Idalia, Debby and Helene in the Big Bend in 2023 and 2024.

Because of that history, buyers here pay close attention to elevation, roof age and the year a house was built. Our flood risk rankings show how much location matters even here: Fort Walton Beach, on the mainland side of the sound, scores 1.9 out of 10, while Navarre Beach and Pensacola Beach on the barrier island score 7.9 and 7.1.

Time zones, bases and airports

Central time covers most of the Panhandle west of the Apalachicola River, including Pensacola, Destin, 30A, Panama City Beach and Mexico Beach. Port St. Joe and Apalachicola are on Eastern time, so a short drive along US 98 can cross a time zone.

The military has a large presence. Naval Air Station Pensacola, Eglin Air Force Base, Hurlburt Field and Tyndall Air Force Base are major employers, and many service members retire here after a final posting.

The region has three commercial airports: Pensacola, Destin Fort Walton Beach and Northwest Florida Beaches, near Panama City Beach. Many trips connect through Atlanta, Dallas, Charlotte or Houston.

Growth

In places, quickly. Walton County grew about 20% from 2020 to 2025, to roughly 90,500 people, according to Florida's official state estimates. Santa Rosa County, which includes Navarre and Gulf Breeze, grew about 12.5% and Okaloosa about 7%. Summer traffic on US 98 through Destin and along 30A is a familiar complaint.

Who it suits

The Panhandle tends to suit people who want a beach town that still feels like the small town South: military families, retirees looking for cooler winters and lower prices than the peninsula, and the many people from Alabama, Georgia and Tennessee who grew up vacationing here. It is a harder fit for anyone who needs a big job market, nonstop flights or the lowest possible storm risk.

Common questions

Is the Florida Panhandle a good place to live?

For many people, yes: beautiful beaches, cooler winters, a small town feel and prices well below South Florida outside of 30A. The main trade off is that it has had more hurricane direct hits than any other part of Florida.

What time zone is the Florida Panhandle in?

Most of the Panhandle west of the Apalachicola River, including Pensacola, Destin, 30A and Panama City Beach, is on Central time. Port St. Joe and Apalachicola are on Eastern time.

Does it get cold in the Florida Panhandle?

By Florida standards, yes. Pensacola's normal January low is about 44 degrees, freezes happen most winters, and snow fell near Pensacola in January 2025 and January 2026.

What is the cheapest place to live in the Florida Panhandle near the beach?

Among the towns we cover, Pensacola and Fort Walton Beach have typical home prices around $345K to $350K and condos under about $185K, with beaches across the bridge.

How often do hurricanes hit the Florida Panhandle?

NOAA counted 66 hurricane direct hits on Northwest Florida from 1851 to 2018, including 14 majors. That count includes Michael in 2018, and Sally, Idalia, Debby and Helene have hit Northwest Florida since.
