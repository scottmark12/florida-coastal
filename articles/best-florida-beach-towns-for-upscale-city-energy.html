The Best Florida Beach Towns for Upscale City Energy, and Why None of Them Are Quiet

Great restaurants, late nights and the water close by. Only ten Florida coastal towns have this mix, and not one with top nightlife is quiet.

By Mark and Betsy ScottNov 3, 20265 min read

Atlantic Avenue in Delray Beach runs straight to the ocean. You can walk it for breakfast, swim at the end of the street and walk back for dinner and music, with the galleries of Pineapple Grove one block over. It stays lively all year.

Which is exactly why it isn't quiet.

You can't have both

This style is for people who want serious restaurants, a night out that doesn't end at nine and the water close by. When we scored 167 towns on the coast, the trade was the cleanest we found anywhere. Among the towns with the sleepiest nights, 82% get a 4 or better for peace and quiet. Among the 10 towns with the best nightlife, none do.

If you want both, the answer is usually two neighborhoods, not one town. Live a few streets back, or a town over, and go out where it's loud. Several of the cities below are big enough to do that inside their own limits.

Of the 10 Florida coastal towns with the best nightlife, not one is quiet.

The eight we'd look at first

Only ten towns on the coast carry what we call the High Energy style. We ranked them on dining and arts and on nightlife.

South Beach is the purest version: art deco, ocean views and a 5 for dining, nightlife and walkability. You run the beachwalk at sunrise and head to dinner late, and with Lincoln Road a pedestrian mall you can live here without a car. It's expensive, with flooding, traffic and high insurance.

Delray Beach is the one we'd point most people to, for everything above. Houses run about $768K, but condos and townhomes are about $210K, which makes it more reachable than it looks.

St. Petersburg is the Gulf's answer: a walkable downtown, the Dalí Museum, murals on every block and the Saturday Morning Market, at about $430K. The beaches are about 20 minutes away and low neighborhoods flood.

Miami is Brickell, Edgewater, Coconut Grove and Wynwood, each a different city, plus The Underline's trail and gardens under the Metrorail. The ocean beaches are across the bay, and condo fees keep rising.

Fort Lauderdale is a boating city: out through the canals in the afternoon, dinner on Las Olas at night, the airport close. Busy, pricey and prone to flooding.

Tampa has the Riverwalk, about 2.6 miles of waterfront path, Ybor City after work and a Lightning game downtown, with the airport about 15 minutes away. The beaches are about 40 minutes off.

Key West is bikes, live music and the nightly sunset at Mallory Square, at about $1.05M and a long way from the mainland.

Miramar Beach is the Panhandle entry, between Destin and 30A: sugar white sand, Sandestin's shops, a Saturday market on Grand Boulevard and a lot going on all year. Summer traffic on US 98 is the cost. It's close to home for us on 30A.

The other two: Panama City Beach brings big beaches and a lot going on after dark, and Hallandale Beach has oceanfront condos for less than Miami Beach, with Gulfstream Park for racing, shops and dinner.

City life costs less than you'd think

St. Petersburg (about $430K) and Tampa (about $479K) are the most reachable, and Delray condos run about $210K. Key West is the top of the range. In the Miami area and Fort Lauderdale, a condo is the usual way in, so read the fees and any assessments before you fall for the view. The six bills nobody puts on the listing covers what to add.

Before you choose a town

  • Visit on a weeknight and walk home from dinner. Notice the noise on the block you'd live on.
  • Check the flood zone and elevation of the exact address. King tides in Miami and low streets in St. Petersburg and Tampa are real. Start with our insurance guide and the hurricane season checklist.
  • Decide how much you'll drive. South Beach, Delray and Key West score a 5 for walkability. Eight beach towns where you can park the car has more.
  • Torn between two? Read Sarasota vs St. Petersburg or Gulf, Atlantic or Panhandle?

Pick the street you want to come home to. Then find the one you want to go out on.
