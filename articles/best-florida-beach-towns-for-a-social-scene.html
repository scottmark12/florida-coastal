The Best Florida Beach Towns for a Social Scene Aren't on the Best Beaches

We scored 167 coastal towns on how easy it is to meet people. The winners have real downtowns. Only 2 of Florida's 26 best beach towns made the cut.

By Mark and Betsy ScottNov 3, 20266 min read

Pensacola Beach has some of the whitest sand in the country. When we score it on how easy it is to meet people, it gets a 2.

Drive 20 minutes back across the bay to downtown Pensacola and the score doubles. On Saturday mornings Palafox Street turns into a market. There are restaurants and galleries up and down the street, and in season you can catch the Blue Angels practicing. Most people picture the beach when they think of Pensacola. For meeting people, downtown is the better bet.

If the part of moving to Florida that worries you most is knowing nobody, that is the most useful thing we found when we scored 167 towns along the coast. The towns that make it easy to meet people are almost never the ones on the postcards.

We've moved around Florida ourselves, from Tallahassee to 30A, and both times what made a new place feel like home was finding our people fast. Here is where that's easiest, and why.

The beach doesn't make the friends

We score every town from 1 to 5 on meeting people: weekly clubs, rec leagues, meetups, volunteer groups and community events. Then we score everything else, from beaches to walkability.

Twenty six towns get a 5 for beaches. Only two of them, Panama City Beach and New Smyrna Beach, score a 4 or better for meeting people. Our own town, 30A, has some of the best sand in Florida and scores a 3. We'd know.

What the social towns have instead is a downtown. Of everything we score, meeting people tracks most closely with dining and arts, then with walkability. The chart below shows the walkability side.

In the towns where you need a car for everything, 1 in 30 makes it easy to meet people. In the towns where you can walk to most of what you need, it's 3 in 4.

Of the 26 towns with the best beaches in Florida, two make it easy to meet people.

Seventeen towns carry our Social Scene style. We ranked them on nightlife and meeting people.

Miami is the obvious answer, and it earns it. It's the only town on the coast with a 5 for nightlife and a 5 for meeting people. Brickell, Edgewater, Coconut Grove and Wynwood each feel like a different city, and The Underline, a park and trail under the Metrorail tracks with courts, gardens and a bike path, is the kind of place where a weekly routine builds itself. Go in clear eyed. Traffic is real, low streets flood and condo fees keep climbing.

Jacksonville Beach is the one we'd send a surfer to: a real beach town with a big city next door, patios a block from the sand, and summer weekends that get packed.

Pensacola is our favorite surprise on the list, for everything above. It's also more than 450 years old, the airport is about 15 minutes from downtown, and a typical home runs about $367K. That airport matters more than you'd think once the family starts visiting.

Fort Myers has the brick streets of the River District, monthly art and music walks, and the Edison and Ford winter estates on the river. The beaches are 30 to 40 minutes away, and Ian hit here in 2022.

Fort Walton Beach is close to home for us and easy to like. It's unpretentious, the old downtown is walkable, Okaloosa Island's beaches are just over the bridge, and the Landing on Santa Rosa Sound has free Friday concerts. At about $319K it's the most affordable town on this list. Brooks Bridge construction runs until about 2029, and Highway 98 traffic is no joke.

Lake Worth Beach is the artsy one. Galleries on Lake Avenue, live music downtown, an old school public beach and pier across the lagoon, and every February hundreds of artists chalk the streets for the Street Painting Festival. Some blocks near Dixie Highway and US 1 are rough around the edges.

Key West scores a 5 for meeting people, and if you've been, you know why. Every evening the town walks down to Mallory Square for the sunset. It's also about $1.05M for a typical home, a long way from the mainland, with high insurance. A wonderful place to be social, and a big commitment to live.

Melbourne doesn't get talked about much, and it should. Two walkable old downtowns, First Friday in the Eau Gallie Arts District, its own airport about 10 minutes away, and the ocean about 15 minutes across the causeway.

Panama City Beach is the exception that proves the rule: a 5 for beaches and a 4 for meeting people, with live music near the pier at night. It's very busy in season, spring break brings a crowd, and we rode out Michael in 2018 just down the road, so we don't say hurricane exposure lightly.

Also carrying the style: South Beach, Fort Lauderdale, Delray Beach, Jacksonville, Cocoa Village, Miramar Beach, North Miami Beach and Palm Harbor. If you want something small, Cocoa Village has a little brick downtown with pubs, a historic playhouse and concerts in the riverfront park.

Being social doesn't cost more

You might expect the lively towns to be the expensive ones. In our data, a town's meeting people score has almost nothing to do with its home prices. Four of the nine above come in under about $370K: Fort Walton Beach, Fort Myers, Melbourne and Pensacola. What you usually give up is the walk to the water. For more towns at the lower end, see Florida's most affordable beach towns.

Show up to the same thing every week

That's most of it. Pick two things, the Saturday market on Palafox, an art walk in Fort Myers, First Friday in Eau Gallie, the Friday concerts at the Landing, and go every week for a month. The faces start to repeat. Our guides to finding your people on the Florida coast and your first 90 days in a new town go deeper.

Before you choose a town

  • Walk the downtown. Go on a weeknight, not just a Saturday. See what's open and who's out.
  • Time the beach drive. Melbourne is about 15 minutes, Pensacola about 20, Fort Myers 30 to 40. Decide what you'll actually do. The beach towns where you can't walk to the beach helps.
  • Check the flood zone and get insurance quotes for the exact address before you make an offer. Start with our insurance guide and hurricane season guide.
  • Try it off season. How to test drive a town has a plan.

The beach is why people move to Florida. The people are why they stay.
