The Best Florida Beach Towns for Healthy Living (2026)

Where daily walks, time outside and friendly faces come easy, including the certified Blue Zones communities around Naples. Ten towns, ranked.

Style Guides·By Florida Coastal·Nov 3, 2026·6 min read

The best Florida beach towns for healthy, outdoor living are Sarasota, where you walk the bayfront at sunrise and the community calendar never runs dry, 30A, where a paved trail links small walkable beach towns and coastal dune lakes, and Naples, one of eight Southwest Florida communities certified as a Blue Zones Community. In all three, moving every day, getting outside and running into people you know happen without much planning.

The Blue Zones Project works with towns to make healthy habits like walking, eating well and staying connected the easy choice. Of the 167 towns we score, 13 carry our Blue Zone vibe, and none of them has it as its lead vibe, so we ranked them on three scores together: outdoors and wellness, walkability, and how easy it is to meet people. Only three towns on this list hold the official certification, and we mark them below.

1. Sarasota

Sarasota tops the list because it scores high on all three counts, with a 5 for meeting people. You walk the bayfront at sunrise, wander the 66 acres of The Ringling in the afternoon and catch a show downtown at night. St. Armands Circle is a walkable ring of shops and restaurants a few blocks from Lido Beach. The catch: heavy traffic in season, bridge traffic to the beach, prices that have climbed, and storm surge from Helene and Milton in 2024. See Sarasota, or compare it in Sarasota vs St. Petersburg.

2. 30A (Santa Rosa Beach)

The Timpoochee Trail is a paved path linking the 30A communities, so a normal morning is a bike ride to coffee in Seaside and a paddle on a coastal dune lake before the wind picks up. Grayton Beach State Park adds 2,220 acres of beach and nature trails. The catch: it is very expensive, peak season traffic is heavy, HOA rules are common, and year round jobs are limited. See 30A, or read Destin vs 30A.

3. Naples

In January 2023, Naples was among the eight communities in Collier and Lee counties that became the first in the Southeast to earn Blue Zones Community certification. The project reported that regular exercise among residents of those communities was up 20.1 percent (Sharecare announcement). Day to day, that looks like a walk down Fifth Avenue South for lunch and a sunset on calm Gulf beaches that run from the pier north to Vanderbilt Beach. The Naples Pier itself is closed for a rebuild expected to finish around mid 2027. The catch: it is expensive, summers are hot and quiet, public beach parking is limited, and Ian brought storm surge in 2022. See Naples.

4. Sanibel Island

Sanibel has 25 miles of bike paths, no traffic lights and more than half the island preserved. You shell at sunrise, ride to the J.N. Ding Darling National Wildlife Refuge to see thousands of acres of mangroves and wading birds, and ride home for the sunset. The catch: it is expensive, Hurricane Ian hit hard in 2022 and the island has mostly rebuilt, and the causeway is the only road. See Sanibel Island.

5. St. Augustine Beach

A relaxed town on Anastasia Island with a wide beach made for long walks and a farmers market at the pier park when it is on. Anastasia State Park has four miles of protected beach and a tidal lagoon for paddling, and the old city is ten minutes away for dinner. The catch: summer crowds, erosion, hurricane exposure and about an hour to the Jacksonville airport. See St. Augustine Beach.

6. Fort Myers

The River District downtown has brick streets, restaurants and monthly art and music walks, which is why Fort Myers scores a 4 for meeting people. You can tour the Edison and Ford winter estates on the river and walk First Street for dinner. It is also the most affordable town on this list. The catch: the beaches are 30 to 40 minutes away, summers are hot and stormy, and the river floods. See Fort Myers, or compare it in Naples vs Fort Myers.

7. Anna Maria Island

Old Florida on a small island, with no high rises and a free trolley that runs the length of it, so you can ride to breakfast, read on a quiet beach and watch the sun drop into the Gulf without touching a car. The catch: it costs about $1.15M, and island flooding and storms come with the view. See Anna Maria Island.

8. Jupiter

Jupiter earns a 5 for the outdoors: you paddle the Loxahatchee River in the morning and snorkel the clear water of the inlet in the afternoon, under the red Jupiter Inlet Lighthouse. The catch: it is pricey, and it scores only a 2 for walkability, so more of daily life happens in the car. See Jupiter.

9. Bonita Springs

Bonita Springs is one of the eight certified Blue Zones Communities. Riverside Park on the Imperial River hosts concerts and art festivals downtown, and Lovers Key State Park next door has two miles of beach, trails and kayak routes through the mangroves. The catch: much of the city is gated communities where you drive everywhere, US 41 backs up in winter, the beach was hit hard by Ian, and county beach parking lots were closed for repairs in fall 2026. See Bonita Springs.

10. Marco Island

Also a certified Blue Zones Community, Marco is the largest of the Ten Thousand Islands. You shell on Tigertail Beach at low tide, where a lagoon and sandbar draw shorebirds, then take the boat into the mangroves for the afternoon. The catch: it is one of the priciest towns we cover, far from everything else, with storm surge and high insurance. See Marco Island.

Also on the list

Ponte Vedra Beach has miles of trails and undeveloped beach at the Guana Tolomato Matanzas Reserve, and Longboat Key and Captiva Island trade walkability for some of the quietest beaches on the Gulf.

Which Florida towns are certified Blue Zones Communities?

The eight certified in January 2023 are Naples, Immokalee, Ave Maria, Bonita Springs, Estero, Golden Gate, East Naples and Marco Island, all in Collier and Lee counties (Blue Zones). The other towns on this list carry our wellness vibe because of how they score, not because of a certification.

What does it cost to live in one of these towns?

Typical prices on our list run from about $325K in Fort Myers to about $1.38M on Marco Island. Walkable centers and islands cost the most. Insurance and flood coverage add a real yearly bill, which the six bills nobody puts on the listing breaks down.

What about hurricanes and summer heat?

Most of these towns are on the Gulf, and Ian, Helene and Milton all left marks between Sarasota and Marco Island. Check the flood zone and elevation before you buy, and read our guides to hurricane season and home insurance. A walking life also looks different in August, so visit in both seasons if you can.
