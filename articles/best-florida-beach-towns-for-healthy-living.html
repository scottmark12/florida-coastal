The Best Florida Beach Towns for Healthy Living Are the Hardest to Afford

Daily walks, time outside and friendly faces, including the certified Blue Zones communities around Naples. By our numbers, it's the priciest style on the coast.

By Mark and Betsy ScottNov 3, 20266 min read

In Sarasota a normal day can start with a walk along the bayfront at sunrise and end at a show downtown. In between there's The Ringling, 66 acres of it, and a community calendar that never runs dry. You don't have to plan to exercise. It just happens.

That's the idea behind what we call the Healthy Living style: towns where walking, getting outside and running into people you know are the easy choice. It's also, by our numbers, the most expensive way to live on the Florida coast.

The healthiest towns cost the most

We score every town from 1 to 5 on affordability. The 13 towns in this style average 1.8, the lowest of any style we track.

It makes sense. The things that make a town easy to live well in, like trails, parks, a walkable center, good food and a busy calendar, are the same things people pay for. The good news is that most of the habits travel. You can walk every morning in a cheaper town. It just takes more planning.

Of every style we track on the Florida coast, the healthy one is the hardest to afford.

Only three are certified

The Blue Zones Project works with towns to make healthy habits the default. In January 2023, eight communities in Collier and Lee counties became the first in the Southeast to earn its certification: Naples, Immokalee, Ave Maria, Bonita Springs, Estero, Golden Gate, East Naples and Marco Island. The project reported regular exercise among residents up 20.1 percent (Sharecare announcement). Three of them are on our list. The rest carry the style because of how they score.

The ten we'd look at first

None of these towns has Healthy Living as its lead style, so we ranked them on three scores together: outdoors, walkability and meeting people.

Sarasota tops the list with a 5 for meeting people and high marks on the rest. St. Armands Circle is a walkable ring of shops a few blocks from Lido Beach. Traffic in season is heavy, and Helene and Milton brought surge in 2024. Compare it in Sarasota vs St. Petersburg.

30A is home. The Timpoochee Trail links its communities, so a normal morning is a bike ride to coffee in Seaside and a paddle on a coastal dune lake before the wind picks up. It's very expensive, and HOA rules are common. Destin vs 30A compares it with its neighbor.

Naples is certified. Day to day that looks like a walk down Fifth Avenue South for lunch and a sunset on calm Gulf beaches running north to Vanderbilt. The pier is being rebuilt until about mid 2027, and summers are hot and quiet.

Sanibel Island has 25 miles of bike paths, no traffic lights and more than half the island preserved. Ride to the Ding Darling refuge and home for the sunset. One causeway on and off.

St. Augustine Beach has a wide beach for long walks, Anastasia State Park's four miles of protected sand and a tidal lagoon, and the old city ten minutes away for dinner.

Fort Myers is the most affordable town here, at about $325K, and its River District art and music walks earn it a 4 for meeting people. The beaches are 30 to 40 minutes off. Naples vs Fort Myers lays out the trade.

Anna Maria Island lets you ride a free trolley to breakfast and never touch a car, at about $1.15M.

Jupiter gets a 5 for the outdoors: the Loxahatchee River in the morning, the inlet under the red lighthouse in the afternoon. It scores a 2 for walkability, so more of life happens in the car.

Bonita Springs is certified, with concerts at Riverside Park and Lovers Key State Park next door. Much of the city is gated communities where you drive everywhere.

Marco Island is certified too: shelling at Tigertail Beach at low tide, then the boat into the mangroves. It's one of the priciest towns we cover, at about $1.38M.

Also carrying the style: Ponte Vedra Beach, with the trails of the Guana Tolomato Matanzas Reserve, plus Longboat Key and Captiva Island, which trade walkability for some of the quietest beaches on the Gulf.

Before you choose a town

  • Walk it in August. A walking life looks different in the heat. August tells you more than February.
  • Map your daily loop: coffee, groceries, a trail and somewhere to see people. If it all needs a car, the score won't help you.
  • Price the whole year. Insurance and flood coverage add a real bill. The six bills nobody puts on the listing breaks it down.
  • Check the flood zone and elevation. Ian, Helene and Milton all left marks between Sarasota and Marco Island. See our insurance guide and the hurricane season checklist.

The healthiest move is the one you can afford to keep making every morning.
