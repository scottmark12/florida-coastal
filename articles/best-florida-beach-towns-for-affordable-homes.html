The Most Affordable Florida Beach Towns, and What You Actually Give Up

Ten coastal towns ranked on home prices. A lower price on the Florida coast usually means a drive to the sand, not a long way from the airport.

By Mark and Betsy ScottNov 3, 20266 min read

In Daytona Beach you can still drive onto the sand. It's wide and hard packed, you park in marked zones, and the oceanfront Bandshell has concerts. The airport is in town. And the typical home runs about $322K.

That surprises people who've only priced the Gulf islands. Florida's coast still has plenty of towns under $350K. The question is what you give up, and it's narrower than most people think.

You give up the beach and the walk, not the airport

We compared the 46 towns in our Most Affordable Homes style with the rest of the coast. They score lower for beaches, walkability, dining and nightlife. They score about the same for airport access and peace and quiet.

So a lower price usually means a drive to the sand and a car for errands. It doesn't usually mean being cut off. Many of these towns are close to an airport, and they're no louder than the expensive ones.

The other trade doesn't show on the chart: several of these towns are cheaper because they took a recent storm. That's not a dealbreaker, but it should be the first thing you check.

The cheaper Florida coast isn't farther from the airport. It's farther from the sand.

Daytona Beach is where we'd start: a beach you can drive on, an airport in town and anything from an older condo near the ocean to a small house on the mainland. Race weeks, Bike Week and Biketoberfest fill the roads, and condo fees and assessments need a close read.

Holly Hill sits on the mainland bank of the Halifax River at about $247K. Riverside Drive is an easy walk along the water, there's a Sunday market at Sunrise Park, and the ocean is about ten minutes over the bridge. Some of the housing is older.

Titusville has launch pads across the river and Merritt Island National Wildlife Refuge next door. You watch rockets from the riverfront and ride the three long bike trails that meet downtown. The beach at Playalinda is a drive through the refuge and closes for launches.

New Port Richey is a 1920s river town with a walkable downtown, concerts in Sims Park and nine days of Chasco Fiesta each spring, at about $235K. No real beach.

Holiday is the lowest price here, at about $229K, on canals out to the Anclote River and the Gulf. Run out to the 1887 lighthouse on Anclote Key and stop at the Sponge Docks in Tarpon Springs for lunch. Real surge exposure west of US 19.

Port Charlotte has some of the most affordable canal homes in Southwest Florida, out to Charlotte Harbor for tarpon and snook, with Punta Gorda's airport 15 to 20 minutes away. Charley in 2004 and Ian in 2022, and the seawall may be yours to maintain.

Hudson has a white sand swimming beach at SunWest Park and Tampa within an hour. Canal homes flooded badly in Helene in 2024.

Jacksonville is the most affordable big city on the Florida coast, at about $285K. Riverside, Avondale and San Marco sit on the St. Johns under the oaks. The ocean is 25 to 35 minutes east.

Carrabelle is a working harbor on the Forgotten Coast with Carrabelle Beach a mile and a half west and Tate's Hell State Forest next door. Tallahassee's airport is about an hour and 15 minutes on two lane roads.

Steinhatchee is a river village where summer means scallops. Idalia and Helene pushed surge through town, it's still rebuilding, and there's no beach.

Also on the list: South Daytona at about $265K, Fort Pierce at about $280K, Ruskin and Palm Bay in the low $300Ks, Daytona Beach Shores for oceanfront condos and Port St. Joe on St. Joseph Bay.

Before you buy

  • Price the month, not the house. Property taxes reset to full value when you buy, flood insurance is separate, and the hurricane deductible is a percentage of the home's value. The six bills nobody puts on the listing walks through each.
  • Ask how the house came through the last storm, and check its elevation.
  • Read condo reserves. Older buildings now have to fund structural reserves.
  • Get a real insurance quote for the exact address. See our insurance guide and the hurricane season checklist.

Affordable Florida is real. Just budget for the drive to the beach, and for the storm that made it affordable.
