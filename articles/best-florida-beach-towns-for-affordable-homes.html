The Most Affordable Florida Beach Towns, and What You Actually Give Up

Ten coastal towns ranked on home prices. A lower price on the Florida coast usually means a drive to the sand, not a long way from the airport.

By Mark and Betsy ScottNov 3, 20266 min read

Most cheap beach town lists are just the lowest prices. Ours looks for the towns where that price still buys a good life near the water. We score every one of the 167 towns on Florida's coast the same way, from things we can count and check, never reviews or ads.

Thirty four towns carry this style. We ranked them on our affordability score and set each price next to what you get. For the longer story behind each price, read Florida's most affordable beach towns, and the catch with each. Every score below shows where the town ranks out of all 167 (here's how we score).

1. Daytona Beach

Central AtlanticTypical home price about $322KNearest sand In town, and you can drive on it

No. 1 on our listAffordability 5/5 #11 of 167

Daytona Beach is where we'd start: a beach you can drive on, an airport in town and anything from an older condo near the ocean to a small house on the mainland. Race weeks, Bike Week and Biketoberfest fill the roads, and condo fees and assessments need a close read.

Why it ranks
Affordable
Keep in mind
Event crowds, hurricane exposure

2. Holly Hill

Central AtlanticTypical home price about $247KNearest sand About 10 min across the river

No. 2 on our listAffordability 5/5 #11 of 167

Holly Hill sits on the mainland bank of the Halifax River at about $247K. Riverside Drive is an easy walk along the water, there's a Sunday market at Sunrise Park, and the ocean is about ten minutes over the bridge. Some of the housing is older.

Why it ranks
Affordable
Keep in mind
Event crowds, hurricane exposure
See Holly Hill's full scorecard →

3. Titusville

Space CoastTypical home price about $283KNearest sand Playalinda, a drive through the refuge

No. 3 on our listAffordability 5/5 #5 of 167

Titusville has launch pads across the river and Merritt Island National Wildlife Refuge next door. You watch rockets from the riverfront and ride the three long bike trails that meet downtown. The beach at Playalinda is a drive through the refuge and closes for launches.

Why it ranks
Affordable
Launches and wild beaches close by
Keep in mind
Hurricane exposure, lagoon water quality
See Titusville's full scorecard →

4. New Port Richey

Tampa BayTypical home price about $235KNearest sand A drive south to Fred Howard Park

No. 4 on our listAffordability 5/5 #2 of 167

New Port Richey is a 1920s river town with a walkable downtown, concerts in Sims Park and nine days of Chasco Fiesta each spring, at about $235K. No real beach.

Why it ranks
Affordable
A downtown on the rise
Keep in mind
Flooding along the river and coast
See New Port Richey's full scorecard →

5. Holiday

Tampa BayTypical home price about $229KNearest sand Fred Howard Park, about 15 min

No. 5 on our listAffordability 5/5 #1 of 167

Holiday is the lowest price here, at about $229K, on canals out to the Anclote River and the Gulf. Run out to the 1887 lighthouse on Anclote Key and stop at the Sponge Docks in Tarpon Springs for lunch. Real surge exposure west of US 19.

Why it ranks
Very affordable for being this close to the Gulf
Great boating, fishing and island trips right out the river
Keep in mind
Low coastal neighborhoods west of US 19 flood in surges; the Anclote pier had hurricane damage; US 19 traffic and strip malls
See Holiday's full scorecard →

6. Port Charlotte

Southwest GulfTypical home price about $310KNearest sand Englewood Beach, about 40 min

No. 6 on our listAffordability 5/5 #10 of 167

Port Charlotte has some of the most affordable canal homes in Southwest Florida, out to Charlotte Harbor for tarpon and snook, with Punta Gorda's airport 15 to 20 minutes away. Charley in 2004 and Ian in 2022, and the seawall may be yours to maintain.

Why it ranks
Affordable canal homes
Great fishing
Keep in mind
Storm damage history (Charley in 2004, Ian in 2022), flood zones
See Port Charlotte's full scorecard →

7. Hudson

Nature CoastTypical home price about $286KNearest sand SunWest Park, in town

No. 7 on our listAffordability 5/5 #8 of 167

Hudson has a white sand swimming beach at SunWest Park and Tampa within an hour. Canal homes flooded badly in Helene in 2024.

Why it ranks
Affordable waterfront compared with Tampa Bay
Close enough to Tampa for flights and city trips
Keep in mind
Waterfront and canal homes flooded badly in Helene (2024), heavy traffic on US 19
See Hudson's full scorecard →

8. Jacksonville

First CoastTypical home price about $285KNearest sand Ocean beaches, 25 to 35 min

No. 8 on our listAffordability 5/5 #6 of 167

Jacksonville is the most affordable big city on the Florida coast, at about $285K. Riverside, Avondale and San Marco sit on the St. Johns under the oaks. The ocean is 25 to 35 minutes east.

Why it ranks
Very affordable for a big Florida city
Historic river neighborhoods with real character
Keep in mind
River flooding in low areas during big storms, long drives across a huge city, stadium construction downtown through 2028
See Jacksonville's full scorecard →

9. Carrabelle

Forgotten CoastTypical home price about $260KNearest sand Carrabelle Beach, 1.5 miles west

No. 9 on our listAffordability 5/5 #4 of 167

Carrabelle is a working harbor on the Forgotten Coast with Carrabelle Beach a mile and a half west and Tate's Hell State Forest next door. Tallahassee's airport is about an hour and 15 minutes on two lane roads.

Why it ranks
Affordable for the coast
Great fishing and boating
Keep in mind
Storm surge, few services nearby
See Carrabelle's full scorecard →

10. Steinhatchee

Big BendTypical home price about $285KNearest sand None nearby, the flats are the draw

No. 10 on our listAffordability 5/5 #7 of 167

Steinhatchee is a river village where summer means scallops. Idalia and Helene pushed surge through town, it's still rebuilding, and there's no beach.

Why it ranks
Affordable
Outstanding fishing and scalloping
Keep in mind
Hit hard by Idalia in 2023 and Helene in 2024. Check rebuilding and flood zones
See Steinhatchee's full scorecard →

Also on the list: South Daytona at about $265K, Fort Pierce at about $280K, Ruskin and Palm Bay in the low $300Ks, Daytona Beach Shores for oceanfront condos and Port St. Joe on St. Joseph Bay.

Before you buy

  • Price the month, not the house. Property taxes reset to full value when you buy, flood insurance is separate, and the hurricane deductible is a percentage of the home's value. The six bills nobody puts on the listing walks through each.
  • Ask how the house came through the last storm, and check its elevation.
  • Read condo reserves. Older buildings now have to fund structural reserves.
  • Get a real insurance quote for the exact address. See our insurance guide and the hurricane season checklist.

Affordable Florida is real. Just budget for the drive to the beach, and for the storm that made it affordable.
