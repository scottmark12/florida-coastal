The Best Florida Beach Towns for Affordable Homes (2026)

Ten coastal towns ranked on home prices, with the typical price, how far the sand is and what to check before you buy.

Style Guides·By Florida Coastal·Nov 3, 2026·6 min read

The most affordable Florida beach towns in our 2026 ranking are Daytona Beach, Holly Hill and Titusville. All three have typical home prices under about $325K, and each puts the Atlantic within a short drive, with Daytona's drive on beach right in town.

Of the 167 towns we score, 46 carry our Most Affordable Homes vibe. We ranked them on our affordability score, then set each price next to what you get for it: how far the sand is, what there is to do and what to check first. For the longer story of why each low priced town is priced that way, read Florida's most affordable beach towns, and the catch with each.

1. Daytona Beach

About $322K median (fall 2026) buys anything from an older condo near the ocean to a small house on the mainland. You still drive onto the wide, hard packed beach and park in marked zones, the oceanfront Bandshell hosts concerts, and Daytona Beach International sits right in town. The catch: race weeks, Bike Week and Biketoberfest fill the roads, and condo fees and assessments need a close read before you sign. See Daytona Beach.

2. Holly Hill

A small city on the mainland bank of the Halifax River, between Daytona Beach and Ormond Beach, at about $247K median (Redfin, July 2026). Riverside Drive makes an easy walk along the water, the Sunday Riverside Market sets up at Sunrise Park, and the ocean is about ten minutes over the bridge. The catch: no beach of its own, limited dining and an older housing stock in places. See Holly Hill.

3. Titusville

About $283K median (fall 2026), with launch pads across the river and Merritt Island National Wildlife Refuge next door. You watch rockets from the riverfront, ride the three long bike trails that meet downtown and catch a show at the Titusville Playhouse. The catch: the ocean at Playalinda is a drive through the refuge and closes for launches, and the lagoon's water quality is an ongoing concern. See Titusville.

4. New Port Richey

About $235K median (fall 2026) for a 1920s river town with a downtown you can walk. Sims Park has concerts on the Cotee River, the Richey Suncoast Theatre is the main stage, and Chasco Fiesta takes over for nine days each spring. The catch: no real beach, US 19 traffic just west of downtown and flood zones near the river. See New Port Richey.

5. Holiday

The lowest price on this list, about $229K median (RealtyTrac, Sept 2026), on canals that run out to the Anclote River and the Gulf. You launch at Anclote River Park, run out to the sandbars and the 1887 lighthouse on Anclote Key, and stop at the Sponge Docks in Tarpon Springs for lunch. The catch: the US 19 strip, no sand beach on the mainland and real surge exposure west of US 19. See Holiday.

6. Port Charlotte

About $310K median (fall 2026) for some of the most affordable canal homes in Southwest Florida. Canals lead out to Charlotte Harbor for tarpon and snook, Punta Gorda's airport is 15 to 20 minutes away, and spring training fills Charlotte Sports Park. The catch: Charley in 2004 and Ian in 2022, a car town layout and a seawall that may be yours to maintain. See Port Charlotte.

7. Hudson

About $286K typical value (Zillow, Sept 2026), with Tampa within an hour. Boats run out the Hudson channel to the flats, SunWest Park has a white sand swimming beach and trails, and Sam's Beach Bar is the sunset spot. The catch: canal homes flooded badly in Helene in 2024, and US 19 sprawl takes away from the charm. See Hudson.

8. Jacksonville

About $285K typical value (Zillow, Sept 2026), the most affordable big city on the Florida coast. Riverside, Avondale and San Marco sit on the St. Johns under the oaks, the Riverside Arts Market sets up every Saturday, and the Timucuan Preserve is close. The catch: the ocean beaches are 25 to 35 minutes east, and you'll drive almost everywhere. See Jacksonville.

9. Carrabelle

About $260K median (fall 2026) for a working harbor town on the Forgotten Coast. The river opens straight into the Gulf, Carrabelle Beach is a mile and a half west, and Tate's Hell State Forest borders town. The catch: much of town sits low, dining is a short list and Tallahassee's airport is about an hour and 15 minutes on two lane roads. See Carrabelle.

10. Steinhatchee

About $285K median for a river village where summer means scalloping on the grass flats and the Fiddler Crab Festival fills Presidents Day weekend. The catch is plain: Idalia in 2023 and Helene in 2024 pushed surge through town, it's still rebuilding, there's no beach and Gainesville's airport is about an hour and a half away. See Steinhatchee.

Also on the list: South Daytona at about $265K, Fort Pierce at about $280K, Ruskin and Palm Bay in the low $300Ks, Daytona Beach Shores for oceanfront condos and Port St. Joe on St. Joseph Bay.

What does it really cost each month?

The sale price is the start. In Florida, property taxes reset to full value when you buy, flood insurance is a separate policy, the hurricane deductible is a percentage of the home's value, and older condo buildings now have to fund structural reserves. The six bills nobody puts on the listing walks through each one.

Why are these towns cheaper?

Usually one of three reasons: the sand is a drive away, the town took a recent storm, or there's less to do nearby. None of that is a dealbreaker, but each one shows up somewhere, in your commute, your insurance or your weekends.

What about hurricanes and insurance?

Several towns here flooded in 2022, 2023 or 2024. Get a quote on the exact address, ask how the house came through the last storm, and check its elevation. Our insurance guide covers what to ask, and the hurricane guide has the checklist.
