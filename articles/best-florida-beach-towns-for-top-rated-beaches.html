The Best Florida Beaches to Live Near, a Locals' Guide (2026)

Thirty towns ranked by our beach score, with how the people who live there actually use the sand. Siesta Key comes out on top.

By Mark and Betsy ScottNov 3, 20266 min read

Ask a local which beach is best and you'll get an answer about parking, the sunrise and which end of the island is quiet. That's this guide. We score every one of the 167 towns on Florida's coast the same way, from things we can count and check, never reviews or ads.

Thirty towns carry our Best Beaches style, the ones the national lists keep naming (Dr. Beach, Tripadvisor) plus a few we think belong. We ranked them by our beach score, then wrote down how the people who live there actually use the sand. Every score below shows where the town ranks out of all 167 (here's how we score).

1. Siesta Key

Sarasota, Bradenton and VeniceTypical home price about $1.1MThe honor No. 28 in the world, World's 50 Best Beaches 2026

No. 1 on our listBeaches #1 of 167

Siesta Beach is the only US beach on this year's World's 50 Best Beaches list, and locals will tell you the sand is the reason. It's 99 percent quartz, so it stays cool underfoot even in August. The main lot is the problem. On a winter Saturday it fills early and both bridges back up, so people who live here go at sunrise or head south to Turtle Beach, where it's calmer.

The locals' move
A sunrise walk on the cool sand, and the Sunday drum circle on Siesta Beach at sunset.
Keep in mind
Crowded most of the year, and the Village gets loud on weekend nights.

2. 30A

Pensacola and the Emerald CoastTypical home price about $915KThe honor Grayton Beach, No. 1 in America, Dr. Beach 2020

No. 2 on our listBeaches #2 of 167

Twenty six miles of the same sugar white sand, cut into little beach towns with dozens of public accesses, so nobody here has one beach. They have the one at the end of their bike ride. Grayton Beach State Park was Dr. Beach's top beach in America in 2020, and the rare coastal dune lakes behind the sand are what locals show visitors first.

The locals' move
Coffee, then a bike ride down the Timpoochee Trail to the next beach town. Late in the day, paddle a dune lake out to where it spills into the Gulf.
Keep in mind
Both airports are 45 to 50 minutes away, and it's one of the priciest stretches of the coast.

3. Panama City Beach

Pensacola and the Emerald CoastTypical home price about $425K

No. 3 on our listBeaches #3 of 167

Miles of wide, soft white sand with public access all along the beach road and two long piers. Locals skip the strip in summer and go to St. Andrews State Park, where the jetties make a calm, clear swimming hole and a shuttle runs to undeveloped Shell Island.

The locals' move
A walk on the sand before the beach chairs go out, and a late afternoon swim inside the jetties.
Keep in mind
Spring and summer bring heavy traffic and a full event calendar. January is about as calm as it gets.
See Panama City Beach's full scorecard →

4. Key Biscayne

Miami and Fort LauderdaleTypical home price about $1.49M

No. 4 on our listBeaches #4 of 167

Two big beach parks on one small island, Crandon Park and Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park, with Biscayne Bay on the other side. It feels like a village, which is why people who live here treat the beach like a backyard.

The locals' move
A late afternoon swim at Crandon, then the walk home under the palms. Sunset drinks at the Rusty Pelican looking back at the city.
Keep in mind
The Rickenbacker Causeway backs up with beach traffic on weekends, and the median sale is around $1.49M.
See Key Biscayne's full scorecard →

5. Naples

Naples, Marco Island and the EvergladesTypical home price about $685KThe honor Delnor-Wiggins Pass, No. 6 in the US, Dr. Beach 2026

No. 5 on our listBeaches #5 of 167

In Old Naples the beach is at the end of nearly every avenue, so locals walk to it. North of town, Delnor-Wiggins Pass ranked sixth on Dr. Beach's 2026 list, Vanderbilt Beach has its own garage and Clam Pass has a mangrove boardwalk with a free tram. The Naples Pier is closed for a rebuild until around mid 2027.

The locals' move
A sunrise walk stopping for shells every few steps, then a chair down at sunset like half the town.
Keep in mind
January through April is crowded, Fifth Avenue parking fills and US 41 crawls.
See Naples's full scorecard →

6. Clearwater Beach

St. Pete and the Clearwater BeachesTypical home price about $650K to $800KThe honor No. 2 in the US, Tripadvisor 2026

No. 6 on our listBeaches #6 of 167

Tripadvisor put it second in the country this year, and the sand earns it, wide and soft right at Pier 60. Locals love the sunset at the pier, with street musicians and the whole town out. When they want room, they cross the pass to Sand Key Park's 95 acres or take the boat north to Caladesi Island, third on Dr. Beach's 2026 list.

The locals' move
A sunrise walk on the soft sand before the beach fills up, and Pier 60 for the sunset.
Keep in mind
One of the busiest beaches in Florida. The causeway backs up on sunny weekends and all spring.
See Clearwater Beach's full scorecard →

7. Destin

Pensacola and the Emerald CoastTypical home price about $540K

No. 7 on our listBeaches #7 of 167

Sugar white sand and the clear green water the Emerald Coast is named for. Henderson Beach State Park is the local favorite, with 30 foot dunes and no buildings behind them, though day visits there need a reservation since May 2026. On weekends half the town is standing in the water at Crab Island.

The locals' move
A walk on the sand before the day gets busy, and a Saturday on the boat at Crab Island.
Keep in mind
US 98 is the one road through town and it jams from spring break through summer.
See Destin's full scorecard →

8. Anna Maria Island

Sarasota, Bradenton and VeniceTypical home price about $1.3M to $1.65M

No. 8 on our listBeaches #8 of 167

Seven miles of soft white sand and shallow, clear Gulf water, nearly all of it public, with no high rises behind any of it. Visitors find Manatee Public Beach. Locals bike to Bean Point at the north tip, the quiet one. The free trolley runs the length of the island, so you rarely need to park.

The locals' move
Bike to the north end for a quiet morning walk. On Saturday, golf cart to the beach with the cooler and stay all day.
Keep in mind
The priciest address in the area, and very few homes come up.
See Anna Maria Island's full scorecard →

9. Delray Beach

The Palm BeachesTypical home price about $546KThe honor Blue Flag, fourth year in a row

No. 9 on our listBeaches #9 of 167

A lifeguarded city beach at the end of Atlantic Avenue, so dinner, the beach and home are one walk. It earned the international Blue Flag for clean water and safety for the fourth year in a row in 2026, one of only three beaches in the country to have it.

The locals' move
A sunrise walk past the sea grapes and lifeguard stands. In season, the Saturday green market downtown, then the beach.
Keep in mind
Parking on the Avenue is metered. A block or two off Atlantic it's free and calmer.
See Delray Beach's full scorecard →

10. Port St. Joe

The Forgotten CoastTypical home price about $460K

No. 10 on our listBeaches #10 of 167

The town faces the bay, so the Gulf beach is a half hour drive out to Cape San Blas and St. Joseph Peninsula State Park. That's the trade locals happily make: ten miles of white sand and tall dunes with almost nothing built behind them, and a bay at home that's calm enough to paddle every evening.

The locals' move
Paddle the grass flats of St. Joseph Bay and stay out for the sunset. On Saturday, drive out to the state park for a long, empty beach.
Keep in mind
Quiet after dark, and the nearest airport is about an hour away.
See Port St. Joe's full scorecard →

The rest of our Best Beaches list

11 through 30, in order: St. Pete Beach, Treasure Island, St. George Island, Tierra Verde, Dunedin, Bonita Springs, Hollywood, New Smyrna Beach, Lauderdale by the Sea, Longboat Key, Captiva Island, St. Augustine Beach, Sanibel Island, Jupiter, Fort Lauderdale, Hutchinson Island, Pensacola Beach, Navarre Beach, Pompano Beach and Deerfield Beach.

The lists rate visits, not lives

Dr. Beach is Stephen Leatherman, who rates public beaches on 50 criteria. Tripadvisor's awards come from a year of traveler reviews. Neither one asks what it's like on a Tuesday in August with a mortgage, which is why we pair them with our own scores.

Before you buy near a famous beach

  • Price the insurance first. Nearly every town on this list sits on a barrier island. Get wind and flood quotes for the exact address before an offer. Our insurance guide covers what to ask.
  • Count the bridges. One bridge on and off changes errands, evacuations and spring break.
  • Visit in season. Drive the causeway on a sunny Saturday in March.
  • Look one town over. The beach is the same. The parking and price often aren't.
  • Read the hurricane season checklist before you sign.

Pick the beach you want to visit every week. Then live where that's easy.
