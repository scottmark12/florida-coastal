The Best Florida Beaches to Live Near, a Locals' Guide (2026)

Thirty beaches ranked on the sand, the water, the weather and the crowds, measured from satellite photos and state data, with how locals use them. 30A comes out on top.

By Mark and Betsy ScottNov 3, 20266 min read

Ask a local which beach is best and you'll get an answer about parking, the sunrise and which end of the island is quiet. That's this guide. We rated the beaches themselves, not the towns, from things anyone can check, never reviews or ads.

We took the 50 beach towns that the national lists (Dr. Beach, Tripadvisor) and our own scores rate highest, and measured each beach: how white the sand is and how clear the water is, from satellite photos; how many days a year the normal high sits between 60 and 85; how many hotels and towers crowd the sand; and whether there's good snorkeling you can swim to. Red tide knocks a beach down a few spots, and famously soft quartz sand nudges it up. Below are the top 30, with how the people who live near them actually use the sand (here's how we score).

1. 30A

Pensacola and the Emerald CoastTypical home price about $915KThe honor Grayton Beach, No. 1 in America, Dr. Beach 2020

No. 1 on our listWhitest sand #5 of 50Clearest water #23 of 50

Twenty six miles of the same sugar white sand, cut into little beach towns with dozens of public accesses, so nobody here has one beach. They have the one at the end of their bike ride. Grayton Beach State Park was Dr. Beach's top beach in America in 2020, and the rare coastal dune lakes behind the sand are what locals show visitors first.

The locals' move
Coffee, then a bike ride down the Timpoochee Trail to the next beach town. Late in the day, paddle a dune lake out to where it spills into the Gulf.
Keep in mind
Both airports are 45 to 50 minutes away, and it's one of the priciest stretches of the coast.

2. Navarre Beach

Pensacola and the Emerald CoastTypical home price about $765KThe honor No. 2 in the US, Condé Nast Traveler Readers' Choice 2025

No. 2 on our listWhitest sand #8 of 50Clearest water #16 of 50

About twelve miles of sugar white sand on Santa Rosa Island, with low buildings and long stretches of protected dunes. The pier is the longest fishing pier in Florida, 1,545 feet out into the Gulf, and the Navarre Beach Marine Sanctuary keeps a snorkel reef just off the sand, so a lot of beach days here start with a mask.

The locals' move
Walk the pier at sunrise, then snorkel the reef east of the pier on a calm morning.
Keep in mind
One bridge on and off the island, and far fewer restaurants than Destin or Pensacola.

3. Destin

Pensacola and the Emerald CoastTypical home price about $540KThe honor No. 8 in Florida, USA Today 10Best 2026

No. 3 on our listWhitest sand #2 of 50Clearest water #19 of 50

Sugar white sand and the clear green water the Emerald Coast is named for. Henderson Beach State Park is the local favorite, with 30 foot dunes and no buildings behind them, though day visits there need a reservation since May 2026. On weekends half the town is standing in the water at Crab Island.

The locals' move
A walk on the sand before the day gets busy, and a Saturday on the boat at Crab Island.
Keep in mind
US 98 is the one road through town and it jams from spring break through summer.

4. Miramar Beach

Pensacola and the Emerald CoastTypical home price about $735K

No. 4 on our listWhitest sand #1 of 50Clearest water #18 of 50

The west end of South Walton, where 30A's sugar sand meets Destin's conveniences. The beach is wide, the water is that clear Emerald Coast green, and the Dolphin Reef, a snorkel reef about 685 feet out, sits right off the sand. Locals use the regional beach accesses and leave the resort stretches to visitors.

The locals' move
Paddleboard out to the snorkel reef on a flat morning, then dinner at Grand Boulevard or Baytowne Wharf.
Keep in mind
US 98 past the outlets and Sandestin is slow all summer, and some of the beach sits behind private resorts.

5. Pensacola Beach

Pensacola and the Emerald CoastTypical home price about $738KThe honor No. 1 in the US, Condé Nast Traveler Readers' Choice 2025

No. 5 on our listWhitest sand #9 of 50Clearest water #17 of 50

The liveliest stretch of Santa Rosa Island, but Gulf Islands National Seashore runs on both sides, so an empty beach is never far. Escambia County keeps snorkel reefs off Park East and Casino Beach, and the Fort Pickens end of the island has miles of white dunes with nothing built on them.

The locals' move
Drive out to the National Seashore for an empty morning beach, then snorkel the Park East reef.
Keep in mind
One bridge from Gulf Breeze, and summer weekends and the July Blue Angels show back it up.

6. Port St. Joe

The Forgotten CoastTypical home price about $460KThe honor St. Joseph Peninsula, No. 1 in America, Dr. Beach 2002

No. 6 on our listWhitest sand #21 of 50Clearest water #29 of 50

The town faces the bay, so the Gulf beach is a half hour drive out to Cape San Blas and St. Joseph Peninsula State Park. That's the trade locals happily make: ten miles of white sand and tall dunes with almost nothing built behind them, and a bay at home that's calm enough to paddle every evening.

The locals' move
Paddle the grass flats of St. Joseph Bay and stay out for the sunset. On Saturday, drive out to the state park for a long, empty beach.
Keep in mind
Quiet after dark, and the nearest airport is about an hour away.

7. Ponce Inlet

Daytona, New Smyrna and FlaglerTypical home price about $521K

No. 7 on our listWhitest sand #20 of 50Clearest water #20 of 50

A small town at the end of the Daytona peninsula, built around Florida's tallest lighthouse and the inlet. The beach is wide and light colored, it's far quieter than anything up the road in Daytona, and red tide doesn't reach it. Lighthouse Point Park at the jetty is where locals fish, watch dolphins and walk the boardwalk.

The locals' move
An early walk south to the jetty at Lighthouse Point Park, then breakfast on the river side of town.
Keep in mind
The Atlantic here is greener and choppier than the Gulf, and Daytona's big event weeks bring traffic down the peninsula.

8. Bradenton Beach

Sarasota, Bradenton and VeniceTypical home price about $860K

No. 8 on our listWhitest sand #11 of 50Clearest water #25 of 50

The south end of Anna Maria Island, with Coquina Beach's long stretch of white sand and the old Bridge Street pier in the middle of town. The free island trolley stops right here, and the wreck of the Regina sits a couple hundred feet off the beach for snorkelers. Across the bridge is Cortez, one of the last working fishing villages in Florida.

The locals' move
Trolley down to Coquina Beach in the morning, and grouper in Cortez at sunset.
Keep in mind
Red tide reaches this stretch some years, and Coquina's lots fill on winter weekends.

9. New Smyrna Beach

Daytona, New Smyrna and FlaglerTypical home price about $450K

No. 9 on our listWhitest sand #19 of 50Clearest water #22 of 50

A real surf town with a beach street, Flagler Avenue, that walks straight onto the sand. Part of the beach still allows cars, which locals love, and south of town the sand runs into Canaveral National Seashore's Apollo Beach, miles of dunes with nothing built on them.

The locals' move
A surf check at the inlet in the morning, then lunch walking Flagler Avenue barefoot. On weekends, drive down to Apollo Beach for an empty one.
Keep in mind
The water is greener and choppier than the Gulf, and Daytona's big event weeks spill over into town.
See New Smyrna Beach's full scorecard →

10. Siesta Key

Sarasota, Bradenton and VeniceTypical home price about $1.1MThe honor No. 28 in the world, World's 50 Best Beaches 2026

No. 10 on our listWhitest sand #3 of 50Clearest water #21 of 50

Siesta Beach is the only US beach on this year's World's 50 Best Beaches list, and locals will tell you the sand is the reason. It's 99 percent quartz, so it stays cool underfoot even in August. The main lot is the problem. On a winter Saturday it fills early and both bridges back up, so people who live here go at sunrise or head south to Turtle Beach, where it's calmer.

The locals' move
A sunrise walk on the cool sand, and the Sunday drum circle on Siesta Beach at sunset.
Keep in mind
Red tide reaches this stretch some years, the beach is crowded most of the year and the Village gets loud on weekend nights.

11 through 30, by coast

The rest of our Best Beaches list, grouped the way a local would give directions. The number is where each beach lands on our list.

The Emerald and Forgotten Coasts

  • 12. Perdido Key. The quiet west end of the Panhandle. Much of the sand sits inside Gulf Islands National Seashore and Perdido Key State Park, and there's a snorkel reef straight out from Johnson Beach.
  • 16. Panama City Beach. The same quartz sand as the rest of the Emerald Coast, with towers along much of the beach road. Locals head to St. Andrews State Park, where the rock jetties are one of the best snorkel spots in west Florida.
  • 30. St. George Island. Miles of dunes and low houses, with the state park filling the east end. The sand reads a little warmer from space than the Emerald Coast, but you'll often have a whole stretch to yourself.

Tampa Bay and the Pinellas beaches

  • 11. Tierra Verde. The way into Fort De Soto Park, a former No. 1 on Dr. Beach's list, with wide, soft sand, a long fishing pier and almost nothing built behind it.
  • 17. Dunedin. Its beaches are the islands: Honeymoon Island at the end of the causeway, and Caladesi Island, a short ferry ride beyond it, both soft quartz sand with no buildings behind it.
  • 20. St. Pete Beach. Wide, soft sand in front of the old resort hotels. Pass-a-Grille at the south end, with its small historic main street, is where locals go.
  • 21. Treasure Island. Some of the widest sand in Pinellas County, with a laid back beach drag and Sunset Beach at the south end.
  • 22. Clearwater Beach. Tripadvisor's No. 2 beach in the country this year, with soft sugar sand. Towers and crowds keep it lower on our list, so locals cross the pass to Sand Key Park for room.

Sarasota and Bradenton

  • 13. Anna Maria Island. Seven miles of soft white sand with no high rises behind it and a free trolley the length of the island. Locals bike to Bean Point at the north tip.
  • 14. Longboat Key. About ten miles of quiet beach with few public accesses, so even in season it rarely feels busy.
  • 23. Sarasota. Lido Beach, a short walk from St. Armands Circle, with the same bright white sand as Siesta next door.
  • 26. Venice. Florida's shark tooth capital. Snorkelers hunt fossil teeth in the shallows, and the sand is darker for it.

Southwest Florida

  • 15. Marco Island. Some of the whitest sand we measured anywhere in the state, plus the lagoon and sandbar at Tigertail Beach.
  • 24. Fort Myers Beach. Very bright, wide sand and a lively strip by the pier. Lovers Key State Park next door is the quiet escape.
  • 27. Sanibel Island. Florida's shelling island. The beaches are about the shells and the wildlife more than the sand, and red tide and greener water keep it lower on our list.

The Atlantic coast

  • 18. Crescent Beach. A wide, quiet beach south of St. Augustine with low houses instead of towers, a few minutes from Fort Matanzas.
  • 19. Hutchinson Island. Bathtub Reef Beach, where a reef right off the sand makes a calm, shallow pool you can snorkel from the beach.
  • 25. Key Biscayne. Two big beach parks, Crandon and Bill Baggs Cape Florida, ten minutes from downtown Miami, with clear water and a village feel.
  • 28. Juno Beach. A quiet beach with a long pier and the Loggerhead Marinelife Center, close to some of the best shore snorkeling in Palm Beach County.
  • 29. South Beach. The famous one. Light sand and clear water, but the towers and crowds put it near the bottom of our 30.

The lists rate visits, not lives

Dr. Beach is Stephen Leatherman, who rates public beaches on 50 criteria. Tripadvisor's awards come from a year of traveler reviews. Neither one asks what it's like on a Tuesday in August with a mortgage, which is why we pair them with our own scores.

Before you buy near a famous beach

  • Price the insurance first. Nearly every town on this list sits on a barrier island. Get wind and flood quotes for the exact address before an offer. Our insurance guide covers what to ask.
  • Count the bridges. One bridge on and off changes errands, evacuations and spring break.
  • Visit in season. Drive the causeway on a sunny Saturday in March.
  • Look one town over. The beach is the same. The parking and price often aren't.
  • Read the hurricane season checklist before you sign.

Pick the beach you want to visit every week. Then live where that's easy.
