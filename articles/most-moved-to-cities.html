Everyone Is Moving to These 5 Florida Cities. Here's Why You Probably Shouldn't.

The top of the moving lists is mostly inland. If you are coming for the water, here is what the rankings leave out and where to look instead.

Moving Tips·By Florida Coastal·Oct 7, 2026·3 min read

Florida is all over the moving lists again. Look closer at which Florida, though. Most of the cities at the top are nowhere near the water.

If you are moving for a job or a price, the popular picks can be the right call. If you are moving for salt air and a sunset you can walk to, here is what the rankings leave out, and where to look instead.

1. Ocala

U-Haul's Growth Index has named Ocala its top growth city in the country three of the last four years. The PODS moving report has ranked it second three years running. It is horse country, with springs, pasture and room to spread out.

The catch: it sits in the middle of the state. The Gulf is about an hour west and the Atlantic is farther.

Look instead: Crystal River and Homosassa. Same part of Florida and the same slow pace, but the spring fed rivers here run out to the Gulf.

2. Orlando, Kissimmee and Clermont

Kissimmee and Clermont rank fourth and fifth on U-Haul's list, and Orlando is back in the PODS top 20. You get jobs, a huge airport and plenty of new homes.

The catch: the beach is a day trip. Cocoa Beach is about 50 minutes from the Orlando airport when traffic behaves.

Look instead: Titusville or New Smyrna Beach. Titusville sits on the Indian River with rocket launches across the water. New Smyrna Beach has a real downtown and a surf beach, and it is one of only four towns on our site with no weak score.

3. Jacksonville

Tenth on the PODS list two years in a row, and the big practical pick: jobs, an airport, a river through downtown.

The catch: Jacksonville covers more ground than almost any city in the country. A Jacksonville address can put you 30 or 40 minutes from the sand.

Look instead: the beach towns on its east edge. Atlantic Beach and Neptune Beach share a walkable town center at the ocean end of Atlantic Boulevard. Or go north to Fernandina Beach for a Victorian main street and a fort inside a state park.

4. Sarasota and North Port

Sarasota is ninth on the PODS list. North Port, just south, is number two on U-Haul's.

The catch: Sarasota's median sale price is about $520,000 (Redfin, three months ending August 2026), and the roads out to the keys fill up in winter. North Port costs less, but it is inland and the beach is a drive.

Look instead: Venice. It has its own historic downtown on an island, its own beaches, and a fishing pier that fully reopened in September 2025. The Legacy Trail runs north to Sarasota when you want the bigger town.

5. Fort Lauderdale

Seventh on U-Haul's list. But PODS ranks South Florida second among the places people are leaving, and moveBuddha's 2026 report puts Hollywood, right next door, in its top 10 exit cities.

Both are true. People come for the boats and the city. The price of staying is another story.

Look instead: Stuart, up the coast. It has an inlet to the ocean, a small downtown on the river and no weak score on our site. Or cross the state to St. Petersburg, where the median sale price was near $430,000 in summer 2026 (Redfin) and downtown sits on the bay.

What the lists get right

The newest ranking hints that the crowd is catching on. moveBuddha's 2026 top 10, built from moving searches, includes Punta Gorda, St. Augustine and Vero Beach. All three are smaller towns on the water.

A list tells you where everyone else is going. It can't tell you where you belong. Find your mile.
