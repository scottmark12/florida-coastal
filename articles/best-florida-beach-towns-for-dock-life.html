The Best Florida Beach Towns for Dock Life Mostly Aren't Beach Towns

A boat lift out back, a canal to open water and dinner you can pull up to. Florida's best boating towns sit on calm water, and most have ordinary beaches.

By Mark and Betsy ScottNov 3, 20266 min read

Lots of towns are near the water. We wanted the ones where a boat out back is just part of normal life. We score every one of the 167 towns on Florida's coast the same way, from things we can count and check, never reviews or ads.

Twenty eight towns carry what we call the Dock Life style: a home on the water, a boat out back and dinner you can pull up to. We counted every house in the 2026 Florida property rolls that sits on navigable water, meaning bays, rivers, the Intracoastal and the canals that connect to them, not beachfront. Then we ranked towns on the share of homes on the water (40%), how many waterfront homes there are (30%) and boating, from marinas and ramps to our life on the water score (30%) (here's how we score).

1. Marathon

The Florida KeysTypical home price About $715K (fall 2026)Kind of water Ocean and bay canals, Boot Key Harbor

No. 1 on our listHomes on the water 55%Waterfront homes 2,148

Marathon is the Middle Keys town where the boat is the car. Canal homes run off both the ocean and the bay, Boot Key Harbor is one of the busiest anchorages in the Keys, and the reef and the flats are both a short run out. Sombrero Beach is there when you want sand, and there's a hospital and an airport in town.

Why it ranks
Water on both sides, ocean and bay
A real town with marinas, fuel and boatyards
Keep in mind
Hurricane exposure and insurance costs. Miami is about two hours up US 1

2. Punta Gorda

Fort Myers, Sanibel and Charlotte HarborTypical home price About $333K (fall 2026)Kind of water Sailboat canals to the harbor

No. 2 on our listHomes on the water 68%Waterfront homes 5,441

Punta Gorda is the one we'd pick if you want a small town with it. Brick streets, a waterfront Harborwalk, Fishermen's Village for lunch and sailboat canals out through Charlotte Harbor. The airport is about 10 minutes away. Ian brought surge in 2022, downtown flooded again in 2024, and the beaches are about 40 minutes off.

Why it ranks
A walkable, pretty downtown
Excellent boating and fishing
Keep in mind
Storm surge. Hit by Ian in 2022, and downtown flooded again in 2024
See Punta Gorda's full scorecard →

3. Marco Island

Naples, Marco Island and the EvergladesTypical home price About $1.01M (fall 2026)Kind of water Canals to the Gulf and the Ten Thousand Islands

No. 3 on our listHomes on the water 64%Waterfront homes 4,933

About two thirds of Marco's single family homes sit on the water, most on canals that run out to the Gulf. Take the boat south into the Ten Thousand Islands for the afternoon, or shell on Tigertail Beach at low tide. It's polished and quiet, and Naples is about 30 minutes away.

Why it ranks
Most homes are on the water
Gulf beaches and the backcountry both close
Keep in mind
Among the priciest towns on the list, plus storm surge and insurance costs
See Marco Island's full scorecard →

4. Islamorada

The Florida KeysTypical home price About $1.44M (fall 2026)Kind of water Flats, backcountry and the reef

No. 4 on our listHomes on the water 51%Waterfront homes 2,067

Islamorada calls itself the sportfishing capital of the world, and about half its homes sit on the water. You're on the flats at sunrise, out to the reef by midmorning and at a waterfront fish house by sunset. Charter captains and guides are everywhere.

Why it ranks
World class fishing out your back door
Half the homes are on the water
Keep in mind
Typical homes run well over $1 million. Limited services and storm exposure
See Islamorada's full scorecard →

5. Cape Coral

Fort Myers, Sanibel and Charlotte HarborTypical home price About $369K (fall 2026)Kind of water Canals out to the river and Gulf

No. 5 on our listHomes on the water 25%Waterfront homes 23,143

Cape Coral has more than 400 miles of canals, and a boat in the back yard is normal there. You lower it off the lift, idle out to the river and you're fishing the Gulf within the hour, then tie up for dinner and live music at the Cape Harbour marina village. Not every canal reaches the Gulf, Ian hit hard in 2022, the city is spread out, and it scores a 1 for beaches.

Why it ranks
Waterfront homes at lower prices than most of the coast
More waterfront homes than any town in Florida
Keep in mind
Storm surge (Ian in 2022) and insurance costs. Not every canal reaches the Gulf
See Cape Coral's full scorecard →

6. Naples

Naples, Marco Island and the EvergladesTypical home price About $685K (fall 2026)Kind of water Naples Bay canals out Gordon Pass

No. 6 on our listHomes on the water 35%Waterfront homes 1,917

In Naples the dock homes cluster around Naples Bay, in neighborhoods like Port Royal and Aqualane Shores, with a short run out Gordon Pass to the Gulf. You can be at the beach at the end of the street in the morning and tie up for dinner on the bay at night.

Why it ranks
Polished bayfront neighborhoods with Gulf access
A walkable downtown and beaches in town
Keep in mind
Prices are high and Ian brought surge in 2022
See Naples's full scorecard →

7. Merritt Island

The Space CoastTypical home price About $460K (fall 2026)Kind of water Canals off the Banana and Indian Rivers

No. 7 on our listHomes on the water 28%Waterfront homes 3,546

Merritt Island sits between the Banana River and the Indian River, and canal neighborhoods line both sides. Manatees and dolphins are regular visitors, and Port Canaveral is the run out to the ocean. Cocoa Beach is about 15 minutes away.

Why it ranks
Calm river water on both sides of town
Prices below most of the coast
Keep in mind
The lagoon is shallow, so draft matters. Reaching the ocean means going through the Canaveral lock
See Merritt Island's full scorecard →

8. Fort Lauderdale

Miami and Fort LauderdaleTypical home price About $605K (fall 2026)Kind of water Canals and the Intracoastal

No. 8 on our listHomes on the water 16%Waterfront homes 6,533

Fort Lauderdale is the boating capital of the world. The city has thousands of waterfront homes on canals off the New River and the Intracoastal, some of the biggest yacht yards anywhere and an international boat show every fall. Neighborhoods like the Las Olas Isles are built around docks, and you can take the boat to dinner on Las Olas.

Why it ranks
Big city boating with every service you could need
Fast access to the ocean through Port Everglades
Keep in mind
Prices, traffic and flooding in heavy rain. Bridge heights decide which boats fit on some canals
See Fort Lauderdale's full scorecard →

9. Fort Myers Beach

Fort Myers, Sanibel and Charlotte HarborTypical home price About $492K (fall 2026)Kind of water Back bay canals to the Gulf

No. 9 on our listHomes on the water 48%Waterfront homes 983

Nearly half the homes on Fort Myers Beach sit on the water, mostly on back bay canals off Estero Bay and Matanzas Pass. The shrimp boats still head out at dusk, and you're minutes from the Gulf.

Why it ranks
Half the homes are on the water
Gulf beach and back bay boating on one island
Keep in mind
Still rebuilding after Ian in 2022. Storm surge and insurance costs
See Fort Myers Beach's full scorecard →

10. Madeira Beach

St. Pete and the Clearwater BeachesTypical home price About $604K (fall 2026)Kind of water Canals off Boca Ciega Bay

No. 10 on our listHomes on the water 51%Waterfront homes 730

About half of Madeira Beach's homes sit on canals off Boca Ciega Bay, with John's Pass right there for the run out to the Gulf. The fishing fleet and the boardwalk at John's Pass Village are the heart of town.

Why it ranks
Half the homes are on the water
Quick Gulf access through John's Pass
Keep in mind
Helene and Milton flooded the beach towns in 2024. Insurance costs
See Madeira Beach's full scorecard →

Also carrying the style: Hernando Beach, Lighthouse Point, Cocoa Beach, Apollo Beach, North Palm Beach, Longboat Key, Key Largo, Pompano Beach, St. Petersburg, Cortez, Matlacha, Boca Grande, Palm City, Port Charlotte, Homosassa, Tierra Verde, Destin and Boynton Beach.

The town price isn't the dock price

The prices above are town medians that include homes away from the water, so a canal home with a lift usually costs more. On the Nature Coast and in Cape Coral you can still find waterfront for far less than on the southeast Atlantic, where Lighthouse Point runs about $825K. Flood insurance often decides the budget. What nobody tells you about Florida home insurance explains what to ask.

Before you buy a home with a dock

  • Start with the water, not the house. Make sure the canal actually reaches the Gulf or the ocean, and count the bridges between you and open water.
  • Ask how deep it is at low tide.
  • Inspect the seawall, dock and lift like you would a roof, and ask what the last storm did to them.
  • Run the route by boat once before you make an offer.
  • Read Florida's best boating towns and the hurricane season checklist.

Buy the water first. The beach is a boat ride away.
