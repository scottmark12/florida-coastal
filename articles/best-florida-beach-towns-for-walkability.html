The Best Florida Beach Towns for Walkability (2026)

Ten coastal towns where your errands, your dinner and the water fit inside a short walk, ranked for 2026 with typical prices and the honest catch.

Style Guides·By Florida Coastal·Nov 3, 2026·6 min read

The most walkable Florida beach towns are St. Augustine, where the colonial streets of the oldest city in the country put coffee, dinner and the Castillo de San Marcos a few blocks apart, Fernandina Beach, with a Victorian main street that starts at the marina, and Venice, where a palm lined avenue carries you from downtown shops to the Gulf. All three score a 5 out of 5 on our walkability scale.

Of the 167 towns we score, 30 carry our Walkable vibe. We ranked them on our walkability score, which measures how many daily needs sit within a short walk of the center of town, and then by how much there is to walk to. For a tour of the walkable towns coast by coast, see Eight Florida Beach Towns Where You Can Park the Car. This is the ranked 2026 list, with prices and the catch in each.

1. St. Augustine

Have your coffee in the old town before the tour buses arrive, then bike over the bridge to the beach. The historic district packs the Castillo de San Marcos, brick streets and a 5 rated dining scene into a few dozen blocks, which is why it leads this list. The catch: visitors fill downtown much of the year, low lying streets flood in big storms, and the Jacksonville airport is about an hour away. See St. Augustine.

2. Fernandina Beach

Centre Street is a Victorian downtown you can walk end to end for coffee, then stand at the water and watch the shrimp boats come in. Afternoons are for the uncrowded beaches on the ocean side of Amelia Island, near the Georgia line. It scores a 4 for peace and quiet, rare for a town this walkable. The catch: it is pricey and the island sees storm surge. See Fernandina Beach.

3. Venice

Venice was laid out in the 1920s with palm lined avenues, and Venice Avenue still runs from the shops and restaurants downtown right to the beach. You hunt for shark teeth in the morning, walk to lunch and watch the sunset from the jetty. The Legacy Trail, a paved rail trail of about 18 miles, runs north to Sarasota. The catch: it is quiet at night, the island costs more than the mainland, and from mainland neighborhoods you drive to the beach. See Venice.

4. Delray Beach

Atlantic Avenue starts downtown and ends at the ocean, so breakfast, the beach and dinner with live music are all on one street. It stays lively all year and scores a 4 for nightlife. The catch: houses run about $768K, condos are the cheaper way in, and season brings crowds, traffic and higher insurance. See Delray Beach.

5. Dunedin

A laid back downtown where you walk for coffee, take the ferry to Caladesi Island for a beach day and ride the Pinellas Trail home, since it runs right through the middle of town. Tampa and St. Pete Clearwater airports are both close. The catch: winter season gets busy, and homes near the water face storm surge. See Dunedin.

6. Key West

The end of the road is one of a kind: you bike to coffee, spend the afternoon on the water and walk to Mallory Square for the nightly sunset celebration. A car can sit for days. The catch: it is the most expensive town on this list at about $1.05M, lots are small, insurance is high and the evacuation route is long. See Key West, or read The Keys, Mile by Mile.

7. South Beach

Big city energy on the sand. You run the beachwalk at sunrise, work from a cafe and head out to dinner late, all within a few blocks of the art deco architecture along Ocean Drive. The catch: it is expensive, it scores a 1 for peace and quiet, and flooding, traffic and insurance costs come with the address. See South Beach.

8. Lauderdale by the Sea

A low rise beach village where the coral reef sits close enough to shore to snorkel from the sand. You walk to lunch in Anglin's Square by the pier and come back for outdoor music at night. The catch: it is small, with limited homes for sale, and pricey. See Lauderdale by the Sea.

9. West Palm Beach

The real downtown of Palm Beach County. Walk the waterfront to the Saturday GreenMarket, spend the afternoon at the Norton Museum of Art and stay out on Clematis Street. The Brightline station and the airport are minutes away. The catch: the ocean beach is across the bridge in Palm Beach, and prices are rising fast. See West Palm Beach.

10. Gulfport

An artsy little waterfront town with a friendly main street. You wander the galleries and cafes on Beach Boulevard down to the water and catch live music by the water, with St. Petersburg next door. The historic Casino Ballroom is closed for storm repairs. The catch: the streets are low lying and flood prone, and Helene hit hard in 2024. See Gulfport.

Also on the list

Hollywood has a 2.5 mile brick Broadwalk along the ocean, Wilton Manors is built around the restaurants of Wilton Drive, and Neptune Beach shares Beaches Town Center with Atlantic Beach. Bradenton Beach, Clearwater Beach, Palm Beach, St. Petersburg, Apalachicola and Cedar Key carry this vibe too.

What does a walkable beach town cost?

On our list, typical prices run from about $384K in mainland Venice to about $1.05M in Key West. The blocks closest to the center cost more than the town median, because walkable and near the water is about the most wanted combination in Florida. Condos are often the most affordable way into a walkable core, as Delray's prices show. Read the six bills nobody puts on the listing before you set a budget.

Can you really live without a car?

In Key West and South Beach, many days, yes. In the rest, most people keep a car for the big grocery run, the airport and the doctor, and walk or bike for everything else. Before you buy, find the nearest full grocery store on foot and walk the route at 2 in the afternoon in August. You'll live there in August explains why.

What about flooding and hurricanes?

Many walkable downtowns are old, and old towns sit close to the water. St. Augustine, Gulfport and South Beach all list flooding as their main watch out. Check the flood zone and elevation of any home, and read our guides to hurricane season and home insurance.
