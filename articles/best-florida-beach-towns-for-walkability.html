The Most Walkable Florida Beach Towns, and Why You Pay for the Sidewalk

Ten coastal towns where errands, dinner and the water fit inside a short walk. None of the most walkable is easy to afford, and the condo is how most people get in.

By Mark and Betsy ScottNov 3, 20266 min read

In St. Augustine you can have your coffee in the old town before the tour buses arrive, walk past the Castillo de San Marcos on the way home and bike over the bridge to the beach. Coffee, dinner and a fort from the 1600s, all a few blocks apart.

There aren't many places in Florida like that, and the market knows it.

You pay for the sidewalk

We score every town from 1 to 5 on walkability: how many daily needs sit within a short walk of the center of town. Among the towns where you need a car for everything, 53% get a 4 or better for affordability. Among the 8 most walkable towns, 0% do.

Walkable and near the water is about the most wanted combination in the state. Most of Florida was built after the car, so the walkable towns tend to be the old ones, and there are only so many of them.

The way in is usually a condo. In Delray Beach, houses run about $768K and condos about $210K, on the same Atlantic Avenue. If walking is the point, a smaller place in the center beats a bigger house on the edge.

None of Florida's most walkable beach towns is easy to afford. The condo is how most people get in.

The ten we'd look at first

Thirty one towns carry our Walkable style. We ranked them on walkability, then on how much there is to walk to. For a coast by coast tour, see Eight Florida beach towns where you can park the car.

St. Augustine leads because the historic district packs the Castillo, brick streets and a 5 rated dining scene into a few dozen blocks. Visitors fill downtown much of the year, low streets flood in big storms, and Jacksonville's airport is about an hour away.

Fernandina Beach has a Victorian main street on Centre Street that ends at the marina, where you watch the shrimp boats come in. It also scores a 4 for peace and quiet, which is rare for a town this walkable. It's one of five towns on the coast that score high for both quiet and dining.

Venice was laid out in the 1920s, and Venice Avenue still runs from downtown right to the beach. Hunt shark teeth in the morning, walk to lunch, watch the sunset from the jetty. The Legacy Trail runs about 18 miles north to Sarasota. It's quiet at night, and the island costs more than the mainland.

Delray Beach puts breakfast, the beach and dinner with live music on one street, and stays lively all year.

Dunedin has the Pinellas Trail running through the middle of town and the ferry to Caladesi Island for a beach day.

Key West is the one place on the list where a car can sit for days. It's also the most expensive, at about $1.05M. The Keys, Mile by Mile compares it with the rest.

South Beach is big city energy a few blocks from the sand. It scores a 1 for peace and quiet.

Lauderdale by the Sea is a low rise village where the reef is close enough to snorkel from the sand and lunch is in Anglin's Square by the pier. Few homes come up for sale.

West Palm Beach is the real downtown of Palm Beach County: the Saturday GreenMarket, the Norton Museum and Clematis Street, with Brightline and the airport minutes away. The ocean is across the bridge.

Gulfport is an artsy little waterfront town where Beach Boulevard runs down to the water, at about $400K. Its streets are low and flood prone, and Helene hit hard in 2024.

Also carrying the style: Hollywood with its 2.5 mile Broadwalk, Wilton Manors, Neptune Beach, Bradenton Beach, Clearwater Beach, Palm Beach, St. Petersburg, Apalachicola and Cedar Key.

Most people still keep one car

In Key West and South Beach, many days you won't need it. In the rest, most people keep a car for the big grocery run, the airport and the doctor, and walk or bike for everything else. That's still a different life from driving to everything.

Before you choose a town

  • Walk to the grocery store from the exact address, at 2 in the afternoon in August. You'll live there in August.
  • Price the center, not the town. The blocks closest to downtown cost more than the town median.
  • Look at condos if a house in the walkable core is out of reach, and read the fees and reserves. The six bills nobody puts on the listing helps.
  • Check the flood zone. Old towns sit close to the water. See our insurance guide and the hurricane season checklist.

A walkable town gives you back the hours you'd spend in the car. Decide what those hours are worth to you.
