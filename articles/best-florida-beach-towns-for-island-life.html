The Best Florida Beach Towns for Island Life, Where the Bridge Is Part of the House

Barrier islands with quiet sand and not much to do but go to the beach. Florida's islands beat the mainland on beaches and quiet, and lose on everything across the bridge.

By Mark and Betsy ScottNov 3, 20266 min read

The Navarre Beach Fishing Pier runs 1,545 feet into the Gulf and never closes. At sunrise you can walk out and see what the anglers pulled up overnight. Then you swim off empty sand all day.

Then you need groceries, and you drive back over the causeway.

That's island life in Florida, and the people who love it don't mind the drive. We've ridden out enough storms on the Panhandle to add one thing: on an island, the bridge is part of the house.

Better sand, longer errands

We count 15 towns as true Island Life: barrier islands and small keys where the beach is the main event and the mainland is a bridge away. Against the rest of the coast, they score higher for beaches, the outdoors and quiet. They score lower for airport access and affordability, and about the same for dining.

Nothing on that chart is a surprise, and that's the point. An island gives you the beach by subtracting a lot of everything else. The hospital, the big grocery store and the airport are usually on the other side of the water.

On an island, the bridge is part of the house.

We ranked the 15 on peace and quiet and on the quality of the beaches.

Navarre Beach is the quiet stretch of the Emerald Coast between Pensacola Beach and Destin, with open dunes, a sea turtle center and a calm sound side for paddling. The day ends with live music and the sunset at Juana's. Very little nightlife, one causeway, and groceries are back over it.

Perdido Key sits on the Alabama line with protected dunes at both ends. You paddle Big Lagoon in the morning and drive out to Johnson Beach, where the road runs out into undeveloped national seashore. It's mostly condos and very car dependent, and Ivan and Sally both hit hard here.

Hutchinson Island is the Atlantic answer: free beach parking, low rise condos and miles of quiet sand off Stuart and Fort Pierce. Snorkel the reef at Bathtub Beach, tour the House of Refuge, an old lifesaving station for shipwrecked sailors, and watch the turtles nest in summer. Few shops or restaurants, and erosion at Bathtub Beach.

Captiva Island is small and lush, built for shelling at low tide and biking under the banyans. Ian hit hard in 2022, and very little comes up for sale.

St. George Island has nine miles of undeveloped state park, Dr. Beach's number one in 2023, and no high rises. It's also an hour and 40 minutes from Tallahassee's airport, with one bridge, and Michael brought at least 5 feet of surge in 2018.

Pensacola Beach has national seashore on both sides. Bike the island path at sunrise and walk to the pier for sunset. Summers bring bridge traffic, so it's most laid back the rest of the year.

Bradenton Beach and Anna Maria Island share an island with no high rises, a free trolley and a historic pier on Bridge Street. Helene hit hard in 2024, and Cortez Bridge replacement work runs through about 2029.

Palm Beach Shores is eleven blocks at the tip of Singer Island with a 1950s beach town feel. Watch the boats come through the inlet, paddle over to Peanut Island to snorkel, and have dinner on the docks. It's also the one island on this list about 20 minutes from an airport, and prices are climbing as luxury condos replace older buildings.

Also true islands: Cedar Key, Matlacha, Beverly Beach, Fort Myers Beach, Islamorada and Key West. If the Keys are calling, The Keys, Mile by Mile helps you pick one.

More than the mainland, every time

Typical prices in our top nine run from about $537K on Hutchinson Island to about $1.2M on Captiva. Much of what sells on the Panhandle islands is condos, so budget for flood insurance and for assessments after storms. What nobody tells you about Florida home insurance covers what to ask.

Before you buy on an island

  • Learn the way off. Several of these islands have one causeway or one bridge. Drive it at rush hour and know the evacuation route.
  • Check the elevation and flood zone of the exact home, and what the last storm did.
  • Ask about condo reserves and assessments if you're buying a unit.
  • Pick your sunset. Gulf islands face the sunset, Atlantic islands the sunrise. Gulf, Atlantic or Panhandle? lays out the differences.
  • Read the hurricane season checklist.

Island life is simple on purpose. Make sure the simple part is the part you want.
