The Best Florida Beach Towns for Old Florida Charm (2026)

Shrimp boats, fish camps, river towns and no high rises. Our ranked list of the coast towns where Old Florida still sets the pace.

Style Guides·By Florida Coastal·Nov 3, 2026·6 min read

The best Florida beach towns for Old Florida charm in 2026 are Apalachicola, Chokoloskee and Yankeetown. Apalachicola has a brick downtown and shrimp boats in the harbor, Chokoloskee is a shell mound island at the edge of the Ten Thousand Islands, and Yankeetown is a shady river town of live oaks and fish camps, and all three are quiet, on the water and priced well below the famous coast.

By Old Florida we mean fishing villages and river towns where the boat ramp matters more than the mall. Of the 167 towns we score, 39 carry this vibe. We ranked them on peace and quiet, life on the water and affordability.

1. Apalachicola

An oyster town with brick storefronts from its cotton and oyster days, shrimp boats in the harbor and no hurry at all. You walk to breakfast past the boats, browse the old downtown and watch the sun set over the bay, and the quiet beach on St. George Island is just across the bridge. The catch: the Tallahassee airport is about an hour and a half away, big city services are far off, and low areas take storm surge. See Apalachicola.

2. Chokoloskee

A tiny shell mound island at the edge of Everglades National Park, the last stop before the Ten Thousand Islands, a maze of mangrove islands for fishing, paddling and camping. A good day here means poling a skiff through the mangroves for snook and stopping at the old Smallwood Store on the way in. The catch: it is extremely remote, about an hour and a half from the Southwest Florida airport, very low lying, and the mosquitoes are part of the deal. See Chokoloskee.

3. Yankeetown

An old river town on the Withlacoochee a few miles from the Gulf, with live oaks and fish camps. You launch a kayak at Bird Creek Park, paddle the marsh toward the Gulf and climb the observation tower at the 413 acre Withlacoochee Gulf Preserve. Crystal River is close enough for groceries and errands, and Inglis, just up the river, is even quieter. The catch: the whole town sits in the flood zone, Helene pushed a surge of about 12 feet into nearly all of it in 2024, and few owners carry flood insurance, so check the flood history on any home. See Yankeetown.

4. Horseshoe Beach

A tiny fishing town at the end of the road in Dixie County, built around canals, a little Main Street and open Gulf flats. Mornings are for trout and scallops, afternoons are for working on the dock, and Labor Day weekend brings a parade and a night of live music at Sam's Plaza. The catch is serious: Idalia, Debby and Helene all hit within 13 months, the mayor estimated about 175 of 365 homes were lost after Helene, and groceries are about 25 minutes away in Cross City. See Horseshoe Beach.

5. Everglades City

A small fishing town on the Barron River, known for stone crab and as the way into the Ten Thousand Islands. The national park's new Marjory Stoneman Douglas Visitor Center opened in December 2025 with boat tours and a kayak launch, and the Museum of the Everglades is a short walk from the circle. The catch: Wilma, Irma and Ian all flooded town, nearly every property is in a flood zone, and the supermarket and hospital are 35 to 45 minutes away. See Everglades City.

6. St. Marks

A tiny river town where the road ends at the water, half an hour south of Tallahassee and its airport. The paved Tallahassee to St. Marks trail runs sixteen miles of old railroad right into town, San Marcos de Apalache Historic State Park marks a fort begun in 1679, and the St. Marks National Wildlife Refuge next door has more than 86,000 acres and a lighthouse rebuilt in 1842. The catch: storm surge has put water in town five times since 2005, most recently with Helene, and there is no beach in town. See St. Marks.

7. Ozello

A handful of homes on marsh islands at the end of the Ozello Trail, a nine mile road through salt marsh and hammocks that is a destination by itself. You launch a kayak from the back yard, fish the oyster bars and may not see another boat all morning. The catch: there are almost no services, there is one winding road in and out, and the low ground floods in storms. See Ozello.

8. Englewood

A laid back Gulf town with a small old downtown on Dearborn Street, where the farmers market sets up, and beaches on Manasota Key known for shark teeth. Stump Pass Beach State Park has a mile of undeveloped beach at the tip of the key. It is the easiest town on this list for a real beach day, and the airport in Punta Gorda is about 40 minutes off. The catch: Ian hit in 2022 and Helene and Milton in 2024, and shopping and nightlife are limited. See Englewood.

9. Ormond by the Sea

A quiet beach community on the barrier island north of Ormond Beach, with small streets of beach cottages between the ocean and the Halifax River. You surf fish at North Peninsula State Park in the morning, bike the shaded Ormond Scenic Loop past the centuries old Fairchild Oak, and watch the sunset from the Highbridge Park dock. The catch: there are very few restaurants and shops on this stretch of A1A, and A1A has washed out in past storms, though a new buried seawall was finished around early 2026. See Ormond by the Sea.

Also on the list

Cedar Key, Matlacha, Steinhatchee, Cortez, Carrabelle, Port St. Joe, Tarpon Springs, Suwannee, Keaton Beach and Mexico Beach all carry the vibe too. Our earlier guide, Old Florida Is Still Out There, tells the story of a few of them.

What does it cost to live in an Old Florida town?

Less than almost anywhere else on the water. Typical values in our top nine run from about $216K in Horseshoe Beach to the high $300Ks in Apalachicola, and most of these towns score a 4 or 5 on affordability. The bigger number is often insurance. Low ground means flood coverage, elevation rules and sometimes a costly rebuild, so get real quotes before you fall for a porch. What Nobody Tells You About Florida Home Insurance and the six bills nobody puts on the listing walk through it.

What about hurricanes?

Nearly every town on this list has taken storm surge in the last decade, and the Big Bend took Idalia, Debby and Helene in about a year. Ask for the elevation certificate, the flood history and what was repaired after the last storm, and read our hurricane season checklist before you make an offer.

How far is the nearest airport and hospital?

That is the trade for the quiet. St. Marks is about 30 minutes from the Tallahassee airport and Englewood is about 40 minutes from Punta Gorda, but Chokoloskee, Everglades City and Apalachicola are about an hour and a half from theirs, and several towns send you 25 to 45 minutes for groceries.
