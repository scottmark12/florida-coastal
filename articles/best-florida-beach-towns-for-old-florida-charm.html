The Best Florida Beach Towns for Old Florida Charm Are Quiet Because They're Far

Shrimp boats, fish camps and river towns with no high rises. Old Florida is the quietest, most affordable coast left, and the farthest from an airport.

By Mark and Betsy ScottNov 3, 20266 min read

We score all 167 towns on the Florida coast on the same ten point scorecard, built from things we can count and check, not reviews or ads. Twenty four towns carry our Old Florida style. We ranked them on peace and quiet, life on the water and affordability. Every score below shows where the town ranks out of all 167. How we score

1. Apalachicola

Forgotten CoastTypical home price about $342KBest for A brick downtown on the bay

No. 1 on our listPeace and quiet 5/5 #21 of 167On the water 4/5 #67 of 167Affordability 4/5 #38 of 167

Apalachicola is where we'd start. Shrimp boats in the harbor, a walkable old downtown, and the quiet beach on St. George Island just across the bridge. Low areas take storm surge, and big city services are far off.

Why it ranks
Real Old Florida character
Affordable for the coast
Keep in mind
Storm surge and flooding in low areas

2. Cedar Key

Nature CoastTypical home price about $322KBest for Clams, kayaks and Gulf sunsets

No. 2 on our listPeace and quiet 5/5 #10 of 167On the water 4/5 #78 of 167Affordability 4/5 #30 of 167

Cedar Key is a tiny island town of clam farms and kayaks. You paddle the backwater keys in the morning and eat clams on Dock Street at sunset. Recent hurricanes hit it hard, and the nearest full grocery store is about 40 minutes away.

Why it ranks
Very quiet and small
Strong sense of place
Keep in mind
Hit hard by recent hurricanes; check rebuilding and flood zones. The nearest full grocery store is about 40 minutes away

3. Chokoloskee

EvergladesTypical home price about $310KBest for Backcountry fishing at the door

No. 3 on our listPeace and quiet 5/5 #7 of 167On the water 5/5 #76 of 167Affordability 4/5 #26 of 167

Chokoloskee is a shell mound island at the edge of Everglades National Park, the last stop before the Ten Thousand Islands. A good day means poling a skiff through the mangroves for snook and stopping at the old Smallwood Store on the way in. It's extremely remote and very low, and the mosquitoes are part of the deal.

Why it ranks
Wild Florida at your door
Legendary backcountry fishing
Keep in mind
Very low lying and isolated, with storm surge risk
See Chokoloskee's full scorecard →

4. Steinhatchee

Big BendTypical home price about $285KBest for Scallops off the river

No. 4 on our listPeace and quiet 5/5 #15 of 167On the water 5/5 #57 of 167Affordability 5/5 #7 of 167

Steinhatchee is a river fishing village where summer means scallops. You run the river to the grass flats at dawn and clean your catch on the dock. Idalia hit in 2023 and Helene in 2024.

Why it ranks
Affordable
Outstanding fishing and scalloping
Keep in mind
Hit hard by Idalia in 2023 and Helene in 2024. Check rebuilding and flood zones
See Steinhatchee's full scorecard →

5. Yankeetown

Nature CoastTypical home price about $254KBest for River paddling and a big preserve

No. 5 on our listPeace and quiet 5/5 #9 of 167On the water 5/5 #98 of 167Affordability 4/5 #17 of 167

Yankeetown is an old river town of live oaks and fish camps on the Withlacoochee. Launch a kayak at Bird Creek Park and climb the tower at the 413 acre Withlacoochee Gulf Preserve. Crystal River is close for errands, and Inglis up the river is even quieter. Be careful here: the whole town is in the flood zone, Helene pushed about 12 feet of surge into nearly all of it in 2024, and few owners carry flood insurance.

Why it ranks
Quiet, shady river town with real nature at the doorstep
Close enough to Crystal River for groceries and errands
Keep in mind
The whole town sits in the flood zone. Helene (2024) pushed a surge of about 12 feet into nearly all of Yankeetown and many homes flooded. Few owners carry flood insurance, so check flood history on any home
See Yankeetown's full scorecard →

6. Horseshoe Beach

Big BendTypical home price about $216KBest for A real small town at the end of the road

No. 6 on our listPeace and quiet 5/5 #2 of 167On the water 5/5 #95 of 167Affordability 4/5 #16 of 167

Horseshoe Beach is a fishing town at the end of the road in Dixie County, with canals, a little Main Street and a Labor Day parade. It's also had the hardest few years of any town on this list. Idalia, Debby and Helene all hit within 13 months, and the mayor estimated about 175 of 365 homes were lost after Helene.

Why it ranks
Real small town where neighbors know each other
Excellent fishing and scalloping right out front
Keep in mind
Hit by Idalia (2023), Debby and Helene (2024). The mayor estimated about 175 of 365 homes were lost and every business closed after Helene. Rebuilding is slow, insurance is costly and the next storm is a real risk
See Horseshoe Beach's full scorecard →

7. Everglades City

EvergladesTypical home price about $325KBest for Stone crab and the national park

No. 7 on our listPeace and quiet 4/5 #29 of 167On the water 5/5 #92 of 167Affordability 4/5 #33 of 167

Everglades City is stone crab country and the way into the Ten Thousand Islands. The national park's Marjory Stoneman Douglas Visitor Center opened in December 2025 with boat tours and a kayak launch. Nearly every property is in a flood zone, and the hospital is 35 to 45 minutes away.

Why it ranks
Wild Florida and big water at the door
A real small town with a busy calendar in season
Keep in mind
Storm surge. Wilma in 2005, Irma in 2017 and Ian in 2022 all flooded town, and nearly every property is in a flood zone
See Everglades City's full scorecard →

8. St. Marks

Apalachee BayTypical home price about $219KBest for A river town near Tallahassee

No. 8 on our listPeace and quiet 4/5 #28 of 167On the water 4/5 #122 of 167Affordability 4/5 #15 of 167

St. Marks is the exception on airports: about 30 minutes from Tallahassee's. The paved trail from Tallahassee runs sixteen miles of old railroad right into town, and the wildlife refuge next door has more than 86,000 acres and a lighthouse rebuilt in 1842. Storm surge has put water in town five times since 2005.

Why it ranks
Half an hour to Tallahassee and its airport
Rivers, a refuge and a rail trail at the door
Keep in mind
Flooding. Storm surge has put water in town five times since 2005, most recently with Helene in 2024
See St. Marks's full scorecard →

9. Ozello

Nature CoastTypical home price about $231KBest for Kayaking from the back yard

No. 9 on our listPeace and quiet 5/5 #11 of 167On the water 4/5 #126 of 167Affordability 4/5 #14 of 167

Ozello is a handful of homes on marsh islands at the end of a nine mile road through salt marsh. You launch a kayak from the back yard and may not see another boat all morning. There are almost no services and one road in and out.

Why it ranks
True peace and quiet
Great inshore fishing and paddling
Keep in mind
Very low lying and floods in storms. One winding road in and out
See Ozello's full scorecard →

Also carrying the style: Carrabelle, Suwannee, Keaton Beach, Matlacha, Port St. Joe, Cortez, Tarpon Springs and Islamorada. Our earlier story, Old Florida Is Still Out There, tells a few of their stories.

Cheap to buy, not always cheap to own

Typical prices in our top nine run from about $216K in Horseshoe Beach to about $342K in Apalachicola, well below the famous coast. The bigger number is often insurance. Low ground means flood coverage, elevation rules and sometimes a rebuild, and nearly every town here has taken storm surge in the last decade. What nobody tells you about Florida home insurance and the six bills nobody puts on the listing walk through it.

Before you fall for a porch

  • Ask for the elevation certificate and the flood history, and what was repaired after the last storm.
  • Get real insurance quotes before an offer, not after.
  • Drive the errands. Time the trip to groceries, a hospital and the airport you'll actually use.
  • Stay a week off season. How to test drive a town has a plan.
  • Read the hurricane season checklist.

Old Florida is still out there. It just never moved closer to the airport.
