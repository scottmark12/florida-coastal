The Best Florida Beach Towns for Old Florida Charm Are Quiet Because They're Far

Shrimp boats, fish camps and river towns with no high rises. Old Florida is the quietest, most affordable coast left, and the farthest from an airport.

By Mark and Betsy ScottNov 3, 20266 min read

Old Florida is easy to put on a sign and hard to find. These are the towns that still feel like it. We score every one of the 167 towns on Florida's coast the same way, from things we can count and check, never reviews or ads.

Eighteen towns carry our Old Florida style. To make the list, a town needs a real working waterfront (fish houses, commercial boats, old docks), low buildings with no wall of condos and local shops instead of chains, and it can't be a nightlife town. We ranked them on coastal charm, then peace and quiet. Every score below shows where the town ranks out of all 167 (here's how we score).

1. Cedar Key

Nature CoastTypical home price about $322KBest for Clams, kayaks and Gulf sunsets

No. 1 on our listCoastal charm #6 of 167Peace and quiet 5/5 #10 of 167On the water 4/5 #78 of 167Affordability 4/5 #18 of 167

Cedar Key is a tiny island town of clam farms and kayaks. You paddle the backwater keys in the morning and eat clams on Dock Street at sunset. Recent hurricanes hit it hard, and the nearest full grocery store is about 40 minutes away.

Why it ranks
Very quiet and small
Strong sense of place
Keep in mind
Hit hard by recent hurricanes; check rebuilding and flood zones. The nearest full grocery store is about 40 minutes away

2. Apalachicola

Forgotten CoastTypical home price about $342KBest for A brick downtown on the bay

No. 2 on our listCoastal charm #2 of 167Peace and quiet 5/5 #21 of 167On the water 4/5 #67 of 167Affordability 4/5 #30 of 167

Apalachicola is where we'd start. Shrimp boats in the harbor, a walkable old downtown, and the quiet beach on St. George Island just across the bridge. Low areas take storm surge, and big city services are far off.

Why it ranks
Real Old Florida character
Affordable for the coast
Keep in mind
Storm surge and flooding in low areas

3. Everglades City

EvergladesTypical home price about $325KBest for Stone crab and the national park

No. 3 on our listCoastal charm #40 of 167Peace and quiet 4/5 #29 of 167On the water 5/5 #92 of 167Affordability 4/5 #91 of 167

Everglades City is stone crab country and the way into the Ten Thousand Islands. The national park's Marjory Stoneman Douglas Visitor Center opened in December 2025 with boat tours and a kayak launch. Nearly every property is in a flood zone, and the hospital is 35 to 45 minutes away.

Why it ranks
Wild Florida and big water at the door
A real small town with a busy calendar in season
Keep in mind
Storm surge. Wilma in 2005, Irma in 2017 and Ian in 2022 all flooded town, and nearly every property is in a flood zone

4. Cortez

Sarasota and BradentonTypical home price about $490KBest for Fish houses that date to the 1880s

No. 4 on our listCoastal charm #41 of 167Peace and quiet 4/5 #46 of 167On the water 5/5 #2 of 167Affordability 3/5 #113 of 167

Cortez is one of the last working fishing villages on the Gulf, a few blocks of wood frame houses and fish houses on Sarasota Bay that date back to the 1880s. You eat lunch on the water while the boats unload, and the Anna Maria Island beaches are just over the bridge. It's tiny, so homes rarely come up for sale.

Why it ranks
A real working waterfront
Minutes from the island beaches
Keep in mind
Low lying, with storm surge risk. The Cortez Bridge is being replaced, with work running to about 2029

5. Fernandina Beach

Amelia IslandTypical home price about $611KBest for Shrimp boats and a Victorian main street

No. 5 on our listCoastal charm #3 of 167Peace and quiet 4/5 #63 of 167On the water 4/5 #74 of 167Affordability 2/5 #99 of 167

Fernandina is the most polished town on this list, and it still earns its place. Shrimp boats tie up at the foot of Centre Street, the Victorian downtown runs up from the marina, and the beaches on the north end of Amelia Island stay uncrowded.

Why it ranks
A working waterfront downtown
One of the most charming towns on the coast
Keep in mind
Storm surge on the island, and prices well above the rest of this list

6. Tarpon Springs

Tampa BayTypical home price about $390KBest for Sponge boats and Greek bakeries

No. 6 on our listCoastal charm #13 of 167Peace and quiet 3/5 #113 of 167On the water 4/5 #64 of 167Affordability 3/5 #63 of 167

Tarpon Springs is a working sponge port. Greek bakeries and restaurants line the Sponge Docks, the boats still unload along Dodecanese Boulevard, and bayous wind through the old neighborhoods. It's the busiest town on this list, with Tampa about 40 minutes away.

Why it ranks
One of a kind character
Walkable docks and downtown
Keep in mind
Tourist traffic at the docks in winter, and flooding near the bayous. Helene's surge flooded the Sponge Docks in 2024

7. Chokoloskee

EvergladesTypical home price about $310KBest for Backcountry fishing at the door

No. 7 on our listCoastal charm #43 of 167Peace and quiet 5/5 #7 of 167On the water 5/5 #76 of 167Affordability 4/5 #108 of 167

Chokoloskee is a shell mound island at the edge of Everglades National Park, the last stop before the Ten Thousand Islands. A good day means poling a skiff through the mangroves for snook and stopping at the old Smallwood Store on the way in. It's extremely remote and very low, and the mosquitoes are part of the deal.

Why it ranks
Wild Florida at your door
Legendary backcountry fishing
Keep in mind
Very low lying and isolated, with storm surge risk

8. Matlacha

Pine IslandTypical home price about $495KBest for Bait shops, galleries and a fishing bridge

No. 8 on our listCoastal charm #44 of 167Peace and quiet 4/5 #30 of 167On the water 5/5 #73 of 167Affordability 4/5 #71 of 167

Matlacha is a few blocks of brightly painted galleries, bait shops and fish houses on the road to Pine Island. People fish off the bridge in the morning and paddle the mangroves of Matlacha Pass in the afternoon.

Why it ranks
Quirky and laid back
Mangroves and fishing at the door
Keep in mind
Storm damage history; check flood zones and rebuilding

9. Horseshoe Beach

Big BendTypical home price about $216KBest for A real small town at the end of the road

No. 9 on our listCoastal charm #47 of 167Peace and quiet 5/5 #2 of 167On the water 5/5 #95 of 167Affordability 4/5 #36 of 167

Horseshoe Beach is a fishing town at the end of the road in Dixie County, with canals, a little Main Street and a Labor Day parade. It's also had the hardest few years of any town on this list. Idalia, Debby and Helene all hit within 13 months, and the mayor estimated about 175 of 365 homes were lost after Helene.

Why it ranks
Real small town where neighbors know each other
Excellent fishing and scalloping right out front
Keep in mind
Hit by Idalia (2023), Debby and Helene (2024). The mayor estimated about 175 of 365 homes were lost and every business closed after Helene. Rebuilding is slow, insurance is costly and the next storm is a real risk
See Horseshoe Beach's full scorecard →

Also carrying the style, in order: Suwannee, Steinhatchee, Homosassa, Carrabelle, Ponce Inlet, Islamorada, Sebastian, Keaton Beach and St. Marks. Our earlier story, Old Florida Is Still Out There, tells a few of their stories.

Cheap to buy, not always cheap to own

Typical prices in our top nine run from about $216K in Horseshoe Beach to about $611K in Fernandina Beach, and most sit well below the famous coast. The bigger number is often insurance. Low ground means flood coverage, elevation rules and sometimes a rebuild, and nearly every town here has taken storm surge in the last decade. What nobody tells you about Florida home insurance and the six bills nobody puts on the listing walk through it.

Before you fall for a porch

  • Ask for the elevation certificate and the flood history, and what was repaired after the last storm.
  • Get real insurance quotes before an offer, not after.
  • Drive the errands. Time the trip to groceries, a hospital and the airport you'll actually use.
  • Stay a week off season. How to test drive a town has a plan.
  • Read the hurricane season checklist.

Old Florida is still out there. It just never moved closer to the airport.
