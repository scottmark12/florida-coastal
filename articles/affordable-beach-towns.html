Costs and risks

Florida's Most Affordable Beach Towns, and the Catch With Each

Ten towns score a 5 on affordability. Every one of them is priced that way for a reason, so here is the reason.

By Florida CoastalOct 5, 20263 min read

You can still live near the water in Florida without a seven figure budget. Ten of the towns we cover score a 5 on affordability. None of them is a secret bargain, though. Each one costs less for a reason, and the reason is the first thing to understand.

Waterfront for less

Port Charlotte

About as affordable as waterfront gets in Southwest Florida. Canal homes here cost a fraction of similar ones in Sarasota or Naples. The catch: storms hit hard in 2004 and 2022, so check flood zones and insurance house by house. See Port Charlotte.

Carrabelle

A small fishing town on the Forgotten Coast with older cottages, river lots and manufactured homes in the mix. The catch: much of town sits low, and there are few services nearby. See Carrabelle.

Steinhatchee

A river fishing village on the Big Bend with some of the lowest prices on the Florida Gulf. The catch: Idalia in 2023 and Helene in 2024 pushed surge through town, and it is still rebuilding. Get insurance quotes and elevation rules first. See Steinhatchee.

Near Tampa Bay

New Port Richey

A small river town with a 1920s downtown and plenty of small older homes. The catch: flood zones near the river and the coast, and no real beach in town. See New Port Richey.

Ruskin

About as low as prices get near Tampa Bay, with a lot of new construction to choose from. The catch: homes close to the river and the bay sit in flood zones, and fast growth has brought traffic. See Ruskin.

Near the Atlantic

Titusville

The town across the river from the launch pads. Homes near downtown and the river cost far less than the beach towns to the south. The catch: the ocean beach is a drive out through the refuge, and riverfront property still needs flood insurance. See Titusville.

Palm Bay

Lots of land and newer houses keep prices well under the beach towns across the lagoon, and a mainland address usually helps on insurance. The catch: it's spread out, and the sand is 20 to 30 minutes away. See Palm Bay.

Daytona Beach

Some of the lowest home prices on the Florida coast, from older condos near the ocean to small houses on the mainland. The catch: look closely at condo fees and assessments, and expect event crowds. See Daytona Beach.

Daytona Beach Shores

Oceanfront condos here cost far less than in most Florida beach towns. The catch: carrying costs. Ask about monthly fees, special assessments, reserves and insurance for any building before you commit. See Daytona Beach Shores.

South Daytona

A small riverfront city with the beach about ten minutes away, well under what the beach side costs. The catch: homes near the river can flood, and the beach is always a drive. See South Daytona.

The real price is the monthly number

A low sale price can hide a high cost of living in the house. Before you compare two towns, add these up for each address:

  • Homeowners and flood insurance. Get real quotes for the exact address. Our insurance guide covers what to ask.
  • HOA reserves and special assessments. Ask how healthy the reserves are and if any special assessments are planned. Good reserves help, but a big repair can still mean one.
  • The age of the roof. An older roof can raise your premium or make a policy hard to get.
  • Storm history. Ask what the last big storm did to the house and what was repaired. Our hurricane guide has the full checklist.
