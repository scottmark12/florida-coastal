About Highland Beach

Highland Beach is a thin strip of land between the ocean and the Intracoastal, with one road, A1A, running down the middle. Most people live in oceanfront condo buildings, plus a few streets of homes on the water. There are no shops or restaurants to speak of, so evenings out mean a short drive to Delray Beach or Boca Raton. The people who choose it like it that way: it is calm, safe feeling and mostly full time owners and long time snowbirds.

The trade offs are real. Every home here faces storm exposure, and many buildings have raised condo fees and called special assessments for inspections and structural work. Monthly fees of $1,200 to $4,000 and up are common. Prices start high, and if you want restaurants, a grocery store or a doctor, you will be driving off the island for all of it.

Where it shines

Peace and quietTop 16%

About as quiet as the southeast coast gets. One road, A1A, with no stores, no public beach lots and almost no reason for anyone passing through to stop. Mostly you hear the surf.

Where it falls short

Meet peopleBottom 25%

Thin inside the town limits. The town library on A1A is the main gathering spot, and the resort restaurant has a weekday happy hour. Most clubs, markets and classes you would join are a short drive away in Delray Beach or Boca Raton.

NightlifeBottom 20%

None to speak of. The town is condos and homes along A1A with one resort, and its restaurant stops serving dinner around 9:30. Atlantic Avenue in Delray Beach and downtown Boca Raton are each a 10 to 15 minute drive.

Active outdoorsBottom 12%

Beach walks, ocean swims and bike rides along A1A are the daily routine. There are no parks in town to speak of. Gumbo Limbo Nature Center, with its boardwalk and 40 foot tower, is a few minutes south in Boca Raton.

Dining and artsBottom 11%

One restaurant in town. Latitudes at the Delray Sands Resort has an ocean terrace and a seafood bar. Everything else is a drive, with Atlantic Avenue to the north and Mizner Park to the south.

On the waterBottom 9%

The west side of town backs onto the Intracoastal, and many buildings and homes there have their own docks. There is no public marina or ramp in town. Trailer boaters launch at the ramps in Boca Raton or Delray Beach.

Coastal charmBottom 8%

Mostly new build, chains and towers. Not much old town feel.

WalkabilityBottom 8%

There is nothing to walk to. No shops, no cafes, one resort restaurant. You can walk or ride along A1A for exercise, but groceries, coffee and dinner all mean driving to Delray Beach or Boca Raton.

AffordabilityBottom 8%

Among the most expensive addresses on this stretch of coast. Oceanfront condos and large homes dominate, and condo fees, reserves and wind insurance add a lot to the monthly cost. Older condos on the Intracoastal side cost less.

Common questions

Is Highland Beach walkable?

No. Plan on driving for almost everything. There is nothing to walk to. No shops, no cafes, one resort restaurant. You can walk or ride along A1A for exercise, but groceries, coffee and dinner all mean driving to Delray Beach or Boca Raton.

Does Highland Beach have a good beach?

Yes, a very good one. About three miles of clean, uncrowded sand. The catch is access. There are no public beach parks or parking lots in town yet, so you reach the sand through your own building or a private path.

Is Highland Beach affordable?

No. It is one of the most expensive spots on the Florida coast. Home prices: About $1.0M median sale over the last 12 months (Horizon Palm Realty, Nov 2026). What is the nightlife like in Highland Beach?

For anything more you head north to Atlantic Avenue in Delray Beach or south to Mizner Park in Boca Raton, then come home to dark, quiet streets.

How busy does Highland Beach get?

It is one of the quietest places on the Florida coast. About as quiet as the southeast coast gets. One road, A1A, with no stores, no public beach lots and almost no reason for anyone passing through to stop. Mostly you hear the surf.

Is Highland Beach good for boating and fishing?

Yes, with decent access to ramps and marinas. The west side of town backs onto the Intracoastal, and many buildings and homes there have their own docks. There is no public marina or ramp in town. Trailer boaters launch at the ramps in Boca Raton or Delray Beach.

About 30 to 35 minutes to the Palm Beach airport and around 40 to Fort Lauderdale (FLL). Brightline trains stop in downtown Boca Raton, about 15 minutes south.
