About Palm Beach Gardens

Palm Beach Gardens is a green, planned city a few miles inland from Juno and Jupiter. Life centers on golf communities like PGA National and BallenIsles, wide boulevards lined with trees, and plenty of shopping and dining along PGA Boulevard. You'll find families, retirees and people who moved down for work.

The trade offs: the beach is a drive, not a walk. Most homes sit in gated communities with HOA rules, and some add club memberships with big initiation fees. PGA Boulevard and Northlake get busy in season, and prices are well above the county median.

Where it shines

Airport accessTop 14%

About 20 minutes down I-95 to the Palm Beach airport, with ramps onto the interstate and the Turnpike at PGA Boulevard. Fort Lauderdale (FLL) is a little over an hour south.

Where it falls short

Peace and quietBottom 24%

Neighborhoods are quiet, mostly set behind gates and landscaping. The traffic is on PGA Boulevard, Military Trail and around The Gardens Mall, where it backs up at rush hour and through the winter.

BeachesBottom 24%

No beach in the city itself. Juno Beach and John D. MacArthur Beach State Park are roughly 10 to 15 minutes east, so the ocean is an easy trip but not a walk.

AffordabilityBottom 20%

Prices run well above the Florida median, and many neighborhoods add HOA or club fees on top. Condos and townhomes are the lower cost way in. Being inland can help on flood insurance compared with the barrier islands.

Active outdoorsBottom 16%

Green but manicured. Golf courses and city parks cover a lot of ground, and county natural areas with marked hiking trails sit on the edges of town. The Sunday GreenMarket is the weekly outdoor habit. For paddling or beach walks you head east.

Coastal charmBottom 13%

Mostly new build, chains and towers. Not much old town feel.

On the waterBottom 2%

Most of the city sits inland, so water here mostly means lakes on the golf courses. The Intracoastal touches the eastern edge near PGA Boulevard, where a few marinas and waterfront restaurants sit. Public boat ramps are a short drive away.

About average for 4 more: nightlife, meet people, walkability and dining and arts
NightlifeTop 34%

Nights are mostly dinner and a drink. The exception is Downtown Palm Beach Gardens, where Voodoo Bayou has bands until midnight on Fridays and Saturdays and Avocado Cantina has live music on Fridays. After that it is a drive to Jupiter or West Palm.

Meet peopleTop 36%

Golf is the easy way in, from the public Sandhill Crane course to PGA National. The Gardens GreenMarket runs Sundays at City Hall, Life Time hosts a run club at Downtown Palm Beach Gardens on Thursday evenings, and the Cognizant Classic comes every year.

WalkabilityTop 50%

This is a driving city. You can park once and walk around Downtown Palm Beach Gardens, Legacy Place or PGA Commons, but getting between them, or to a grocery store, means the car. The roads are wide and the distances are long.

Dining and artsBottom 42%

A deep restaurant bench for a city with no main street. Downtown Palm Beach Gardens, PGA Commons and Legacy Place hold most of it, with names like Stage Kitchen & Bar, Honeybelle and Seasons 52. Theaters and museums are a drive north or south.

Common questions

Is Palm Beach Gardens walkable?

Not really. You will drive for most things. This is a driving city. You can park once and walk around Downtown Palm Beach Gardens, Legacy Place or PGA Commons, but getting between them, or to a grocery store, means the car. The roads are wide and the distances are long.

Does Palm Beach Gardens have a good beach?

It has a modest one. Better beaches are a drive away. No beach in the city itself. Juno Beach and John D. MacArthur Beach State Park are roughly 10 to 15 minutes east, so the ocean is an easy trip but not a walk.

It is busy in season and calm the rest of the year. Neighborhoods are quiet, mostly set behind gates and landscaping. The traffic is on PGA Boulevard, Military Trail and around The Gardens Mall, where it backs up at rush hour and through the winter.
