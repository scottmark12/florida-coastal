About Jacksonville

Jacksonville is huge and spread out, but most people live it one neighborhood at a time. In Riverside, Avondale and San Marco you get old homes under live oaks, walkable little main streets and the St. Johns River at the end of the block. People walk dogs along the river, go to Saturday markets, fish, boat, and head to the ocean on weekends. There are young families, retirees and lots of Navy and hospital workers, and prices are friendlier than most of coastal Florida.

The trade offs: low areas near the river flood in big storms, and some homes in San Marco and Avondale carry real flood insurance costs. It's a long drive from one side of the city to the other, and traffic adds up. Stadium construction downtown runs through 2028. Summers are hot and humid, and game days get busy.

Dining and artsTop 9%

Good and getting better, with Southern and seafood staples, Mayport shrimp, local breweries and a growing chef scene in Riverside, San Marco and Downtown. It is not as deep as Miami or Tampa.

NightlifeTop 12%

Solid but spread out. Five Points and Riverside have bars and music, San Marco has a cozy square, and Downtown fills up for Jaguars and Jumbo Shrimp games. It is more neighborhood bar than big club.

Common questions

Is Jacksonville walkable?

Not really. You will drive for most things. Riverside, Avondale and San Marco are walkable in pockets, with shops and restaurants close to the houses. The rest of the city is car country, and the Emerald Trail, about 4.6 miles open of a planned 30, is slowly linking the core.

Does Jacksonville have a good beach?

It has a modest one. Better beaches are a drive away. No beach in these neighborhoods. Jacksonville Beach, Neptune Beach and Atlantic Beach are about 25 to 35 minutes east, so the ocean is a weekend trip rather than a walk.

What is the nightlife like in Jacksonville?

Good bars and some live music, though it is only busy now and then. Dinner on a patio in Five Points or around San Marco Square, then a brewery or a show. On game nights the whole downtown feels busy, but most evenings are easygoing and neighborhood scale.

How busy does Jacksonville get?

It is busy in season and calm the rest of the year. Most neighborhoods are calm and leafy, especially Ortega and Mandarin. Downtown and the stadium area get loud on game days, and the interstates carry real traffic at rush hour.

Is Jacksonville good for boating and fishing?

Yes. This is a strong boating town. The St. Johns River runs right through town, so docks, boat ramps and river views are part of daily life in Ortega, San Marco and Mandarin. The Intracoastal and the ocean are close for offshore fishing.

Which airport is closest to Jacksonville?
