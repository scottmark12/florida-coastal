About Fernandina Beach

Fernandina Beach sits on Amelia Island near the Georgia line. Centre Street is lined with Victorian buildings, shops and restaurants that run down to the marina and the shrimp boats, and the beaches are wide and uncrowded. It's a small town where people still stop to talk.

The south end of the island, with its resorts and golf, costs much more. Storm surge is a risk on the island, and Jacksonville's airport is about 40 minutes away.

Where it shines

Coastal charmTop 2%

Full of charm: a historic downtown, local everything, no towers, and streets you want to walk.

WalkabilityTop 4%

Centre Street runs from the marina on the Amelia River straight through the Victorian downtown, with the Saturday market, galleries and the community theatre all a few blocks off it. The ocean is about two miles east, an easy bike ride.

Meet peopleTop 5%

A handful of reliable ways in. The farmers market runs every Saturday on North 7th Street, the Island Art Association is a volunteer run gallery with classes and workshops, and Amelia Community Theatre fills two stages with help from volunteers.

Active outdoorsTop 7%

Fort Clinch State Park covers 1,400 acres on the north end, with trails under live oaks, a campground and beach. In town, Egans Creek has 300 acres of marsh with walking trails, and the Saturday market fills North 7th Street.

Dining and artsTop 12%

Centre Street and the blocks around it are lined with restaurants, with an artist run gallery and a community theatre close by. The calendar is full too, with chamber music from December to June, a jazz festival in October and the Shrimp Festival in May.

Where it falls short

AffordabilityBottom 29%

On the high side. Island homes run well above the Florida median, with the biggest premiums in the historic district and near the ocean. Prices ease once you cross the bridge to the mainland, and storm surge makes flood coverage a must.

Long, uncrowded Atlantic beaches with public parks spaced along the shore, including Main Beach Park, Seaside Park, Peters Point and North Beach Park. Fort Clinch State Park adds a wilder stretch at the north tip of the island.

Peace and quietTop 38%

Peaceful most of the year, with slow traffic and beaches that rarely feel full. The exceptions are festival weekends, when the Shrimp Festival in May and Dickens on Centre in December pack Centre Street.

On the waterTop 43%

The city marina sits at the foot of Centre Street on the Amelia River, where the shrimp boats tie up. More than 30 fishing charters, kayak outfitters and sailing trips work from the island, and the inlet to the ocean is a short run north.

NightlifeBottom 38%

Evenings are mellow. The Palace Saloon on Centre Street is the old standby, a few places nearby have live music, and Sounds on Centre puts a free concert downtown on the first Friday of the month from April to October. Most of town is quiet by 10.
