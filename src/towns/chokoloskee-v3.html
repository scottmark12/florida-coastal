About Chokoloskee

Chokoloskee is a little shell mound island tied to the mainland by a causeway, just south of Everglades City. Life here runs on boats, tides and fishing. Most people are anglers, guides, longtime families and winter folks who come back every year for the quiet and the water. You can be out in the mangroves of Everglades National Park a few minutes after you leave the house.

The trade offs are real. The island is very low lying and isolated, and storm surge is the big risk, so flood zones and insurance come first. Groceries, doctors and the hospital are a long drive away, and the airport is about an hour and a half. There are very few homes for sale at any time, and prices swing because so few sell.

Where it shines

Peace and quietTop 5%

About as quiet as the Florida coast gets. One causeway in, no through traffic, and nights with little more than wind and water. Winter weekends bring anglers and campers, but never real crowds.

AffordabilityTop 17%

Far cheaper than Naples or Marco Island. Small houses, stilt homes and RV lots make up most of what sells. The catch is the elevation, so flood insurance and storm prep are a big part of the math.

Where it falls short

Airport accessBottom 6%

A long haul. Southwest Florida International (RSW) is about an hour and a half north, and Miami is a little farther east on the Tamiami Trail. Everglades City has a small airstrip for private planes.

NightlifeBottom 5%

There isn't any. The island goes dark soon after the boats are back at the dock, and the nearest bar stools are a few miles up the causeway in Everglades City, where things also close early.

Meet peopleBottom 4%

No clubs or leagues out here. You meet people at the boat ramp, on the dock or at the old Smallwood Store. The nearest organized events are across the causeway in Everglades City, like the seafood festival each February.

Dining and artsBottom 4%

Very little. A casual spot or two on the island and a few seafood houses in Everglades City, which calls itself the stone crab capital. No galleries or theaters, just the Smallwood Store and its trading post history.

WalkabilityBottom 3%

You can walk the whole island in under an hour, but there is little to walk to beyond the docks and the old Smallwood Store. A full grocery run, a pharmacy and most errands mean a long drive toward Naples.

BeachesBottom 3%

No real beach. The island is an old shell mound ringed by mangroves and seawalls. For sand you run a boat to the outer keys of the Ten Thousand Islands or drive about an hour to Marco Island.
