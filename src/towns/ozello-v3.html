About Ozello

Ozello is a handful of homes scattered across small islands in the salt marsh between Crystal River and Homosassa. You get there on one narrow road that twists about nine miles through the marsh and over little bridges. People live here for the quiet, the birds and the water. Most folks have a boat or a kayak, and the days are about fishing the flats, crabbing and watching the tide come and go. It feels far from everything, and that is the point.

It is also about as exposed as Florida living gets. The islands sit very low and flood in storms, and Helene pushed water into much of the area in 2024. That one road is the only way in or out. Groceries, doctors and hospitals are back on US 19, and Tampa's airport is about 1.5 hours away. Homes are few, so good ones don't come up often, and insurance can be hard.

Where it falls short

On the waterBottom 26%

You're surrounded by it. Ozello Community Park has a public ramp, and kayaks slip into the creeks of St. Martins Marsh Aquatic Preserve. It's shallow and rocky, so bring a skiff or a paddle, not a deep hull.

NightlifeBottom 8%

None to speak of. Two waterfront seafood places serve dinner and that's the evening. After sunset it's stars, frogs and a very dark nine mile road back to US 19.

BeachesBottom 5%

No beach. The shoreline is salt marsh, mangrove islands and oyster bars. For sand you drive back out and around to the small county beach west of Crystal River, the better part of an hour.

Airport accessBottom 5%

Far. Tampa (TPA) is an hour and a half to an hour and 45 minutes once you count the nine mile road out to US 19. Orlando is more than two hours.

WalkabilityBottom 4%

Nothing is walkable in the errands sense. There are no shops or sidewalks, the road is narrow and winding, and the nearest groceries are back out near US 19.

Meet peopleBottom 3%

Very little is organized out here. The Ozello Civic Association is the one hub, with a chili cook off and craft show in February. For clubs, markets and concerts you drive to Crystal River.

Dining and artsBottom 2%

Two places, both seafood and both on the water. Peck's Old Port Cove is known for blue crab and Backwater Fins does seafood boils and brunch. Anything else is a drive to Crystal River or Homosassa.
