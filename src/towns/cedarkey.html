About Cedar Key

Cedar Key is a small island town at the end of State Road 24, about an hour from Gainesville. Life is slow and close to the water. People fish off the pier, paddle around the little islands, farm clams in the shallows and walk everywhere downtown. It's a town of clam farmers, fishermen and artists, and it picks quiet over convenience.

Be honest with yourself about the storms. Recent hurricanes hit Cedar Key hard, and the town is still rebuilding, with a few restaurants and businesses still missing. The grocery store was destroyed and the nearest full grocery is about 40 minutes away in Chiefland. Check flood zones and building elevation on every home, and expect a long drive for doctors and big errands.

Where it shines

Coastal charmTop 4%

Full of charm: a historic downtown, local everything, no towers, and streets you want to walk.

Peace and quietTop 6%

State Road 24 is the only road in, and golf carts share the streets downtown. The island fills up for the two big festival weekends, then goes right back to quiet.

AffordabilityTop 10%

Prices run below most Florida beach towns. The catch is the water. This is a low island that flooded badly in 2023 and 2024, so look at flood insurance and elevation before anything else.

WalkabilityTop 12%

The whole town fits in a few blocks. From 2nd Street you can walk or take a golf cart to Dock Street, the fishing pier and the Cemetery Point boardwalk. The grocery store was lost to Helene, so stocking up means a drive to Chiefland.

Where it falls short

Meet peopleBottom 33%

A small circle, but a real one. The Cedar Key Arts Center runs classes and shows, Cedar Keys Audubon meets at the library, and the Lions Club puts on the Seafood Festival every October.

Dining and artsBottom 29%

A short list. 83 West on Dock Street, clam chowder at Tony's and the Cedar Keyhole Artist Co-Op for browsing. The town is still down a few restaurants since Helene, so the open ones get busy.

BeachesBottom 18%

A small patch of sand at City Park is the town beach. It works for wading and sunsets, but the water is shallow and marshy and this is not a long white sand shoreline.

Airport accessBottom 7%

Gainesville (GNV) is the closest, a little over an hour up State Road 24. For more flights, Tampa and Orlando are each roughly two and a half hours away.

NightlifeBottom 6%

Nights end early. A drink at the Neptune Bar inside the 1859 Island Hotel or at a spot over the water on Dock Street is about it, and most of the island is dark by 9.
