About North Miami Beach

North Miami Beach sits on the mainland in north Miami Dade, between Biscayne Bay and I 95. It is not on the ocean, but the beaches of Sunny Isles and Haulover are a short drive across the bay. The city is a real mix of people: Caribbean, Latin American, Russian and Jewish communities, young families and retirees, and the restaurants along 163rd Street and Biscayne Boulevard reflect it. Oleta River State Park, with its mangroves, bike trails and kayaks, is right next door.

The trade offs: traffic on Biscayne Boulevard, 163rd Street and I 95 is heavy most of the day. Some neighborhoods are much more polished than others, so it pays to know the blocks. Condo prices vary hugely by building, and older buildings may have assessments. Insurance costs in Miami Dade are among the highest in the state.

Where it shines

Dining and artsTop 12%

The draw is range. Small kitchens along NE 163rd Street and the plazas around it cook food from dozens of countries, most of it casual and fairly priced. Greynolds Park adds bluegrass and the annual Love-in Music Festival.

Airport accessTop 16%

Miami (MIA) and Fort Lauderdale (FLL) are each about 25 to 30 minutes, and I-95 is right at the west edge of town. Rush hour at the Golden Glades can add a lot.

Where it falls short

WalkabilityBottom 29%

This is a driving city. The main roads are wide and built for cars, and stores sit in plazas with parking lots. A paved greenway along the Snake Creek canal and the NMB Line city buses help, but most errands need a car.

On the waterBottom 24%

The Oleta River, Maule Lake and a web of canals give boaters a route to the bay, and you can launch a kayak at Oleta River State Park or East Greynolds Park. Slips are mostly private. Public ramps are over at Haulover Park.

BeachesBottom 23%

Despite the name, there is no ocean beach in the city. Oleta River State Park has a small sandy swimming beach on the bay, and the Atlantic at Haulover Park or Sunny Isles Beach is about ten minutes east over the causeway.

Peace and quietBottom 9%

Traffic is the tradeoff. NE 163rd Street, Biscayne Boulevard and the Golden Glades interchange are busy most of the day. Side streets and the parks along the Oleta River are much calmer.

Active outdoorsBottom 6%

Oleta River State Park has 15 miles of off road bike trails, mangrove paddling and rental kayaks, and Greynolds Park adds 249 acres with walking trails and a nine hole golf course. Oleta often fills up on weekends.

Coastal charmBottom 3%

Mostly new build, chains and towers. Not much old town feel.

About average for 3 more: meet people, nightlife and affordability
Meet peopleTop 27%

Decent options if you like the outdoors. Greynolds Park hosts a monthly bluegrass festival, the Friends of Oleta River State Park back cleanups and trail work, and the city puts on events like Bark Fest and a 5K run in October.

NightlifeTop 35%

Nights are low key. Dinner out is the main event, and a few restaurant bars stay open after. For live music or a late night you drive to Hollywood, Gulfstream Park or Miami Beach.

AffordabilityTop 39%

Mid range for coastal South Florida. Single story houses and older condos cost far less than the barrier islands across the causeway, while homes on the canals cost more. Wind insurance is still a real line item.

Common questions

Is North Miami Beach walkable?

Not really. You will drive for most things. This is a driving city. The main roads are wide and built for cars, and stores sit in plazas with parking lots. A paved greenway along the Snake Creek canal and the NMB Line city buses help, but most errands need a car.

Does North Miami Beach have a good beach?

It has a modest one. Better beaches are a drive away. Despite the name, there is no ocean beach in the city. Oleta River State Park has a small sandy swimming beach on the bay, and the Atlantic at Haulover Park or Sunny Isles Beach is about ten minutes east over the causeway.

Is North Miami Beach affordable?

How busy does North Miami Beach get?

It gets busy, especially in season. Traffic is the tradeoff. NE 163rd Street, Biscayne Boulevard and the Golden Glades interchange are busy most of the day. Side streets and the parks along the Oleta River are much calmer.

Is North Miami Beach good for boating and fishing?

Yes, with decent access to ramps and marinas. The Oleta River, Maule Lake and a web of canals give boaters a route to the bay, and you can launch a kayak at Oleta River State Park or East Greynolds Park. Slips are mostly private. Public ramps are over at Haulover Park.
