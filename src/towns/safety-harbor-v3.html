About Safety Harbor

Safety Harbor is a small city on the west shore of Old Tampa Bay. Its Main Street runs downhill under big oaks to the bay, lined with local shops, cafes and porches. People walk their dogs to coffee, bike to the waterfront park and know the folks at the next table. It draws families, retirees and Tampa commuters who want a real small town feel.

The bayfront is exposed to storm surge, so homes near the water need a close look at flood zones and elevation. Prices have climbed with the town's popularity, and homes near Main Street cost more. There is no Gulf beach here; the beaches are about 30 to 40 minutes away.

Common questions

Is Safety Harbor walkable?

Yes, for the most part. Main Street is a compact run of blocks that ends at the bay, with the resort, the waterfront park and the pier at the bottom. Restaurants, the museum and the Sunday market are all on the way.

Does Safety Harbor have a good beach?

Not really. The beach is not the draw here. No beach in town. The bayfront is seawall, mangrove and park. For sand you drive across the county to Clearwater Beach or Honeymoon Island, around 30 to 40 minutes.

What is the nightlife like in Safety Harbor?

Quiet, with a handful of places to go. Dinner on Main Street, then a concert at the Safety Harbor Art and Music Center or a film in the 32 seat screening room at Gigglewaters. On Third Friday the street itself is the party. Otherwise it's an early town.

How busy does Safety Harbor get?

It is quiet most of the year. It sits off the main highways on Old Tampa Bay, so there's no beach traffic and little through traffic. Main Street gets lively for Third Friday and festivals, then settles back down.

Is Safety Harbor good for boating and fishing?

Yes, with decent access to ramps and marinas. This is Old Tampa Bay, not the Gulf. There's a public fishing pier and a small marina at the foot of Main Street, and Tocobaga Tours rents kayaks and paddleboards. Open Gulf is a long run by boat.
