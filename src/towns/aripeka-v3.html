About Aripeka

Aripeka is a small creekside village on the line between Pasco and Hernando counties. There are no stoplights and no shops, just older homes and stilt houses on Hammock Creek, wide salt marsh and a short run to the Gulf. People who live here fish, paddle, sit on the porch and drive to Hudson or Spring Hill for errands. It's quiet in a way few places on this coast still are.

The trade offs are serious. Homes sit right on the creeks and marsh, and many first floors flooded in Helene in 2024. There are no restaurants or services in town, so every errand is a drive. Very few homes sell, and prices swing a lot because the sample is so small.

NightlifeBottom 4%

None in the village. Nights are on the porch or the dock. For a drink or a band you drive about 10 minutes to Hudson Beach or Hernando Beach.

Dining and artsBottom 3%

No restaurants in town. Aripeka Stone Crab Company sells fresh seafood by the bridge, and there is a small store across the creek. Hudson and Hernando Beach have places to eat about 10 minutes away.

WalkabilityBottom 3%

You can walk the village roads and out to the bridge, and that is part of the charm. Groceries and everything else mean driving to US 19.

BeachesBottom 2%

No sand beach. The shore is salt marsh and creek mouths. The closest swimming beaches are Pine Island to the north and the beach parks in Hudson to the south.

Meet peopleBottom 2%

Very small and close knit. Neighbors see each other at the bridge, the fish market and the little store, and the old community clubhouse hosts gatherings. Most clubs and groups are in Hudson or Spring Hill.
