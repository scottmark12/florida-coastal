About Inglis

Inglis is a small town a few miles in from the Gulf, sitting where the Withlacoochee River, Lake Rousseau and the old Cross Florida Barge Canal come together. That means a lot of water in every direction: bass fishing on the lake, paddling the river, and an easy run down to the Gulf. Homes are mostly simple single family houses on good sized lots, many under big trees. People are friendly and the pace is slow, with a few more anglers and paddlers around in spring and fall.

It is a long drive to big shopping and hospitals, and the closest airport is Gainesville, about 1 hour 15 minutes away. Low ground along the river and toward Yankeetown took surge in Idalia (2023) and Helene (2024), so check elevation and flood history on any home near the water. Higher ground inland and around the lake tends to fare better. Services in town are basic.

Where it falls short

Active outdoorsBottom 18%

Lots of protected land. The Cross Florida Greenway, Inglis Dam and Island Recreation Area and the Withlacoochee Gulf Preserve in Yankeetown are all minutes away, with trails, birding and paddling.

On the waterBottom 20%

This is a water town. Lake Rousseau sits above the dam, the Withlacoochee runs below it to the Gulf, and the old barge canal is a straight shot out for boats. Fishing is the main hobby.

NightlifeBottom 11%

There really is no nightlife here. Evenings are a beer at a local bar and grill or a tiki bar down the road in Yankeetown, and most nights are early. For anything more you drive to Crystal River or Ocala.

Airport accessBottom 8%

Gainesville (GNV) is about 1 hour 15 minutes away with limited flights. Tampa (TPA) is closer to 1 hour 45 minutes, and Orlando is around 2 hours.

BeachesBottom 7%

There is no beach in town. The Gulf shore here is marsh and creeks, not sand, so beach days mean a drive south to Fort Island Gulf Beach near Crystal River or up to Cedar Key.

Meet peopleBottom 6%

People meet at the boat ramps, the post office and the few local spots on US 19. Town events are small and occasional, so it takes some effort. Fishing and paddling are the easiest way in.

WalkabilityBottom 5%

You drive for nearly everything. Homes are spread out on big lots and along the water, and there are few sidewalks. The paved trail on Inglis Island is the best place to walk or bike.

Dining and artsBottom 5%

A handful of casual places, like Driftwood Bar and Grill, Main Deck and Galley and Wise Guys Deli, plus Gator Cove Tiki Bar in Yankeetown. Expect fried seafood and burgers. For more choice you head to Crystal River.
