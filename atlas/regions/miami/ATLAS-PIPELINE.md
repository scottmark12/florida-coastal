# Illustrated regional packages in the Florida Coastal atlas

The application entry is `explore_coast.html`, using `regions/app.js`. Miami is loaded by the same renderer, camera, labels, district cards, selectors and phone control layout as approved 30A. It is not an iframe or a separate review application. The 30A geometry and artwork are preserved; its established exporter has not been replaced or rerun. Miami uses the reusable tiled package exporter and a data-package adapter in that shared application.

1. Freeze attributed OSM coast, land, building footprints, roads and land cover; preserve approved geometry. Record any additional acquisition separately. `miami-current.json` merges the requested new districts outside the approved core, with a zero-area symmetric difference in the original extent.
2. Read district/price files. An OSM node or documented mapped-feature centroid anchors each district. Accept either a published median with metric, reporting period, source URL and retrieval date, or a listing range backed by at least ten current listing records. Two examples cannot pass validation.
3. Read `rules.json`: buildings at three times source height, capped at three times the shorter oriented footprint width; trees five times real height with the same cap. OSM footprints do not move. Missing heights use an explicitly reported two-story assumption. Warm cream land, sage planting, white sand, turquoise water. The shoreline wash is illustrative color, not surveyed bathymetry.
4. District recipe format: `walls[]`, `roofs[]`, `trim[]`, `roof`, `detail`, `source`. Optional geographic `styleZones` override nearest-center styling. South Beach is pastel Deco with projecting eyebrows and parapets; Downtown/Brickell blue/teal/white glass bands; Mid Beach warm resort stucco; Key Biscayne pale island homes; Grove and Gables retain distinct materials. Landmark profiles remain keyed to actual OSM IDs.
5. Export a shared base, simplified region buildings and one-kilometer detail tiles. At Region zoom, small illustrated recipe clusters sit at their true anchors. Under 700 pixels the established 30A interaction uses true-position dots; tapping opens Town. Labels stay horizontal, alternate sides, use at most 40-pixel leaders and hide when colliding. District cards use the existing slim phone controls.
6. Audit exported float32 district coordinates against their OSM control. Export CSV including both coordinate systems and meter differences. Mid Beach uses the Fontainebleau footprint centroid, explicitly identified as a representative anchor rather than a surveyed neighborhood center. Coast checks measure serialized land boundaries against source vertices. These prove source alignment, not independent ground-truth accuracy.
7. Run `run_atlas.py work/coastal_pipeline/v3/miami-current.json` with the workspace Python. It runs the same frozen exporter, water renderer and proof scripts, installs district data in the atlas, rebuilds the shared application and checks protected 30A assets. Review Region/South Beach/Brickell at 390 and 1440 pixels; check cards, labels, console and region package size. Physical-phone performance still needs an actual device test.

Manual source/editorial decisions in this pass: Miami recipe palettes and geographic style zones; new OSM acquisition for Mid Beach and Key Biscayne; choice of Fontainebleau as Mid Beach's representative anchor; verification of seven published market medians; ocean-facing beach styling zones. No approved core land geometry was edited.
